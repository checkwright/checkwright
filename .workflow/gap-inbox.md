- 2026-07-31 — GAP (lead protocol, operator-directed, live instance this session): lifecycle-kit/templates/lead.md lets the lead open an iteration on its own initiative, and it should require the operator's explicit approval. The template's Opening an iteration section tells the lead to obtain the operator's standing *directive* -- a theme bounding scope's survey -- before dispatching scope, but it never says the lead must obtain authorization to open an iteration *at all*. The directive slot presupposes the decision to open has already been made and is silent on who makes it. Observed this session: the operator said 'fix all' about a list of four outstanding release defects, two of which were edits to governed surfaces; the lead read that as authorization to open an iteration, reset the boundary, dispatch scope, and stamp -- and the operator afterwards stated plainly that they had not asked for one and that starting a new iteration requires their explicit approval. The lead's inference was defensible on the content (governed-surface edits do route through the lifecycle) and wrong on the scale, which was the operator's to choose. Deliverable: state in lead.md that opening an iteration requires explicit operator authorization, distinct from and prior to the standing directive, and that an ambiguous instruction to fix filed work is not that authorization -- the lead asks. Consider whether enter-stage.sh's first-stage entry can carry a mechanical floor rather than leaving it to prose, since prompts request and guards enforce. Cost while deferred: a lead can spend an iteration's opening cost on scale the operator never chose, and the only detector today is the operator noticing after the stamp has landed.

- 2026-07-31 — No gate parses a workflow permissions: block, so every scope a workflow needs is landed on reading alone. Confirmed exhaustively at the release-step-verification align audit, three ways: a literal grep (whose only hits are Claude Code settings.json permissions, an unrelated concept), a concept grep across GITHUB_TOKEN, GH_TOKEN, least-privilege and token-scope spellings that returned nothing, and an inspection of all nine gates that read workflow YAML at all. gate-sdk/SPEC.md line 1794 declares workflow-security linting an explicit non-goal, so the absence is a ruling rather than an oversight -- but the ruling predates the site-health probe needing contents: read, and the failure mode is now concrete: a permissions: block is an allowlist, an undeclared scope reads back as an HTTP 404 indistinguishable from an absent resource, and on a public repository the omission is invisible until a private-repo consumer copies the workflow. A tractable gate exists and is narrower than a general security linter: a workflow job whose run: bodies invoke gh, or that checks out, must declare the scopes those calls consume, starting with contents: read. Tree-local, hermetic, and cheap -- one gate plus a good/bad fixture pair, a gates.list row and a graph manifest. Costed at one small build unit; charged while deferred against every workflow that gains an API call.
