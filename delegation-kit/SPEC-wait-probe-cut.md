### (3) The exit contract is three-state, and the SPEC sentence is the defect this cut corrects

§bin/wait-probe states "Exit **0** on a completed subcommand, **2** on misuse".
That is false of the shipped tool, and the correction lands with the port rather
than the port silently making the sentence true {design-bearing}.

**Observed by running it, not read out of the source.** With
`GATE_SDK_WORKFLOW_DIR` pointed at a throwaway directory,
`wait-probe.sh report` exits **1** — on an empty evidence file and on an absent
one alike, `[ -s … ]` failing identically. `wait-probe.sh:131`'s `return 1` is the
last command of `cmd_report`, which is the last command of the `case` at `:180`,
which is the last command of the script. `report extra` and an unknown subcommand
exit 2; `--help` exits 0.

**The 1 is preserved, and the ruling is that it is contract rather than a latent
defect.** The tempting reading is the other one — no caller in the tree reads the
status, so folding 1 into 2 would cost nothing today and would make the sentence
true for free. It is refused on the instrument's own subject: *no trials recorded*
is not misuse. The invocation is well formed and the tool ran; 1 is its
substantive finding that there is nothing to classify. Collapsing it into the
misuse code makes an honest empty reading indistinguishable from a typo, on the one
tool in the tree whose entire value is telling **a reading from a non-reading** —
which is the same harm gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The bin/-tool contract records as the
crossing clause's second instance, reached here from a third direction.

**And a port may not change a verdict across the seam.** An `--emit-` spelling
would rewrite 1 to 2 by construction, and no gate in the battery would report it:
`check-assertion-strength` cannot reach the member (delta 10 measures why), and no
fixture pair is owed because this is not a gate. A behaviour change nothing
observes is the shape the parity discipline exists against, and it is not made
lawful by the SPEC sentence it would happen to satisfy.

So the section's exit paragraph is rewritten to the three-state contract with each
code's meaning named — **0** a completed subcommand, **1** `report` with no trials
recorded, **2** misuse — and the correction is recorded as a documentation defect
the port found, not as a behaviour the port introduced.

### (4) The measurement subject stays shell — the wait body, its arming, and the producer's launch

This is the cut's central seam ruling, and it is criterion 7's **the program is the
rule** clause applied to a non-gate member for the first time {design-bearing}.
gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The port-candidate criteria rules that where a program "decides
what the member asserts", the member "ports as a wrapper that spawns the program,
and the program stays a declared dependency". `shellcheck`, `cargo` and the
renderer are that clause's instances; here the program is `bash`, and what it
decides is not an assertion but a **measurement**.

Three parts stay shell and each has its own ground, stated separately because a
reader who accepts one may not accept the others:

- **The wait body.** `cmd_waiter`'s `until [ -f <marker> ]` / `until kill -0 <pid>`
  loop is not an implementation of the instrument — it **is the artifact under
  measurement**, the exact form `templates/agent-execution.md` prescribes and the
  exact form guard-kit rules 13, 15 and 19 are shaped to. A compiled loop would
  measure a form no session ever runs, which is a silent change of subject on an
  instrument whose whole output is evidence for a protocol clause. The loop literal
  and its four exit traps become one Rust `const`, and §bin/wait-probe's "every form
  arms the **same** wait body" survives as a stronger claim than it is today: one
  constant, one exec path.
- **The arming of the `local` form.** The tool's own usage text defines `arm-local`
  as arming the waiter "as a **detached shell child** (the harness-uninvolved
  control)". A crate-armed child makes that form's name false, and the *form* axis
  is a measured axis by §bin/wait-probe's own words — "the *form* axis varies how
  the wait is armed". The arming therefore stays `nohup … &` inside the arm's
  `bash -c` literal, with the launched PID read back and written to the `<key>.run`
  record delta (7) governs.
- **The producer's launch, and this one is a hazard rather than a definition.**
  Probed rather than reasoned: a bash script's backgrounded child **is reaped**, so
  `kill -0` on its recorded PID correctly fails once it exits. Rust's `Child`
  performs no implicit reap, so a compiled producer whose parent outlives it leaves
  a **zombie**, and `kill -0` on a zombie **succeeds**. The waiter's
  `producer_alive_at_exit` field is read at the classification to establish the
  *producer verifiably alive* condition — cause (i)'s whole tell — so a compiled
  producer would invert that field on every trial while the instrument reported
  clean. Keeping the launch in `bash -c` keeps the reaping, and keeping the reaping
  keeps the field true.

**The arm `exec`s into the shell rather than spawning and waiting, and the ground
is measurement validity.** Backgrounding `bash wait-probe.sh waiter …` today puts
**one** process between the harness and the loop. An arm that spawned the loop
would put two, and the reaping cause the instrument exists to detect is a property
of the process the harness signals. `exec` preserves the depth and the signal
disposition, so the ported instrument measures the configuration the shell one
measured — the invariant a port owes and the one this member can most easily lose.

**The refused alternative is the whole-hog port, recorded because it is what a
line-count reading would take.** Compiling the loop, the arming and the producer
would delete every shell literal and read as the cleaner cut. It is refused because
it silently re-points the instrument at a subject no protocol prescribes, and the
resulting trials would still be filed under §bin/wait-probe and still be cited by
two kits' governed prose as evidence about the form sessions actually run.
