# delegation-kit — safe delegated-agent execution for budget-bounded sessions

Delegated agents are cheap to dispatch and expensive to trust. Three failure surfaces dominate:

- **shared mutable state** — two committing agents race the git index, and one sweeps the other's staged files under the wrong message;
- **interrupted long units** — a usage-window wall fires mid-flight, and the uncommitted investigation dies with the session;
- **untrustworthy self-reports** — a sub-agent's "passed" claim, or a gate quietly weakened to make its commit pass.

The kit packages the supervisor-side protocol that closes all three, plus the two mechanizable pieces: a trustworthy budget verdict (`usage-verdict`) and a commit-shape gate over gate tampering (`check-gate-tamper`). The consumer supplies its own validate battery, shared-file roster and toolchain checks, which are project rule content and never ship.

## The delegation model

One **supervisor** session dispatches `Agent` tasks and owns every ruling; agents execute briefs and surface anything the brief does not cover. `templates/agent-execution.md` owns the load-bearing rules: it is the procedure a session loads, and each bullet's bold lead-in is the rule's stable name (§One template, a resident pointer). This section keeps the mechanism contracts and the rationale that earns spec residency: a failure surface, a calibration, a bound that is correctness rather than preference. Each paragraph cites its rule by name rather than restating it.

**Boundary — this protocol stays fire-and-forget.** An agent here executes a bounded brief and its result returns to the supervisor. A paused session that holds its working state and *resumes in place* when a question is answered is a different model, *resume-in-place orchestration*. That model is lifecycle-kit's lead template (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §templates/lead.md), which reuses this protocol's dispatch safety unchanged.

The template's **Serialize on shared files; ≤`DELEGATION_KIT_FAN_WIDTH`-wide otherwise** rule caps an *unlocked* fan-out at read-only work. The git index and HEAD are shared by every committing agent whatever its source files, so the cap is a correctness bound. A consumer may never configure it up for committing agents (the knob's derivation: §Layout and configuration).

**The width bound is discipline, surfaced and never enforced.** `usage-verdict` reports it at every dispatch (§usage-verdict, the `width=<n>` field), and no guard rule counts what is in flight. A `PreToolUse` payload carries no in-flight set (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The reader and its views records its measured keys), and the harness's live-children view arrives only at `SubagentStop`. The transcript is the one reachable record of earlier dispatches, and it does not yet hold a call's own message while that call runs, in one reading. The attested overrun was three dispatches in one message, so a transcript-counting rule would miss the shape that fired. A guard-written ledger of dispatches would see it, but it makes a verdict surface own state it must expire, which *The dispatch guard reaps nothing* refuses.

The template's **Background + notification, never poll** rule reads as universal but is not: backgrounding survives a turn, and a dispatched agent's turn end is its session end. A stage session that backgrounded its validate suite and ended its turn to report progress lost its child: an entry stamp, no results, the suite re-run from scratch. Nothing reddened, because missing evidence looks like pending evidence. Two phrasings fail. A role-neutral "never end your turn with detached work" forbids the supervisor pattern the same rule prescribes. "Await it in the **foreground**" names a lever the agent may not have, since dispatches made without requesting backgrounding have been observed detached.

Reaping holds for an `Agent` child and not for a shell one: a shell producer kept running and writing its output after its session's turn ended. So the rule's mechanism is the loss of the **observer**, not of the work. The surviving producer is the worse case: the unobservable-producer hazard, an unread producer mutating shared files while the next actor moves, arriving through the backgrounding door. The rule rests on an invariant either way. An agent's contract with its caller is its return value, so work whose result is not in the return did not happen for the caller.

**A dispatched role waits in-turn, on the work's own artifact.** A session that reads the liveness hazard correctly and ends its turn to await the completion notification destroys the one channel it meant to use. A rule whose correct application needs a channel the reader lacks defeats careful readers too, so the template names the channel a dispatched role has.

**A wait needs a writer, and a question is no wait.** A mechanical-tier session looped on a marker nothing would create; the harness backgrounded the loop at the foreground timeout, and the session read as live past its hand-back for the background limit. The guard's refusals reach a self-matching `pgrep` and a record-falsified condition, never a condition with no writer, so the template states it. The never-end-a-turn-to-wait clause also read as covering a wait for the caller's answer. That answer arrives as a new turn, so asking is a turn end and the template says so.

**A notification wakes a supervisor only from after its turn.** A notification queued while the supervisor's turn is still running has landed after a turn that made no further tool call, and started none. The template bounds its claim to that; the lead's remedy and its honest limit are lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §The stamp protocol's.

**The wait is named by its property, not its form.** The harness offers waiting primitives with opposite reactivity, and a session choosing among named forms chooses by shape. The template names the property a wait must have, ending when its condition goes true, and sorts the forms under it. That discriminator survives a harness adding a third form, which makes it a correctness bound.

**Two waitable things, and only one of them has a path.** The template splits what a session waits *on*: an `Agent`, by its completion notification, and a shell child, by an artifact the session placed. The split is complete. The artifact's home is the resume journal's scoping widened, so the survivability grounds are stated once.

**The shell child's artifact is a launch-time liveness record**: the child's PID, captured by the launcher when it backgrounds the child. A completion marker the child touches when done is refused. It answers *is it done* to a live observer, and the failure case is the one where the observer is gone. A liveness record answers *is it still running* to whoever arrives next.

**The launcher writes the record; per-producer self-claiming is refused.** Self-claiming reaches only producers the consumer's tree owns, where most backgrounded work is an ad-hoc command. It puts the record's write in the one process whose death is the event being detected. And it bills a program for a property of the *launch*: the same script backgrounded is in the class and run in the foreground is not. The launcher knows the PID, needs to read it back, and is present when the class is entered.

**The liveness clause covers a producer the session started and backgrounded.** A session that started a producer was taken to have a better instrument, its own artifact, but that instrument needs a live reader, and losing the reader is the failure. The widening is on the subject alone. A pattern-matched liveness predicate stays refused wherever a recorded PID is available, and so does the bracket trick below.

**The record's grammar is evidence-kit's, which makes the second reader free.** The record has two readers: the **launching session**, in-turn, as its wait condition, and **whoever arrives next**, after the launcher is gone. In the one-line `pid=<n> run=<key>` grammar of the producer-liveness lock, the record is a legal subject for `check-producer-liveness` unchanged, a reader already specified, fixture-covered and wired. A launch record is not a lock, but the two carry the same field and answer the same question, so a second shape would be a second implementation of a decided thing. How a PID's liveness is decided stays evidence-kit's (evidence-kit/SPEC.md §The producer-liveness lock); delegation-kit owns *when a session waits and on what*.

**Only a producer owes a record.** A record states that something is mutating shared files. A wait loop and a read-only pipeline are **observers** and owe none; a **producer**, a child writing anything a later reader must not race, takes the record. guard-kit draws the same line, exempting an inline wait loop and a read-only pipeline from its launch refusal (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset), and the template states the line rather than pointing at the guard. An obligation over every shell child would oblige a record of the wait loop itself. Where that waiter's condition reads the record set, its own record makes the condition unsatisfiable. Three readers share the set: the stage-entry preflight, guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer`, and the turn-end liveness hook. A reader-side repair that ignores a record named for the stage being entered would fix one of the three, and is refused.

**A recorded wait asks one authoring question, with no oracle behind it.** The exemption sees a wait loop only when spelled **inline**, and guard-kit accepts that limit: a recorded waiter holds tracked-tree mutation only as long as the producer it waits on already does. That ground fails for a waiter on anything else, such as a stage-entry precondition, where the waiter's own record is the only thing blocking the condition. So the template asks a recorded wait whether its own record can falsify its condition. The guard cannot read a wait's condition at `PreToolUse`, and a loop that never exits looks like a slow producer.

**The record's name is a convention that makes the set derivable.** It is written as `<scratch-dir>/<key>.run`, the filename's `<key>` being the record's own `run=` value. Without a convention only the writer knows the path, and whoever arrives next must guess it. The suffix makes the set a glob, read by `check-producer-liveness`'s set mode (evidence-kit/SPEC.md §check-producer-liveness), the stage-entry preflight behind it, and guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer`.

**`.run` rather than `.lock`.** A lock is claimed and released by one owner and its absence means *free*; `EVIDENCE_KIT_LOCK_FILE` is one. A launch record is a statement of fact left behind, and its absence means *nothing was recorded*, never *nothing is running*. A consumer's scratch reset keeps no `*.run`, because a record surviving a work-unit boundary names a producer from the previous one.

**No tool writes or reads the record.** The write is a PID captured at launch and the read a single liveness match, each a shell builtin away and both spelled in the template. A script whose body is one builtin is the interpreter surface this methodology shrinks. The reading tool that earns its place exists already: `check-producer-liveness`. The cost is that a hand-written record can be malformed. That gate exits **2** on a record it cannot parse, so a malformed record reads as *could not be established*, never as *nothing running*.

**The record's write is uncheckable after the fact, so the harm is enforced.** A session that skips the rule writes nothing, so there is no absence a check could expect. A tracked-tree mutation under a live producer arrives as an ordinary tool call at a `PreToolUse` chokepoint, the class the interception axis below admits. guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` blocks it there (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset). `check-producer-liveness` at a stage entry is the backstop for a producer still live when the next entry is stamped.

**The launch is refused without a record.** That enforcement holds only for a session that recorded. So guard-kit rule `background_no_record` blocks a backgrounding call that writes no record, and rule `bounded_wait` grants the one canonical recorded launch of an allowlisted command, so the compliant spelling costs no permission decision under the Bash tool. A PowerShell launch meets the refusal and takes no grant. The rule covers both spellings: a shell `&` in the command text, and the harness's `.tool_input.run_in_background` boolean (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The shell guard records the payload's shape). Building only the `&` arm was refused as worse than not building it. Every attested firing used the harness form, so a shell-only rule would block the form nobody uses and pass the one that fires.

**A call the harness moves to the background on its timeout is the residue.** It was a foreground call when the guard saw it, it writes no record, and no `PreToolUse` payload carries task state. The template covers the recoverable half: size a foreground timeout above the run, record from a pid the moved call names, and hold the turn and the tracked tree until the completion notification. A moved producer naming no pid stays unrecorded. The turn-end hook still refuses a dispatched session's turn end while the harness's task view shows it running (§The turn-end liveness hook), but rule `git_mutation_under_producer` cannot see it. Such a session has no in-turn primitive that ends on the notification: there is no pid for a `kill -0` loop, and a pattern match is refused. It can keep working until the notification arrives, but cannot wait idle.

**An awaited artifact lives in repo-local scratch.** A session that spelled its artifact wait against the harness's own scratchpad path was refused by the consumer's guard and steered to repo-local scratch, and the guard was right. With the split above, a session waiting on an `Agent` never reaches for a path, and a session waiting on a shell child has one the guard sanctions.

**The bracket trick is refused as the sanctioned form.** The template carries why a process-liveness predicate can never go false and the two sanctioned repairs. It defers here why the third, `pgrep -f '[r]un-smoke.sh'`, is refused. It works, so what disqualifies it is its failure mode: a spelling that must be remembered, whose omission is invisible at the call site and silently expensive. A correct form one character from an incorrect one will be got wrong, so the enforcement is a guard rule over the shape (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset).

The template's **Findings you will act on are durable before you act on them** rule closes a near-loss: a close stage dispatched two audit sweeps and hit the session wall, and only luck had them back first. A read-only child had journalled nothing, its findings living only in the parent's context. Both ends now write. The read-only child journals as it goes, an isolated one by a shell append, the one route to the main checkout the harness leaves it. The parent's durability duty stands beside it, because §Resume journal's caveat still holds of the parent's end: recoverability never rests on a child's write alone.

agent-budget-guard closes the gap the template's **Budget-check before *each* dispatch in a fan-out** rule leaves when it relies on the agent *choosing* to run the tool. A memory-quoted percentage acts in its place: a session quoted ~5% while the live verdict read 29%. It is a `PreToolUse` hook (matcher `Agent`) the harness fires on every dispatch once the consumer registers it, a per-dispatch reading that no mid-session window outlives. It runs `usage-verdict` at the decision point and routes on the exit code:

- **PAUSE (1)** → block, exit 2, with the verdict line plus its corrective (wait for the window reset, or re-run with `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT` deliberately raised). This is guard-kit's one sanctioned fail-closed deny. The matcher proves the tool, and PAUSE is reachable only through a fresh, readable, over-threshold snapshot, so blocking cannot wedge a consumer with no producer.
- **STALE / unreadable (2)** and **OK / RESET-OK (0)** → advise, exit 0, feeding the verdict line back as `additionalContext`, so the live reading rides in context at every dispatch. The arm relays the line and adds nothing, since the verdict carries its own consequence (§usage-verdict). Routing STALE to advice keeps a consumer with no snapshot producer working: budget-unknown is decision-relevant, never disqualifying.

The consumers are the dispatching agent, reading the verdict off `additionalContext` at the dispatch decision, and the supervising user, who consumes a block by ruling: raise the knob or wait. The agent never overrides. delegation-kit owns the verdict, its thresholds and the routing; guard-kit supplies only the framework primitives. Registration is the opt-in valve: a consumer wanting pure advice does not wire the hook. The verdict line is the only interface, with no new state and no new `usage.txt` key.

**Covered at every depth, aggregated at none.** The registration is session-wide, so the hook fires inside dispatched sessions too, each fire carrying that session's identity. Each fire is independent and holds no state about the tree, so a verdict prices the one call it gates and never the subtree that call opens. The subtree clause of the template's **Budget-check before *each* dispatch in a fan-out** rule compensates. A stateless hook with no depth counter does reach below the root. A hook whose output only its own session sees looks absent from every other vantage, so a supervisor counting its own hook fires undercounts its tree.

A knob-raise ruling has one working transport: the settings env block (`.claude/settings.local.json`), which hooks re-read per fire. A raised `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT` reaches the guard on the next dispatch with no restart, and deleting the entry restores the default as fast. A Bash-tool inline `export` never reaches the hook, whose process does not inherit the tool's shell. Propagation is asymmetric: the Bash tool's environment keeps an injected value after the entry is deleted, until the session restarts. So the hook's verdict line, not an in-session re-run, is the acting reading. That this transport works sanctions nothing else it reaches: the subagent-model override below rides the same block and is refused.

**The subagent-model environment override is refused, in both of its forms.** Model resolution runs a dispatch's own `model` parameter, then the definition's `model:` frontmatter, then the environment override, then the dispatching conversation's model. A companion force switch lifts the environment rung to the top. Forced, it pins every subagent in one line and silently defeats a supervisor's per-batch tier pin. `lifecycle-kit/templates/lead.md` §Economics — batch, and compact where it pays depends on that pin as a ceiling, not a default. Unforced, it reaches only a dispatch naming no model either way and binds it to one model whatever its class, the class-blind choice §The tier binding replaces. The correct rung is the **frontmatter**. It supplies the standing default the template's **Match the dispatched model and effort to the unit's shape** rule asks for, while the per-dispatch parameter still overrides it. The field is *stated* because an omitted `model:` falls through to the dispatcher's tier, so omission spells a tier; `check-agent-tier-explicit` is that clause's oracle over the tracked surface.

**Isolation is confinement.** The template's **A read-only claim is made by isolation, not by sentence** rule states the disposition; its grounds are these. "It has no Edit tool" is not a safety property. Every agent type available for audit-shaped work carries write tools, or a shell that reaches `git`, and a brief never narrows a toolset.

**A surviving worktree is not a detector.** The harness auto-cleans an unchanged worktree only best-effort: surviving worktrees have been found locked at a stale stamp with an empty `git status --porcelain` inside. So a survivor means *either* the child wrote *or* reclamation did not fire, and one `git status --porcelain` inside tells them apart. The reaping boundary performs that read and reports per path, and the iteration boundary refuses outright on any linked worktree (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §bin/enter-stage.sh). Gitignoring the worktree path would trade the signal away, which is why the rule's second cost sends the parent to `git worktree list` at the reaping boundary. A detector whose positive is unsound trains its reader to investigate nothing. Repeated successful auto-cleans confirmed it until one did not, the lesson `worktree.baseRef` repeats below: repeated confirmation of a behavior is not evidence about its mutability.

**The dispatch guard reaps nothing.** *A verdict surface may not own lifecycle*: the guard's contract is to decide, and a guard removing directories and refs en route to a verdict fails invisibly at the only place anyone looks. That ground alone refuses a dispatch-time sweep. A second ground was timing: a sweep beside in-flight siblings must tell that a worktree belongs to no live child. The lock **reason** answers that, naming the holding process's pid and start time, so liveness is decidable from outside the child; lifecycle-kit's boundary check performs that classification. Wherever the consumer configures no lock-reason pattern the predicate degrades to blind, so a guard-side sweep would still rest on optional config to avoid destroying a sibling's checkout. The iteration-boundary refusal takes no destructive action and runs when the previous work is finished by definition.

**Why worktrees strand.** A locked worktree is refused by `git worktree prune` and by an unforced `git worktree remove`. A harness dying before its cleanup step therefore strands one that is clean, commitless and unreclaimable. Reclamation is tied to the child's own return, not to the dispatching session's lifetime. Which pid the lock reason names is the harness's to decide. A harness writing its supervising process's pid makes this the one record the standing instinct, trust a recorded pid's liveness, misreads, so the template states that limit at the reap step.

**The dispatcher reaps what it minted at its own turn end.** Neither ground above reaches it. The subject is a session, not a verdict surface, and a dispatcher at its own turn end is past every child it started this turn and scoped to trees it minted, so it needs no predicate about a sibling's liveness. The boundary refusal stays the backstop. The obligation buys **latency**, shortening an orphan's carry from the rest of the iteration to one turn.

**No oracle is owed over that turn end.** `SubagentStop` sees it, but a worktree carries no attribution to the ending session without the optional lock-reason configuration. A refusal keyed on `git worktree list` would refuse a dispatcher for a concurrent session's live tree. Registering the worktree for a later sweep is refused too: `git worktree list` already is the registry, and a dispatcher-written record would share the reap's uncheckability.

**`worktree.baseRef` is vendor configuration, and choosing it is the consumer's job.** The template's **Isolation charges five harness costs, and paying them is the parent's job** rule names the knob and both of its values. A vendoring consumer inherits the template and never a settings file. A consumer that dispatches isolated agents sets the knob deliberately. Where its harness config is gated, it pins the knob through context-kit/SPEC.md §check-settings-pins. The value must be *chosen*, never a particular one: a consumer wanting a clean tree per dispatch is right to stay on the default. No kit knob shadows it (§Layout and configuration), since a kit knob over a vendor setting would be a second source for one value.

**The cost names a knob, not a measurement.** "The worktree is cut at the remote tracking ref, so staleness equals the unpushed backlog" describes the `fresh` value alone. Stated as universal, it reached shipped doctrine on attestations all taken under that default. So the rule names the knob and both values, and attributes any measurement to the configuration it was taken under.

**The child-side rev discipline stays as defence in depth.** The parent cannot read the child's *effective* setting: `baseRef` resolves on the dispatching machine's merged settings, and neither the payload nor the return carries it. A vendoring consumer that has not set the knob has the discipline as its whole protection. A managed tier or a command-line override outranks a project settings file, beyond any repo's pin. The check is one command whose output is the evidence, so under a holding pin it is a cheap assertion rather than dead weight.

**The untracked half reaches no configuration, and its disposition is refusal.** An isolated child sees only committed state, so no knob value or named rev helps. A sweep sent to triage an untracked capture log read an empty file and reported its corpus absent. A settings-overlay comparison saw no overlay, the local settings file being uncommitted. Both returned a confident "nothing there", indistinguishable from a clean result. It binds against the read-only rule: isolation makes the read-only claim and blinds the read, so a claimed-read-only sweep over an untracked corpus has no correct form inside its worktree.

**The write half is closed by construction.** A capture line an isolated child writes, such as a guard fall-through, a liveness record or a knowledge-friction stamp, lands in the main checkout's capture tier (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The workflow directory). Close's triage and the KPIs that count it read isolated sessions too. A tracked capture line, a gap bullet or a survey block, is refused in the worktree, and the child hands the finding back (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §The committed gap inbox). Reads are not redirected.

**The dispatch-shape rules are one mechanism.** The template's **Never dispatch a fork to narrow a child**, **A read-only claim is made by isolation, not by sentence**, and **A child's only upward route is a durable artifact** rules each govern the *shape of a dispatch call*. Each failed the same way: the prose did not reach the reader it binds. A fork dispatched as a read-only audit completed a whole stage, commits included. Forks with prose-only "read-only, no edits" briefs committed unreviewed on the shared branch. Fan-outs messaged the top-level session instead of their dispatcher, which no prompt wording could fix.

**The axis is interception, not durability.** A dispatch and a turn end both leave no tracked artifact. Whether an oracle is buildable turns on whether the act passes a **chokepoint**. A dispatch passes `PreToolUse`; a turn end passes no tool-call chokepoint. On the harness's own event axis it does: a `SubagentStop` hook sees it, the harness does not defer the stop while a background child is live, and §The turn-end liveness hook refuses there. So the turn-end rule carries enforcement on both axes, guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` over the harm and that hook over the act. Neither substitutes for the other: the guard rule reaches acts the hook's event never sees, and the hook reaches a session that mutates nothing.

**The chokepoint — what a `PreToolUse` hook can actually read.** The payload carries `session_id`, `prompt_id`, `transcript_path`, `cwd`, `permission_mode`, `hook_event_name`, `tool_name`, `tool_input`, and `tool_use_id`; and, **only when the hook fires inside a subagent**, `agent_id` and `agent_type`. For the dispatch tool, `tool_input` carries the dispatch's parameters: `subagent_type`, `isolation`, `model`, `description`, `prompt`. So the fork ban and the read-only claim have exact triggers, `tool_input.subagent_type` and `tool_input.isolation`. Depth has an exact, documented discriminator: `agent_id` is present iff the dispatching session is itself a subagent, so the call creates a grandchild. The discriminator is the field's *presence*, never its value, so the doubt about the value §The turn-end liveness hook records does not touch it.

**Honest limit on that roster.** It is sourced from the harness's published hook contract, fetched rather than recalled, on the footing of `CLAUDE_CODE_CHILD_SESSION`'s "verified, not trusted" treatment (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §bin/session-id.sh). A harness revision reshaping the payload is drift this SPEC cannot self-detect; only re-reading the contract catches it.

**Two depth routes ruled out.** `CLAUDE_CODE_CHILD_SESSION` is set in top-level sessions too, and `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` carries the *root* id for a child, so the environment cannot tell a depth-1 from a depth-2 dispatcher. The meta record beside a transcript carries an exact spawn depth. Coupling a hook to that *uncontracted* artifact, for what one *contracted* boolean answers, is declined. The stage-economics meter accepts that coupling (drift-kit/SPEC.md §The stage-economics meter, the fan-out row) because it needs the whole spawn forest, which no contracted field supplies.

agent-dispatch-guard is that oracle — a second `PreToolUse` hook on matcher `Agent`, beside the budget guard. It reads the payload once and runs the rules below, in order:

| # | rule | trigger | decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | fork ban | `tool_input.subagent_type` is `fork` | block |
| D2 | isolation claim | `tool_input.subagent_type` ∈ `DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES` and `tool_input.isolation` is not `worktree` | block |
| D4 | default isolation | `DELEGATION_KIT_MUTATING_TYPES` is non-empty, `tool_input.subagent_type` is not a member, and `tool_input.isolation` is not `worktree` | block |
| D5 | chosen tier | `DELEGATION_KIT_REQUIRE_TIER` is `on`, `tool_input.model` is absent, and the dispatched type has no definition under `DELEGATION_KIT_AGENT_DIR` stating `model:` | block |
| D6 | bound tier | `DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` is non-empty and `tool_input.model` matches no bound value | block |
| D3 | depth advisory | payload carries `agent_id` | advise |

Order is load-bearing only between the blocking rules and D3: a blocked dispatch needs no advisory, so D3 runs last. D1 precedes D2 because the fork ban is unconditional and its message is the more specific one for a dispatch violating both. D2 precedes D4 for the same reason, a read-only claim being the narrower message, and D4 precedes D5, since an unconfined dispatch is the graver fault. D5 and D6 trigger disjointly, an absent model against a named one, so their order sets only which message a reader meets first. An absent `subagent_type` is the harness's default type, which no roster declares and no definition names, so D4 and D5 each read it as undeclared.

**D4 closes the complement of D2.** D2 holds a named read-only type to isolation; D4 holds every type nobody declared mutating to it. A type is confined by default, and reaching past isolation takes a named choice: dispatching a type the consumer's `DELEGATION_KIT_MUTATING_TYPES` declares. It keys on the type, never on the prompt, on D3's ground. The knob is indexed and empty by default, so D4 is inert until configured, as D2 is, and the kit ships no type names. The block names the two lawful shapes: isolate the dispatch, or dispatch a declared mutating type.

**What isolation confines, measured, and what it leaves.** An isolated child's file-tool writes into the main checkout are refused by the harness, its scratch dir included, so its journal travels by a shell append. Its shell writes are not refused. Under a strict harness sandbox a child's `touch` into the main checkout's root succeeded, the sandbox's write-root being the session's project directory rather than the worktree. The sandbox would bound a child to the repository and no tighter, so it is not bound as a confinement.

guard-kit rule `worktree_confinement` confines the shell (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset, *A main-checkout path from a linked worktree*), and only for a path the command names. It admits a read-only pipeline or a read-only arm of the gate binary over the main checkout, the forms unable to write there, and refuses every other command naming it outside the scratch dir. A program the child runs can write any path it computes. That residue, and a dispatcher's knowing choice of a mutating type for read-only work (D4 makes the choice named, not impossible), are this design's honest limits. The file-tool refusal is harness behavior, measured in a session loading no project settings, on the footing the payload roster stands on.

**D5 makes the tier a choice.** Selection is affirmative (delegation-kit/templates/agent-execution.md, **Match the dispatched model and effort to the unit's shape**): an unselected dispatch inherits the dispatcher's tier, so D5 blocks a dispatch that names no `model` and whose type has no definition stating one. The read-only type's stated default is its own definition's `model:` field, which `check-agent-tier-explicit` already requires every tracked definition to carry. A definition is found by its first frontmatter block's `name:`, else its file stem, the way the harness names a type. A built-in type with no definition names its tier at every dispatch. The switch is `DELEGATION_KIT_REQUIRE_TIER`, `off` by default, and the block message carries no model name: the kit ships none, because the harness roster churns and the value is the consumer's. D1 owns `fork`.

**D6 holds a named tier to the binding.** Where the consumer binds its classes, a dispatch naming a `model` names one of the bound values, so a per-dispatch override chooses among the consumer's tiers rather than around them. Matching is §The tier binding's. The block message names the bound values, which are the consumer's config and not kit literals, and points at `--emit knob-values DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` through the gate binary's door, read only when D6 blocks. A dispatch naming no model is D5's. An empty binding leaves D6 inert. Under a pinned class, the alias a per-dispatch parameter can carry matches no pinned id and blocks. That is the pin held rather than bypassed at the one chokepoint that sees it, and the consumer dispatches that class through its pinned type (§The tier binding, the honest limit on pinning).

**Why a second guard rather than more rules in the budget guard.** Four reasons, each sufficient alone:

- the budget guard blocks on a **transient** condition and is deliberately knob-overridable, while these block on a **permanent** doctrine violation that must not be overridable per dispatch;
- the budget guard reads no stdin at all;
- a consumer may want either enforcement without the other, and hook registration is the only opt-in valve there is;
- their test lanes take different payload grammars.

The harness fires every hook registered on a matcher and any exit 2 blocks, so the two compose with no dispatcher between them.

**D1 blocks unconditionally**, which the template's own boundary does not: a fork stays correct where the child does the same job at the same authority, and a hook cannot read intent. No sanctioned fork use exists in this doctrine, so the valve is **unwiring the hook**, never a knob: a per-dispatch override is exactly the honour system these rules exist to end, and a knob would restore it under a better name. The block message names the two lawful alternatives, as guard-kit requires of every block message.

**D2 is inert until configured**: the default roster is empty and the kit ships no agent-type names. An inert D2 reports nothing, being a hook with no clean line to print, so a consumer does not infer coverage it has not configured.

**D2 composes with isolation's untracked-blindness cost, and the protocol template states the composition where a dispatcher reads it.** An untracked or gitignored surface is in no commit, so an isolated child cannot resolve it inside its worktree. For a type in `DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES`, dispatching unisolated is the shape D2 blocks. A roster read delegated under that mandate reported "absent" for four surfaces that carried content.

**The route is the absolute read.** The dispatcher names each such surface absolute into the main checkout. The child reads a named file there with the harness's read tool and searches under it with a read-only pipeline. The harness refuses the child's file-tool writes there, and guard-kit rule `worktree_confinement` admits only the read forms (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset). That admitted read keeps D4's isolation default workable for a sweep over an untracked corpus when the harness build carries no search tool. **The child's half stays**: a target the dispatch did not name absolute is a blindness, not a finding of absence, since a parent cannot tell a reported absence from a true empty. Cost (4) has no dispatcher half of that kind. An oracle-running sweep runs its arm inside the worktree on the stamp-matched main binary (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §lib/gate.sh, `gate_verdict_bin`), and the dispatcher classifies only whether that oracle reads a gitignored overlay.

**That constraint lives where the dispatcher reads its protocol.** A guard assertion is declined on cost (3)'s ground: the payload carries the prompt and never the corpus or oracles a sweep will run. A rule would key on whether the prompt "names a path", firing on the word and teaching dispatchers to game the wording. A consumer's own roster emitter could repair one instance and stays available, but a delegable sweep's corpus is often not that roster, so the class stays open there. Always-loaded residency is declined by §Operative residency's condition (a), since the dispatcher fires the trigger that loads the template.

**D2 is evaluated once, at dispatch, and a resume does not re-enter it.** A `SendMessage` resume of an isolated read-only child runs in the main checkout with its worktree gone, holding the write reach D2 would have refused. Measured: a child that reported its own linked worktree at dispatch printed the main checkout as `pwd` and toplevel when resumed after its hand-back. The resume's `PreToolUse` payload names neither the recipient's type nor its isolation. The dispatch payload carries a `tool_use_id` but not the agent id a resume addresses, so the guard cannot see the resume. A join would need a post-dispatch hook writing a map from agent id to dispatch shape, a state file for one class, and is declined as the heavier shape. The template treats a resumed isolated dispatch as a new dispatch for any work over the tree, restricts a resume to re-emitting what the child holds, and has the child confirm its own linked worktree at every start. That check is a request delivered where the child reads it (§Operative residency); nothing refuses a child that skips it.

**D3 advises rather than blocks.** It cannot block: a nested dispatch is legitimate and common, a stage session's read-only fan-out being one, so a block would remove a capability rather than confine one. It must not be silent: its bound reader provably never loads the protocol template (§One template, a resident pointer), so the advisory is the rule's only delivery, §Operative residency's (a)–(c) shape at a hook. It keys on `agent_id`, exact and unforgeable, never on whether the prompt "names a path"; the message carries the judgment the trigger cannot.

**An isolated read-only child's return-value obligation rides the dispatcher's prompt.** Nothing carries D3's warning into the child. An isolated read-only child whose report went to a peer name it could not resolve returned a bare `.`, which reads as a sweep that found nothing. The template's disposition (2) has the dispatcher say so in the prompt. The guard rewriting the prompt is declined: it would make the guard an author of dispatch text rather than a judge of dispatch shape. The agent-type definition is the consumer's surface, since the kit ships no agent-type names (§Layout and configuration, `DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES`); a consumer may carry the line there too, in the type's return contract.

**Degradation — fail-open, but loud**, the posture guard-kit/SPEC.md §The shell guard names for a deny-guard whose matcher proves the tool but whose rule turns on a payload field. An absent binary takes the general fail-open path (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm) before this member runs, silently, with no advisory of this guard's own. Inside a linked worktree it is absent only when the main checkout carries none either, since the front end dispatches there first. Once the arm runs, the degradation surface is:

| what is missing | behavior |
| --- | --- |
| payload unparseable, or `tool_input` absent | allow; one advisory naming D1, D2, D4, D5 and D6 unenforced |
| `subagent_type` absent from a parseable payload | D1 and D2 do not fire (nothing to match); D4 and D5 read the harness's default type, undeclared; D3 unaffected |
| `DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES` resolves empty | D2 inert, and silently so; the other rules unaffected |
| `DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES` declared but its own read fails | allow; advisory naming D2 unenforced, the other rules unaffected |
| `DELEGATION_KIT_MUTATING_TYPES` resolves empty | D4 inert, and silently so |
| `DELEGATION_KIT_MUTATING_TYPES`, `DELEGATION_KIT_REQUIRE_TIER` or `DELEGATION_KIT_AGENT_DIR` fails its own read | advisory naming D4, or D5, unenforced; the other rules unaffected |
| `DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` resolves empty | D6 inert, and silently so |
| `DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` fails its own read | advisory naming D6 unenforced; the other rules unaffected |
| the gate binary's door fails its read where D6 blocks | D6 blocks; its message names the fault in place of the door |

Two implementation facts those rows force:

- **There is no `jq`-absent arm.** The compiled member emits its own advisory envelope, serializing every interpolated value rather than quoting it by hand. The braces and key order stay literal, holding the shell guard's advise wire shape.
- **The guard reads its roster through delegation-kit's validated table, and an unrelated config fault still cannot wedge a dispatch.** The kit's knobs are static (§Layout and configuration), so `DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES` resolves in process, and the table validator runs at the kit's first resolution. A malformed *unrelated* delegation knob therefore fails this roster's read, which is the table's read-fails rows: the typo costs one rule's enforcement, loudly, and never the dispatch. A validator fault fails every read of the table at once, so it names D2, D4, D5 and D6 together. A roster **resolved to nothing** is silent; one whose own read fails is loud.

### One template, a resident pointer

`templates/agent-execution.md` is the single source for the protocol, a binding-shim *template* in lifecycle-kit's grammar (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §check-skill-binding). The consumer creates `.claude/commands/agent-execution.md` as a shim that names the template and binds its two slots, the shared-file roster and the validate battery. `check-skill-binding` enforces the slot pairing on the consumer side.

A spec reference to one of those rules uses the citation grammar `the template's **<name>** rule`, where `<name>` is a bullet's bold lead-in verbatim minus its trailing period. `check-rule-citation` resolves every such citation in §The delegation model forward into the template, so a lead-in rename cannot silently dangle a reference. It is the publisher's own gate and does not ship: its subject is this SPEC, which the payload withholds (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §Consumer payload). Its fixture pair passes a fixture-local spec and template as its two positional arguments, a citation resolving to a lead-in against one naming an absent lead-in.

`templates/dispatch-checklists.md` is a second, load-triggered template the protocol template reaches by one pointer line, for a deletion, rename or heavy cross-spec audit dispatch. It is never resident and binds no slots, so `check-skill-binding` does not scan it. It carries the mechanical pre-flight for those shapes: the importer, collision and sweep-verification checks. The protocol template stays complete alone. The width, journal and validate rules live once there and are cited from the checklists, never restated. This is load-trigger residency a rung deeper: a subset only a narrower trigger needs sits behind its own pointer.

The consumer's CLAUDE.md carries no digest of the bullets, only a resident pointer: the pre-authorization sentence (consumer judgment on what delegation needs no ask) and `/agent-execution`. A rule is resident only when it has no load trigger. Every protocol bullet triggers at `Agent` dispatch **for the role that dispatches**, which already meets a mechanical seam: the per-dispatch budget guard, whose block message names `/agent-execution`. Honest limit: the guards enforce exactly the rules that pass a chokepoint, the budget check and the dispatch-shape rules (§The delegation model), and protocol literacy beyond those stays unenforced. A session that dispatches without invoking the skill carries only the resident pointer for everything else.

**That role qualifier is load-bearing.** A *dispatched* role fires no such trigger: it is executing a stage, and no stage skill or stage template loads this protocol, so for that reader the pointer leaves the rule unreached. §Operative residency governs that case. The bounded imperative it sanctions in a consumer's always-loaded agent definition is this doctrine applied to a different reader, not an exception to it.

### Operative residency

A rule may be **restated as an imperative in a surface that does not own it** when all three of these hold:

- **(a) Unreachable trigger.** The actor the rule binds never fires a trigger that loads the rule's owning doc. This is a property of the *reader*, settled by asking which triggers that reader actually fires.
- **(b) Imperative only.** What is restated is the instruction. The reasoning, the failure analysis, and the mechanism stay with the owner.
- **(c) Adjacent citation.** The restatement names the owning surface beside itself, so the owner stays the single place the rule is explained and changed.

Grounds a surface authors for its own instruction are no restatement and take no sanction; Content-tiering / SSOT places them (doctrine-kit/DOCTRINE.md).

Each condition has a reader at a transition. (a) and (b) are read by whoever authors a restatement, at authoring (build's ritual, the amendment Definition of Done), and by a reviewer or an authoring stage assessing an existing one. (c) is the pointer any later reader, and any content-tiering check, follows back to the owner. The reader the sanction exists for inspects no condition: the bound actor, which receives the imperative at the tier it always loads.

**The anti-licence clause is part of the rule.** The sanction is keyed on (a): a rule the bound actor's own skill or template already loads fails (a) and stays a pointer. Without that clause this reads as "duplicate when it feels important", the failure content-tiering exists to prevent. **(a) licenses one restatement and not one per reader**, so where the bound actor is *any session* the carrier is the consumer's always-loaded tier and the templates those sessions load carry nothing. context-kit/SPEC.md §The consumer footprint rules that case and states the one thing a template may still say.

**Worked cases.** The provenance floor (§Resume journal) and the turn-end worktree reap bind a session that **has dispatched**, which loads `templates/agent-execution.md` through its own trigger. Both fail (a) and stay template-tier: a resident copy would be a second source paid for by every session that never dispatches, and reading as important is the licence (a) withholds. The isolated child's blindness clause and its linked-worktree check at start and at resume bind a dispatched read-only agent, which fires no trigger that loads the template. Both qualify, and ride the carrier already stating the sibling gate-unavailability clause.

**This is load-trigger residency's own logic.** That doctrine makes a rule resident only when no stage, skill or tool-call trigger loads it, and the question is *exists for whom*. A trigger the bound actor never fires is, for that actor, no trigger. The doctrine's justification then inverts: nothing loads the rule anyway, so deferring costs everything.

**(b) is what satisfies content-tiering.** The restatement is still a restatement, which is why it needs this sanction. But its drift surface is one instruction rather than a slab, and the rule is explained and changed in one place.

The attested subjects are the template's **Background + notification, never poll** rule and its **Findings you will act on are durable before you act on them** rule, both binding a dispatched session. Sessions that read a *descriptive* pointer to them at the always-loaded tier derived no constraint from it. Under load a descriptive sentence reads as background about one's situation, not as a constraint on the next action. The defect is pointer-versus-operative-statement, never absence.

**Operative is not obeyed.** A dispatched session ended its turn on work still running while the waiting rule sat in its own agent definition as a bare imperative, the compliant form. Residency makes a rule reachable at the point of use, and reachable is not obeyed. Every restatement this sanction licenses is a request delivered where it is read: worth its small drift surface, never a substitute for an oracle where one is buildable.

**Propagation finds its carriers by grep, and carries the wording.** The template's *when either rule changes here, propagate* header sends a change to every restatement. Each carrier takes an addition sized to its own voice rather than a paste, and none takes the refused alternatives, which is (b)'s line. The carrier set is re-derived at the merge by grepping the rule's phrasing, never read off a roster: a guard's corrective help text restating the clause was found that way, outside every recorded list. A restatement propagates the rule's **wording**, so a wording defect ships in every carrier: N carriers make N copies of a bug rather than N chances to catch it.

**A liveness wait takes `while`, never `until`.** Backgrounded waiters died before their conditions went true with their producers alive, and §bin/wait-probe measured why. Every trial on a completion-marker predicate outlived its condition, on both harness forms and on a detached shell child, at every producer duration swept, so no form carries a wall-clock ceiling in that range. Every early exit tested the producer's **liveness**, and reproduced on the harness-uninvolved control in milliseconds. `until` ends when its condition is true and `kill -0` is true while the producer is alive, so `until` around a liveness test exits at once, cleanly, with the producer running. `until` takes a *done* predicate and `while` a *still-running* one. guard-kit rules `bare_sleep` and `background_no_record` walk `do … done` spans without reading the loop keyword, so the `while` form is exempt exactly as the `until` form is (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset).

**Every carrier spells the PID as a literal, `while kill -0 <pid> 2>/dev/null; do sleep N; done`, with `<pid>` the number read out of the record.** It is the one spelling guard-kit's rules agree on: rule `bounded_wait` grants it, and rule `bare_sleep`'s corrective names it (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset). An expansion such as `"$pid"` meets rule `expansion`'s block, so a carrier spelling one teaches a form the protocol's own guard refuses on the first wait.

**No gate is owed over the act; one is owed over its harm, and it exists.** No check can read a session's choice to end a turn: the act leaves no tracked artifact and passes no `PreToolUse` chokepoint. The harness's turn-end event does see it, and §The turn-end liveness hook refuses there. The harm passes a chokepoint too. A tracked-tree mutation while a recorded producer is still writing arrives as an ordinary tool call, blocked by guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset, whose design §The delegation model owns). Its bound travels with it: it reaches only a session that recorded.

**The anti-restatement gates do not reach a sanctioned restatement.** `check-shim-restatement` scans binding shims under the skills directory against the consumer's always-loaded file plus kit templates. `check-surface-duplication`'s predicate is a glossary term's definition on a canonical SPEC surface. Neither reaches an agent definition, so the sanction ships with **no gate exemption**, and nothing in the battery catches a *non*-compliant restatement either: (a)–(c) are review-enforced. Of the consequence oracles, guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` **prevents** the consequence at the tool call that would cause it. `check-producer-liveness` **detects** a producer still running at the next stage's entry (evidence-kit/SPEC.md §check-producer-liveness). The second detects late by construction, and a firing at an iteration's last stage has no entry after it, so it is the backstop behind the first.

**Interception, not durability, separates the gated dispatch-shape rules from this one.** Both bind acts that leave no tracked artifact. A dispatch passes a chokepoint the harness fires a hook on; a turn end passes none the dispatch-shape rules could use, though its own `SubagentStop` event is reachable (§The turn-end liveness hook). So "no tracked artifact" is why neither rule gets a gate over the tree, and never by itself a reason to stop looking for an oracle.

## The turn-end liveness hook

The `subagent-stop-liveness` hook is an opt-in `SubagentStop` hook, inert until a consumer registers it. It **refuses a dispatched session's turn end while a launch record under the scratch dir names a live producer, or while the harness's task view shows a shell task of that session still running**. The harness does not defer a subagent's stop while a background child is live (§The probe is asymmetric). So the axis §The delegation model's waiting rule exists for holds at the turn end, and a blocking hook is the only lever.

**The refusal is unconditional, and what follows binds any change to it.** First, a **capped** variant is refused: refuse only on a `verdict=red live=yes` reading carrying at least one record, and stay advisory otherwise. Every narrowing below stands on its own stated grounds, never as a safety margin, and a boundary that coincides with the capped variant's says so. Second, a change is weighed on its **class**, an observer turned refuser at a turn end, never on the size of the edit that delivers it.

**The event is `SubagentStop`, never `Stop`.** A dispatched session is a subagent, so its turn end fires `SubagentStop`, and every attested firing of the waiting rule was a dispatched session's. `Stop` is deliberately not registered: the main session's turn end has no attested firing, so registering it would widen the subject past the evidence. The main session's turn end stays unreached.

**The contract: exit 2 on `red`, `corrupt` or `unresolved`, or while the payload's `background_tasks` shows a running `shell` task the emitting session launched; exit 0 on every other path.** Neither path writes hook JSON, except the knob-fault decline. That decline writes the `systemMessage` envelope gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The harness-integration arm rules, so the operator learns the turn end went unguarded. The hook reads its payload from stdin and asks the liveness reader whether any launch record under `${GATE_SDK_TMP_DIR:-.tmp}` names a live producer. It appends one line to `DELEGATION_KIT_STOP_LOG`, then exits 0 or exits 2 with its refusal on **stderr**.

**The refusal needs no emitter.** The harness's published hook contract lists `SubagentStop` as an event a hook can block and exit 2 as the blocking route. When the hook emits no JSON decision, its **stderr is shown to the session model as the blocking reason**. So the refusal mints no guard-kit primitive, though all three of guard-kit's emitters hardcode `hookEventName:"PreToolUse"`. The hook **does not source guard-kit's lib**, which keeps delegation-kit free of a dependency on guard-kit being vendored. Reading the payload costs the compiled member one stdin read and one parse.

**There is no advisory tier at this event.** At exit 0 a hook's stderr goes to the debug log only, and the session model never sees it. Exit 2 is the only delivery, so the refusal has no softer form.

**Honest limit:** that contract is the harness's published one and nothing in this tree. It stands on the footing §The delegation model declares for the `PreToolUse` payload roster: a harness revision reshaping the event is drift no gate here detects, and only re-reading the contract catches it.

**The predicate is the reader's exit class, and it refuses on three of seven arms.** Reader **exit 2 is read through two names**, chosen by the hook's own `*.run` count: `corrupt` over a non-empty record set, `unresolved` over an empty one. The count picks the **name**, never the decision: both refuse.

| reading | the hook holds | decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `green` | no live producer | log, exit 0 — there is nothing to refuse |
| `red` | a live producer under a launch record | log, **exit 2** — the whole subject |
| `corrupt` | reader exit 2 over a non-empty record set: a record that does not parse | log, **exit 2** — see below |
| `unresolved` | reader exit 2 over an **empty** record set: a reader that could not run at all | log, **exit 2 once** — allows when `stop_hook_active` is true; see below |
| `unavailable` | no reader resolved: an **override** that fails the resolution predicate — the default always resolves | log, exit 0 |
| `unstarted` | a resolved reader the operating system would not start | log, exit 0 |
| `error` | a configured reader that ran and did not answer | log, exit 0 |

**`unstarted` is a reader whose argv resolved and whose spawn the operating system refused:** `ETXTBSY`, `ENOENT` from an absent shebang interpreter, `EACCES`, `ENOEXEC`, `ENOMEM` or `EAGAIN`. **`unavailable` is an override that resolved to no reader.** They take two fixes, a reader or host that refused to start and a knob to correct, so they take two names. One name over both would be a fail-open with no signature, in which a reader that cannot start reads as one never configured.

**`corrupt` refuses, diverging on purpose from guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer`, which declines on a record that does not parse.** A guard is not where a corruption verdict is taken, and `check-producer-liveness` already exits 2 on one. That rule reads the records **one at a time**, so a malformed record declines for itself while a sibling naming a live PID still blocks. This hook reads the whole set through **one exit code**, resolved *exit 2 wins over red wins over green* (evidence-kit/SPEC.md §check-producer-liveness). Allowing on `corrupt` would let **one malformed record anywhere under the scratch dir suppress every refusal in the tree**, a bypass the per-record rule cannot have. guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset records the divergence from its side.

**The corrupt arm is cheap to be wrong about.** `check-producer-liveness` is a battery member, so the next commit reds on a corrupt record. The false-refusal window is one malformed record's lifetime; without the arm, the bypass window is however long a session leaves one in place.

**`unresolved` is the same refusal under another diagnosis, and its ground is the reader's own contract.** Set mode derives corruption **per record**, so over an empty glob there is nothing to aggregate and the gate **cannot** return corrupt. Exit 2 at `records=0` is the reader failing for a reason that is no record's. Its routine producer is a **worktree-isolated dispatch** whose reader dispatches to a compiled binary: a fresh `git worktree add` checkout carries no build output, so the reader fails closed before reading a record. The hook already counts `records` by its own glob over the same directory, so the split costs no new field, knob or reader. The row stays: a reader whose binary is missing in a main checkout lands here, as does an override that does not meet the worktree requirement below.

**The count labels the diagnosis and decides nothing.** `unresolved` maps to `decision=refuse` although the case provably is not record corruption. Allowing it would narrow the secured refusal set at `records=0`, and the unconditional refusal is worth more than the one provably-empty reading it costs. A reader who re-derives the *cannot be corruption* proof finds the decision here.

**`unresolved` refuses once.** A refused turn end is retried, and a reader that cannot run stays unable under anything the child does, so an unbounded refusal is a **loop**: one read-only audit spent 142 tool uses refusing and lost its report. The hook reads the payload's `stop_hook_active`, true when the harness is already continuing because of a stop hook. On that firing the `unresolved` arm allows and logs `verdict=unresolved decision=allow`, so triage tells a bounded refusal from a first one. The first refusal on every arm stands, which is why allowing `unresolved` inside a linked worktree is refused. Under the bound, a worktree-isolated read-only dispatch was refused once and allowed at its next stop, its report delivered.

**Loop protection reads the producer on the arms whose condition resolves.** `red` and `corrupt` ignore `stop_hook_active`: their condition ends when the producer ends or the record is deleted, so the rule's own subject bounds the loop. The field is contracted, listed in the `SubagentStop` input schema with the reference's guidance to check it "to avoid blocking on a condition that will never resolve". The harness's 8-consecutive-block cap did not bound the measured loop, presumably because a child's tool calls between refusals break the consecutive count. That is observed, not explained, and this tree relies on no such cap.

**Record count decides no refusal anywhere in this hook.** The capped variant's "at least one record" is vacuous here, since the reader cannot return red over an empty set. The `corrupt`/`unresolved` split is the second coincidence with it: exit 2 genuinely occurs at zero records, and the count still only names which refusing arm a reading gets.

**`unavailable`, `unstarted` and `error` allow on the degradation posture.** Each means the hook obtained no reading: an override failed the resolution predicate, the operating system would not start a resolved reader, or a reader ran and failed to answer. Refusing there would refuse every turn end behind a mis-set knob, a refusing host or a broken reader, in a kit that ships this hook opt-in and inert. `unresolved` is a configured reader that **ran and returned its own fail-closed verdict**, and second-guessing it would be the fail-open its exit 2 exists against. This is guard-kit/SPEC.md §The shell guard's fail-open-but-loud posture for a deny-guard turning on an external reader, the posture §The delegation model's dispatch guard takes. The `verdict=error`, `verdict=unstarted` and `spawn` values supply the "loud".

**`unstarted` does not refuse, not even once.** Refusing under the `stop_hook_active` bound would widen the refusal set past the arms this section rules refusing. A spawn the host refused is no reading at all, the class of `unavailable` and `error`. What the arm buys is a degradation the close-stage triage can count.

**The harness's task view refuses beside the reading, on the session's own launches.** A `background_tasks` element with `type` `shell` and `status` `running` refuses whatever the reader said, when the session launched it. The session launched it when the element's `id` occurs as a whole token in the transcript the payload's `agent_transcript_path` names. The harness records there every shell task a session launches, a moved call included, while the view carries no owner. An absent or unreadable transcript counts every running `shell` element, so scoping narrows only off a key the hook holds and never fails open. The condition covers a call the harness moved to the background on its timeout, which no launch record names. It supplements the record set and replaces nothing: a detached producer the view does not enumerate is still the record set's. Like `red` it is unconditional and ignores `stop_hook_active`, because it resolves when the task ends.

**A malformed view degrades to the record-set decision.** An element of `type` `subagent`, any other `status`, an absent key, a non-array value and a non-object element contribute nothing: a supplement's absence must not refuse. The hook reads `type` and `status` to decide and logs nothing of the view or the transcript, not even a count, so the grammar below and §What `background_tasks` carries' key-names-only ruling are untouched. A task-held refusal stays legible, because `verdict` stays the reader's. It shows as `decision=refuse` beside a non-refusing verdict (`green`, `unavailable`, `unstarted`, `error`), a pairing no other arm produces. Its stderr names the arm and tells the session to await the task's completion notification.

**The ownership test is a whole-token match, read lazily and once.** A whole token is bounded on both sides by a character outside `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` or by the file's start or end, so an `id` inside a longer token is not a match. An element whose `id` is absent, not a string or empty counts as own. The transcript is read only when the view holds a running `shell` element, and at most once per firing.

**The view is scoped where the record set is shared.** The record set binds every session (the shared-scratch-dir paragraph below), because a record declares a producer and observers write none. The view makes no producer/observer split and spans more than the emitter, and its commonest foreign element in a dispatch is the dispatcher's own wait on the child. Unscoped, that wait turns circular. A dispatcher's recorded producer still refuses the child through the record set. **Honest limit:** the transcript is harness state no contract covers, as the payload is. A revision that stopped writing task ids there would fail the arm open on the session's own moved call. Only a re-run of the two-sided live check below catches it.

**Three narrower repairs are refused.** Unscoped, the arm stalled a no-tool child on its dispatcher's backgrounded observer loop, nine refusals in 24 seconds. *Refusing once* would let a session end its turn on its **own** moved call at the second try, weakening the case the arm exists for. *Retiring the arm* leaves the record set blind to the moved call again (§The probe is asymmetric). *Logging an owner field* adds nothing: the decision needs the key, the log does not, and a value log is refused (§What `background_tasks` carries).

**The scoped arm is live-checked from both sides.** *Foreign:* a no-tool child whose dispatcher held a running backgrounded loop logged `decision=allow` at its only stop. *Own:* a child whose foreground call the harness moved to the background was refused at each turn end beside `verdict=green`, then allowed once the moved call completed. That settles both premises the join rests on. A `shell` element's `id` is the token the transcript records as the launch's `backgroundTaskId`, and `agent_transcript_path` names the emitting session's own transcript.

**Whether a finished task leaves the array is not a premise.** The condition keys on `status` being `running`, so a finished task that leaves the array or carries another status allows either way. The residual is a finished task still reporting `running`, which would hold the refusal across the session's retries. Its signature is a run of `decision=refuse` beside a non-refusing verdict that outlasts the completion notification; the live check read none. **Honest limit:** that is one reading, not a bound, and a session caught in it has no in-band way out. A session's own backgrounded wait loop is a running `shell` task, so a turn end during it refuses, which is the waiting rule itself and needs no exemption.

**A harness helper's firing is not a dispatched session's turn end, and it allows on every arm.** While a dispatched session runs, the harness fires `SubagentStop` about every thirty seconds for a progress-summary helper. One out-of-band read of raw payloads over eleven firings attributed them, taken as §What `background_tasks` carries took its read. A helper's `agent_type` is empty, its `agent_id` is new at every firing, and its `agent_transcript_path` names no existing file. Its `last_assistant_message` summarizes the running session's latest step. A dispatched session's own tool steps fire nothing; its turn end carries its type, the `id` the view lists for it, and an existing transcript.

**Refusing a helper delivers nothing.** Refused helpers never fired again, and no later firing carries `stop_hook_active` true. A helper's absent transcript sent the task view down its unscoped branch, so a refused helper only overstated the stalls the close-stage triage counts. Allowing a helper narrows no refusal, since its firing is not the subject. The hook still logs it with the reader's verdict and `decision=allow`, so `red` or `corrupt` beside `allow` marks a helper and no other arm. A task-held refusal in the log is always the session's own.

**The helper test is a conjunction of three marks, each read positively.** The `agent_type` key is present and empty. The `agent_transcript_path` key names a non-empty path the filesystem reports as not found. The `agent_id` key is non-empty, and the `background_tasks` array holds no `subagent` element carrying it. An absent key, an empty id or a missing view fails its mark, so an unreadable payload is never a helper. The member's cases cover every combination of the marks, each absent key and a missing view, and assert that only the full conjunction allows. The hook logs none of the three values. **Honest limit:** each mark is uncontracted harness state, so a real session is misclassed only if all three drift at once. A revision reshaping the helper makes it refuse again, losing no refusal; one under which a real session meets all three marks fails the hook open for it. Only a fresh read of the raw payloads catches either.

**An unreadable payload does not disable enforcement.** The verdict comes from the liveness reader over the run directory, so an unparseable payload costs the log line its `event`, `session` and `keys` columns and leaves the verdict exact. Two decisions read the payload. `stop_hook_active` flips an `unresolved` refusal into an allow, and the task-view condition drops with the payload, leaving the record-set decision. An unreadable **transcript** leaves that condition unscoped rather than dropping it. Every other arm decides on the verdict alone, and no separate JSON program is on this path.

**The reader runs under a wait bound the member applies unconditionally, so a hung reader is never read as a live producer.** A timeout is an unmapped exit code, so it is `error` and allows; a refusal is only ever the reader's own verdict. **The bounded call's error is typed.** A **spawn** failure, from the spawn funnel or the operating system, carries the operating system's own error and reads `unstarted`. A **wait** failure on a child that did start reads `error`: a reader that ran and did not answer.

**The liveness reading reuses `check-producer-liveness` and copies no grammar.** §The delegation model rules that the reading affordance which would earn its place already exists and is that gate. Re-implementing the `pid=<n> run=<key>` parse and the PID predicate would be a third copy of a grammar evidence-kit owns. So the hook invokes the reader in **set mode** over the scratch dir, which quantifies the per-record verdict and resolves the set. It maps the exit class onto `verdict`, `live` and the exit code.

**The default reader is the running binary.** An empty knob, the default, resolves to `current_exe()`, spawned with the gate name and the scratch dir as its argv. The executable that is running is present by definition, so a tree that can fire this hook can take a reading. The kit ships no default naming a path no tree resolves: such a fake default reads as a shipped capability and is none.

**`DELEGATION_KIT_LIVENESS_CMD` is the override, for a consumer whose reader is not this gate.** It is an indexed argv, one line per element, spawned directly with no shell and the scratch dir appended after its last element. A consumer names an interpreter as an element when its reader needs one. **Executability is the resolution predicate:** a path carrying a separator must carry the execute bit, and a bare name must resolve on `PATH`. An override that fails it resolves to no reader and logs `verdict=unavailable decision=allow` on every line, never run under an interpreter the kit chose. That is honest degradation, preferable to a third parse that would drift from its owner. One that resolves and cannot be spawned reads `unstarted`. The knob resolves no gate name, which would be a kit-contract change, the ground evidence-kit/SPEC.md §check-evidence-manifest gives for the pre-flight knob.

**The default is spawned, never called in process, because the predicate is a child's exit class.** The seven-arm table is written over a child process's status, with "no reading at all" as an arm no return value can express. An in-process call would give the default and an override two code paths with two arm sets, and `unavailable` would become unreachable for the default alone. One code path keeps every row true of both, which the close-stage triage and the refusal text depend on. For the same reason a spawn that never started reads `unstarted`, not `error`, which names a reader that ran.

**A consumer's override must resolve from a worktree-isolated dispatch.** The default is discharged by construction: the running binary answers inside a linked worktree with no build output, with nothing to resolve. The bar sits on the override because only its consumer knows where its reader lives. An override that dispatches to a compiled binary fails closed there, and the hook refuses on `unresolved`. The harness returns only a dispatched session's **last** assistant message, so that refusal **displaces the child's report** on the only channel the dispatcher reads. The returned text reads as a liveness complaint, not a dropped report. Where a consumer's dispatch rules require isolation for read-only agents, every such dispatch arms this, and no dispatch shape avoids it while staying in contract. The ordinary cost is one wasted round-trip; the worst attested tail looped across three resumptions, spent a full context budget and returned nothing.

**The kit carries no resolving adapter for an override, deliberately**, so the `unresolved` and `unavailable` rows stay reachable for a consumer whose override names a compiled-binary path. A consumer who needs one owns it: resolve the configured binary against the **main checkout** when the path is absent and the cwd is a linked worktree, deriving the main checkout from `git rev-parse --git-common-dir`. That narrow resolution is safe because `check-producer-liveness` reads only `*.run` records, so a main-checkout binary answers the same question. It is not safe for gates in general, which `check-gate-binary-fresh` compares against their own tree's source. A verdict-bearing arm takes a main-checkout binary only through the stamp comparison of gate-sdk/SPEC.md §lib/gate.sh, `gate_verdict_bin`.

**Such a resolution resolves an artifact and builds none.** An isolated agent still may not build the crate, and no hook reaches a worktree that does not exist at dispatch time. The front end's fail-open branch takes a main-checkout resolution on another ground (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The harness-integration arm): it dispatches the session's policy hooks, not a gate's verdict, and leaves the knob's resolution untouched.

**One line per firing**, appended, space-delimited `key=value` after a leading timestamp:

```
<UTC ISO-8601>  event=<hook_event_name|->  session=<session_id|->  live=<yes|no>  verdict=<green|red|corrupt|unresolved|error|unavailable|unstarted>  spawn=<the spawn error, one token|->  records=<n>  runs=<comma-separated run keys|->  decision=<refuse|allow>  keys=<comma-separated top-level payload keys>
```

Every field has a reader at a named transition, and no field is carried without one. The **close-stage triage** reads the log at the close-surface drain, where it is read and cleared; "the triage" below is that reader at that transition.

- **timestamp** — read by the triage to order firings and to tell a fresh line from a stale one.
- **`event`** — the payload's `hook_event_name`. Read at the **first** firing, to confirm the event fires for a dispatched session and is spelled `SubagentStop`.
- **`session`** — the payload's `session_id`, `-` otherwise. Read to separate one top-level session's firings from another's in a shared log. **It is not the subagent's own id.** A dispatched agent and its dispatcher log the *same* `session_id`, measured across a worktree-isolated child's log and its dispatcher's. Neither matches a stage stamp's identifier, so this field attributes no firing to a stage session. `agent_id` attributes a dispatched session's own firing, carrying the `id` the view lists for it, while a helper's is new at every firing. The hook reads `agent_id` only to class a helper and logs it nowhere (the omissions paragraph below). Raw payloads stay unlogged, and reading them is operator-class under the no-values ruling (§What `background_tasks` carries).
- **`live`** — `yes` exactly when the reader reported a live producer. It stays two-valued and paired with `verdict`.
- **`verdict`**, **`records`** — the reader's exit class and the number of `*.run` records under the scratch dir, read together. `records=0` makes `live=no` uninformative; `records=2 live=no` says records existed and their producers had exited. `verdict=corrupt` carries `live=no`, so the pair is the reading, never `live` alone. The pair also **names** the exit-2 arm, `corrupt` at `records>0` and `unresolved` at `records=0`, and the two want different fixes.
- **`spawn`** — the operating system's own rendering of a spawn error, sanitized to one token by the record's whitespace rule (for example `Text_file_busy_(os_error_26)`). It is populated on `verdict=unstarted` alone and `-` on every other firing. No errno table is kept: the value is derived, never transcribed. The triage tells from it whether a failure was **transient** (`ETXTBSY`, `EAGAIN`, `ENOMEM`: a re-run clears it) or **persistent** (`ENOENT`, `EACCES`, `ENOEXEC`: fix the override). This member's case assertions print it at a red.
- **`runs`** — the **record set the decision was taken over**: the run dir's `*.run` basenames with the suffix stripped, sorted and comma-joined, `-` over an empty set. It is the listing `records` counts, so the count and the names cannot disagree. The triage reads it for **whether the guard is working or wedged**. A refusal whose run key names a *sibling* producer is the guard working; one naming the refused session's **own** work is a session waiting on itself. A session whose wait wrote a record naming its own pid was refused 21 times in ten minutes, on rows a correct refusal also prints; this field makes that legible at the first row.
- **`runs`' honest limit:** above `records=1` it names the candidate set, not the match. `check-producer-liveness` prints the matched record on stdout, and the bounded call discards that stream. At `records=1` it names the match exactly, and every attested refusal read `live=yes records=1 decision=refuse`. A superset is still more than the nothing the record carried without it. A run key is a name the writing session chose for its work, not a harness identifier, so the field carries no identity and attributes nothing.
- **`verdict=error`** — a **configured** reader that ran and did not answer: an unmapped exit code, the bound firing, or a wait that failed on a child that did start. The triage reads it to tell *this tree's override resolves to nothing* (`unavailable`) from *this tree's enforcement is broken* (`error`).
- **`verdict=unresolved`** — a configured, readable reader that ran, exited 2 and held no record to be about. It takes its own name because its fix differs: the reader could not run, where `error` answered off-contract and `unavailable` resolved to nothing. It also **refuses**, so the triage asks of it whether a refusal was diagnostic rather than about a real producer.
- **`verdict=unstarted`** — a reader that resolved and that the operating system would not start. The triage reads it, with `spawn` beside it, to tell a refused start from a mis-configuration (`unavailable`).
- **`decision`** — `refuse` exactly when the hook exits 2, `allow` otherwise, on every firing. The triage reads it for whether a firing was **acted on**, and it makes a refusal countable. `verdict` cannot answer that across the commit landing enforcement: one append-only log spans it, and the same `verdict=red` line means *observed* before it and *refused* after. It derives from neither `live` nor `verdict`: a `corrupt` refusal carries `live=no`, and a task-view refusal a non-refusing verdict. `DELEGATION_KIT_STOP_LOG` is capture, so an isolated agent's line lands in the main checkout's log, not the reaped worktree (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The workflow directory). An isolated firing whose reader resolves reads `verdict=green records=0 decision=allow`; one whose reader does not reads `unresolved … refuse`.
- **`keys`** — the payload's top-level key set, **sorted**, nothing more. Read **once**, at the first firing, to settle what a `SubagentStop` payload carries without asserting it in advance; it showed a top-level `background_tasks`. Nothing reads the order, since the field answers a set question and the record is parsed by key; keeping document order would cost a dependency. Values stay unlogged: they would put transcript paths and prompt ids into a file for no reader. §What `background_tasks` carries settles that key's contents off this field.

**`keys` is the one free-ish field and stays last**, so the space-delimited parse never steps over it, and `decision` sits before it. `runs` lands before `keys` and beside `records`, the count and the names of one set. `spawn` sits immediately after `verdict`, the one value it qualifies. `runs` is a new field rather than a widened `records`, whose count keeps its named reader.

**The record's field set is OPEN.** A reader parses the record **by key**, never by position or arity, and the writer's field set is one table in the member's own module. A field added to the table appears in the line without any existing reader changing. This settles only that the **shape does not lock**, never what is logged: the list above is the whole of it.

**The record set stands in for the matched run key, which is unavailable to the hook.** The bounded call discards both of the reader's streams, so the hook holds an exit code and nothing else. Recovering the matched key would need a bounded **and capturing** call, or the hook parsing records itself, which the reuse paragraph above forbids. `runs` gives the same discrimination from a listing the hook already performs.

**Two omissions are deliberate: no session-attribution field and no refusal counter.** `session_id` does not attribute, and the bound on repeated refusals is the producer's own life, not a count. **`agent_id` and `agent_type` are refused as logged fields.** No reader in this record needs either, because the hook decides on them in process, and both are *payload values* under the key-names-only ruling. Should a reader appear, a logged attribution field is a new name to file, never a delta to this refusal.

**Dropping `session=` is refused too.** This list's rule is that **no field is carried without a named reader**, never that every field discriminates every question. `session` has its reader, so dropping it would delete a live reader to fix a misreading, and `runs` carries the discrimination `session` never claimed. `runs` is a *record* field, not an *identity* field: it names the consumer's own launch records and mints no field the refusals above reject.

**`records` is the hook's own `*.run` glob, never parsed out of the reader.** `check-producer-liveness` publishes a count only on its green line, printing one finding per blocking record on red and no total. Same directory, same glob, same number, and the field stays meaningful when no reader resolved. The reader's first output line is not carried either: it adds only the blocking record paths, and free text would break the space-delimited parse. `runs` is the same listing's membership, so this covers both fields.

**The glob is taken *after* the reader has run.** A record created between the two would, in the other order, produce `records=0` beside a reading that saw it, naming a genuine corruption `unresolved`. Globbing after does not close the window, which only a lock could, but it inverts how an in-flight record errs. A record appearing during the reader's run is counted, so the reading is named `corrupt` and the message points at a record that exists. The residual window is the corrupt arm's own price.

**The refusal message names the finding and the lawful exits, as guard-kit requires of every block message.** It branches three ways, one arm per refusing verdict. On `red` it states that a launch record names a live producer, and on `corrupt` that a record does not parse. On `unresolved` it states that the reader produced no reading and held no record. `red` and `corrupt` share the two exits guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` names. **Wait for the producer on its own artifact, in a loop that ends when the condition goes true, or delete the record once the producer has exited.** `unresolved` has no producer to wait for and no record to delete. It names the **reader** as the thing to fix, plus the isolated-dispatch limit (`templates/agent-execution.md`) and the lawful response, reporting the gate unavailable and returning. Every arm says the turn may not end on it and names the reader command with the run directory. On `red` it shows the record set, on `corrupt` the malformed record, and on `unresolved` the reader's own reason for failing.

**The `red` and `corrupt` arms also name the record set the decision was taken over**, the `runs` derivation rendered for the refused session. At `records=1` that hands it the one fact deciding whether to wait or to retract its own record. `unresolved` takes no such field, since its record set is empty by construction. A run key carries no session identity, and the hook has none to carry.

**Folding `unresolved` onto the `corrupt` arm is the mistake the three-way branch exists against.** A two-way branch would print "a launch record does not parse" over a case holding no record, pointing at a remedy that cannot be carried out. It is the misdiagnosis gate-sdk/SPEC.md §lib/gate.sh guards `gate_command`'s callers against, a build problem re-diagnosed as a missing gate. A refusal naming no reachable remedy invites a read-only agent to invent a mutating one.

**There is no knob, and unwiring the hook is the valve.** §The delegation model rules the same for the dispatch guard's D1 unconditional block. A per-dispatch override is the honour system these rules exist to end, and a knob would restore it under a better name.

**The ordinary escape is not the operator's.** Unwiring edits the consumer's settings, operator-class work no stage session may do, so it cannot be the only recovery path. The record set is the session's own artifact, and `check-producer-liveness` names the blocking record. Deleting a record whose producer has exited is, in guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer`'s words, "not a workaround, it is the statement of fact becoming false and being retracted". The operator-class valve is the last resort.

**The shared scratch dir is the subject.** The reading is over `${GATE_SDK_TMP_DIR:-.tmp}`, which concurrent sessions in one checkout share, so one session's record can refuse another's turn end. guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` already binds **every** session to that set, blocking index-writing `git` while any `*.run` record names a live PID. Extending that from committing to ending a turn widens the *act set*, not the subject. Narrowing the turn-end rule to the record's writer would leave the two disagreeing about whose producers bind whom on one record set.

**The log is capture-tier**, gitignored and advisory (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The workflow directory), and declares itself on the close-surface roster (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §The close-surface roster) with its rotation as the reclaim path. Close reads the drain file that command prints, never the live log, then removes it with `--done`:

close-surface: .workflow/subagent-stop-liveness.log advisory reclaim=bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --emit capture-drain .workflow/subagent-stop-liveness.log

`advisory` rather than `forced`, on the reasoning guard-kit's friction log takes: nothing refuses a close that skips it, and a visible skip is the honest mode for a log whose enforcement lives in the exit code rather than in the file.

**No gate observes the registration.** `check-settings-pins` asserts only the pinned paths in the consumer's pins file, none under `hooks`. `check-settings-paths` resolves a registration that is present, its script path and its `--hook` member (context-kit/SPEC.md §check-settings-paths), but never asks that one is. So an absent registration reds nothing, and an unwired tree has no turn-end enforcement with nothing in the battery saying so. A session decides the wiring by reading this section, never by predicting a verdict.

**The resolved reader is observed**, on `scripts/gate-tests/subagent-stop-reader.test.sh`, which fires this repo's wired `--hook subagent-stop-liveness` arm over a scratch run dir it builds. An empty dir must read `green` and allow, with `runs` the absent token. A record naming an always-alive PID must read `red`, `decision=refuse` and exit 2, with the record's key in the row and the message. The reason names the argv the hook spawned, so the lane sees the *default* resolving. A real, spawnable override producing no reading over an empty dir must read `unresolved decision=refuse`, the isolated-dispatch shape; `unavailable` there fails the lane by name.

**`runs` carries a firing and a non-firing case in both lanes**, the fixture-pair discipline transplanted to a hook member, which owes no `good/`+`bad/` pair. A firing over records asserts the row names their keys, sorted and comma-joined; a firing over an empty dir asserts the absent token, so an absent set is told from a field rendering nothing.

**The arity is under test, because the line zips the field table against a values array and a length mismatch truncates silently.** One case asserts the two arrays agree and one asserts the line's field count. A third lists every field in its expectation rather than a subset.

**`check-crate-arms` reaches a hook member in this repo.** Its couples list carries a `native/src/*.rs`-shaped glob, and a `couples=` pattern matches as POSIX `case` matching does (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The `# graph:` manifest), so `*` matches `/`. Read as a filesystem glob it would look blind, and that misreading would hide a real blindness elsewhere: verify the predicate, never the manifest line alone. Where a trigger does not reach, run the gate explicitly in the landing commit, `bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --only check-crate-arms`. Building the binary runs neither arm.

**The stub-driven lane lives in the member's own module.** It drives a stub reader through every arm, `corrupt` and `unresolved` once over a record and once over an empty dir. It asserts the exit code, the `decision` column and the refusing arms' stderr wording apart from the verdict, with `keys` sorted. It also carries the resolution. The empty knob resolves to the running executable and the gate name, and an override to itself with the run dir appended. An interpreter-led override resolves on `PATH`, and an override lacking the execute bit or naming no program resolves to no reader. The real-reader lane reaches neither `corrupt` nor `error`: the stub lane chooses a reader's exit class, and the real lane proves the resolved reader runs.

**The compiled lane pins `unstarted` rather than leaving it to a race.** Its stub's shebang names an interpreter that does not exist, so the spawn fails with `ENOENT` every time. It asserts `verdict=unstarted`, a `spawn` value that is not `-`, `decision=allow` and exit 0. Every assertion on a firing's exit code prints that firing's log line, so a red names its `verdict` and `spawn` value.

**A reader stub is written by a child process, never by the test process that spawns it.** The module's cases run as threads of one process whose sibling tests fork children. A sibling forking while a stub's write descriptor is open hands its child a copy, which lives until the child's `exec`, since `O_CLOEXEC` closes it there and not at the `fork`, so a stub spawned in that window fails with `ETXTBSY`. A test process that never opens the stub for writing has no descriptor to hand on. **The cause is inferred from measurement, never traced:** the reds were too fast for the reader bound and fell only on freshly written stubs. `spawn` names it at the first residual red. A retry on `ETXTBSY` is refused, since it weakens the assertion in a test and changes a refusing hook's behavior. A lock is refused, since it cannot reach other modules' forks.

### Attribution was weighed and is not available

The payload's `session_id` is shared by a dispatched agent and its dispatcher, and the `pid=<n> run=<key>` grammar carries no writer identity. Adding one is a grammar change across evidence-kit and guard-kit for a narrowing the shared-scratch-dir paragraph argues against wanting.

**`runs` leaves this ruling standing.** The ruling is about **identity**: who wrote a record, whose turn end is refused. `runs` is about **shape**: which records the reading ranged over, a fact about the record set and no session.

**Two firings stay out of the record set's reach.** A launch the harness **moves to the background on its timeout** writes no record, guard-kit rule `background_no_record`'s stated residue. The harness's own `background_tasks` view enumerates what was *launched* rather than what is *running* (§What `background_tasks` carries), so it supplements the record set and cannot substitute for it.

**The session's own firing is its turn end, and it delivers.** A dispatched session that ended its turn while its own `.run` record named a live producer logged `decision=refuse`. The reason reached it as stop-hook feedback, and it resumed work on the producer. A turn end taken while holding a running shell task was refused the same way. When the producer exits, the next firing reads `verdict=green decision=allow`, so the refusal window closes with the producer's life. Firings between a session's steps are a helper's (*A harness helper's firing*, above), and the helper test tells a helper from a session, never one of a session's firings from another. All of this sits under the contract's limit: only a fresh firing catches a harness revision.

### What `background_tasks` carries

The `keys` field settled that the payload carries this top-level key. A one-off read took its contents **out of band**, without touching this hook: the consumer's hook copy dumped five firings' raw payloads to gitignored scratch and was restored byte-for-byte.

**The ruling: the hook logs key names only, and the read strengthened that.** The only reader a value log would have had was substituting the harness view for the record set, and the read falsified it. A derived non-value, a count or a type tally, is refused on the same ground: it serves no reader. Should a reader appear, it reopens as a new question.

**It is a live-children enumeration.** An array of objects in two shapes, both carrying `id`, `type`, `status` and `description`: a `type` of `subagent` adds `agent_type`, a `type` of `shell` adds `command`. It spans more than the emitter: the emitting session, its backgrounded shell task and its dispatched grandchild all appeared with `status` `running`. A child holding no task of its own was refused on a running shell task its dispatcher held. Whether it spans a sibling's tasks is untested. Alongside it the payload carries `transcript_path`, `cwd`, `prompt_id`, `permission_mode`, `agent_id`, `agent_type`, `stop_hook_active`, `agent_transcript_path`, `last_assistant_message` and `session_crons`, beside the three fields the grammar reads. §The turn-end liveness hook's `session` bullet owns what `agent_id` attributes.

**It enumerates what the harness launched, not what is running.** A producer detached from a foreground tool call, live and carrying a `*.run` record the reader reported red, appeared in no firing. That is the unrecorded reading of `live=no` §The probe is asymmetric names, and the class guard-kit rule `background_no_record` refuses at launch. So the view **cannot substitute** for the record set: the two disagree on the class the waiting rule exists for. The hook supplements with it, refusing on a running `shell` element (§The turn-end liveness hook).

**No entry carries a pid.** Those six field names are the whole schema, and a `shell` entry's `id` is the harness's own opaque task id. Joining a record's `pid=<n>` would mean parsing `command`, the value the ruling withholds. Ownership is read off the emitting session's transcript instead, which records each launched task's id, so the join needs no value beyond `id`.

**The privacy ground is wider than first written.** `command` is a verbatim shell command line and `description` is free-text prose. Logging them would put command lines and task prose into a capture-tier file in whatever tree vendors this kit, beyond the transcript paths and prompt ids the ruling first named.

### The probe is asymmetric, and no reading may treat it otherwise

**A `live=yes` line proves the harness does not defer the stop.** One firing with a live producer settles the unknown in that direction.

**No accumulation of `live=no` lines proves that it does.** `live=no` is equally consistent with *the harness deferred the stop*, *the session waited correctly*, and *the session recorded nothing*. The last is refused at an explicit launch by guard-kit rule `background_no_record` and still reachable through a call the harness moves to the background on its timeout. Passive accumulation cannot return the finding that would dissolve the class.

**So the reading is bought by a deliberate firing, never by a wait.** A dispatched session backgrounds a long producer, writes its `<key>.run` record, and ends its turn. If the hook fires at once with `live=yes`, the harness does not defer, and §Operative residency's axis holds at the turn end. If it fires only when the child exits, the harness defers, the class dissolves, and this hook's value is the evidence for retiring it.

**The firing returned the first branch.** A dispatched agent backgrounded a producer with a recorded PID and ended its turn without waiting. The log took `live=yes verdict=red records=1` four seconds later, and again at the agent's own stop, while the producer wrote on for two and a half minutes. **The harness does not defer the stop**, so the class stands, the relocation to guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` stands, and the hook blocks (§The turn-end liveness hook).

**The same firings raised whether `SubagentStop` is the session-end event.** It fired seventeen times inside one dispatched session that had ended no turn, spaced by assistant steps rather than by the clock. Those firings were a harness helper's, and a helper's firing allows (§The turn-end liveness hook, *A harness helper's firing*). The session's own event is its turn end.

## Resume journal — agent writes, scratch reset sweeps

**The contract, in four clauses.** An agent whose run an interruption could cost, mutating or read-only, journals to a repo-local gitignored scratch directory in the main checkout. An isolated agent writes there by a shell append, since the harness refuses its file-tool writes there. The supervisor grants that path **absolute** in the dispatch prompt, each finding lands inline as it is confirmed, and a `DONE` marker is appended on success. The operative text, with why a temporary worktree and a system temp dir both fail the survivability test, is `templates/agent-execution.md`'s **Resume journal — agent writes, scratch reset sweeps** bullet. This section owns the lifetime rule's reasoning and the two readings the marker carries.

**A stage session takes no grant.** Where the consumer runs a stage machine, the journal's path is a **derivation from the stage** that the machine computes. Its entry tool opens the journal and reports the path to the entering session, and its journal arm appends there for a session that spells no path (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §The state machine, §The journal arm). That report is the path's one source. A grant restated in the prompt is a second source the session prefers, so its journal lands under a name the next entry cannot find. The derivation's **artifact on disk** replaces the grant as the record of what the session owes; every other mutating child is still granted its path. This kit owns the journal contract and states no path convention, and that kit owns the path and the arm that resolves it and states no contract.

**The file may pre-exist the agent.** Under such a machine the shape is *the machine opens, the agent writes, the reset sweeps*: a journal at its derived path may carry only an opening line naming the session that owes it. The four clauses are unchanged, and the agent appends its findings and its final `DONE` under whatever the machine left. A journal's presence already signals nothing (below); the predicate that reads past the opening line is the consumer's own (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §bin/enter-stage.sh).

**Lifetime — retention, not an eager deletion chore.** Cleanup is the consumer's own scratch reset at its next work-unit boundary (here, `--enter-stage`'s wipe of `.tmp/` at the next scope entry, lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §bin/enter-stage.sh). A supervisor deleting on its own schedule breaks two things once an agent is **resumable**. The resumed agent is still writing to the file, and a supervisor reading the journal as its **pull channel** (lifecycle-kit/templates/lead.md §Channel design) destroys that channel at the first verified commit. A one-shot sweep hides both. The costlier harm is the false signal: a resumed session that finds its journal gone mid-write reports a scratch-directory reliability defect against an unrelated unit.

**Reading `DONE` under retention.** Every journal survives its session, so finding one signals nothing by itself. `DONE` counts **only as the file's last line**, since a session resumed past its own marker appends after it. A missing marker means interruption only where the supervisor never consumed the agent's return. On the ordinary path that return plus its post-commit verification (§Verify after every agent commit) is the recovery contract, and the marker is redundant. In a **cold read**, a journal whose session died before returning (a sandbox died, a crash, a timeout), the marker is the sole signal: no `DONE` means interrupted, so resume from it. The consumer's work cursor and `git log` tell a live journal from a spent one, and the boundary sweep bounds the ambiguity to the current work unit. The inline-findings rule insures the cold read, since a pointer-only journal would make a `DONE` lie about recoverability.

**A shared journal answers the same question.** Per-session naming discriminates only where the dispatcher chooses the path. Under a stage machine the journal is named for the stage, so several sessions of one stage append to one file. The last-line rule makes it answer *did the session that wrote last finish*, which is the question, and an earlier session's mid-file marker is no claim the later content contradicts.

**Caveat — a journal write once failed, cause unexplained.** A background agent could not `Write` to its granted path and silently returned its findings in its final message instead, defeating the journal on the long, interruptible run it exists for. The template's explicit-grant clause follows from this, and so does its rule that a read-only fan-out's return value stays the contract with the journal beside it.

**The caveat is no property of isolation.** A worktree-isolated agent reaches an absolute main-checkout path by a shell write, though the harness refuses its file-tool write (measured, §The delegation model). So an entry-time assertion on a journal's existence is fair: a missing journal is a session that did **not** write, not one that **could not**. One observation of capability does not falsify one of failure, so the remedy stands. Its content is *do not make recoverability depend on a backgrounded child's write*. The template's **Findings you will act on are durable before you act on them** rule binds the other end, **the parent**, which has shown it can write; the two are disjoint readings of one contract.

**Journalling is a discharge path, keyed on capability, never role.** A session journals when it cannot commit and commits when it can. A role-keyed reading granted the journal to dispatched sessions alone, on the premise that a top-level session can always commit. That premise fails whenever another session holds the shared index, the normal condition while a work unit is in flight. Improvised scratch then carries none of the contract (no path inside the sweep's reach, no inline landing, no marker), so the loss is silent. Every discharge path lands in the scratch directory the boundary already sweeps, so the widening adds no lifetime, keep-list entry or triage surface.

**The provenance floor.** A dispatching session can narrate a finding from a subagent output it never received, and nothing reds. The operative text is the template's **A return you did not receive is not a finding, and an identifier you did not read is not a citation** bullet. This section holds why one of its halves gets a rule instead of a gate.

**No gate reaches the relayed-return half, by proof.** The tree cannot observe what a session received. A return lives in the parent's context and leaves no artifact, so tree state is byte-identical whether the return was held or invented. Arrival can be unobservable to the parent as well: a child that could not reach its dispatcher by name delivered to the top-level session instead. A gate would have to assert a fact about a conversation, which no repository scanner reaches. **The minted-identifier half leaves a token**, so it is gated one record at a time (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §check-survey-record).

**The mitigation is the journal, a bound and never a fix.** The journal lands a child's output on **disk**, so a parent's claim is checkable wherever the child wrote one. That narrows the class's reach without closing it, since the child's write can fail silently (the caveat above).

## Verify after every agent commit
Re-run the relevant gates plus the consumer's validate battery: for this repo and any gate-sdk consumer, `bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh` and the kit fixture runners. A toolchain consumer adds its compile, lint and test set in the template's marked section, with a zero-byte-file sweep after renames, since silent edit corruption is real. **Diff every gate change in an agent commit before accepting it.** An agent blocked by a gate will weaken it with a false exemption rather than fix the code. "The gate is in my way" almost always means the code does not fit the convention.

**The act is `verify`; the artifact stays the `validate battery`.** A supervisor performs the act, and the battery is the command set the act runs. One word for both made completing the act read as completing whatever else the consumer names with that word, a stage among them. The battery's name is a binding slot every consumer's shim carries (§One template, a resident pointer, read by `check-skill-binding`), and the act is named only in prose, so the act takes the distinct name.

**A verify step never runs the producer of what it is verifying.** Re-running is safe while the unit's output is *work* and the check's output is *a verdict about that work*. Where the deliverable **is** an evidence artifact, the verify is a **read of the committed artifact**, never a re-execution of its writer, which mutates or duplicates the very record the check exists to confirm.

**The two wrong re-runs differ in harm.** A check that writes nothing the artifact depends on is *inert* on it: misrouted, wasted, and nothing lost. Re-running the artifact's **sole writer** is *destructive* and leaves evidence to restore. A supervisor that already ran the wrong one reads this split to learn which it holds.

**Honest limit — this rule installs no oracle.** The misrouting recurred, and an operator caught it each time. Catching it needs a gate over a *supervisor's choice of command*, which leaves no tracked artifact to read, the reason §Operative residency owes no gate. A consumer whose evidence producer claims a liveness lock has a partial artifact-side proxy, whose boundary that consumer states. Detection stays human, and the limit is stated so no later session reads it as an oversight to close with a gate that cannot exist.

`check-gate-tamper` is the mechanical floor under the diff-every-gate-change duty. Two assertions block the two tamper shapes:

- **A — gate-edit isolation.** A commit that touches a gate file may touch only meta-layer paths: `DELEGATION_KIT_META_PATHS` prefixes, root `*.md`, and the install manifest `checkwright.lock` at the toplevel. Co-staging product code with a gate edit is blocked; split the gate change into its own commit. The manifest is the installer's record of the vendored tree, so every `update` and profile move co-stages it with the gate files it rewrites, and counting it as product code would refuse that commit under the hooks it vendors. Where the gate's spec pointer names a section outside the meta layer, the order is forced: the section lands first, green with the gate absent, and the gate second. A gate file is a path a `DELEGATION_KIT_GATE_FILES` glob matches, **or** a registered member's resolved declaration, **or** the gate-sdk library. The reader unions the last two in, each spelled against the toplevel, so no consumer value can leave a registered gate or the library uncovered.
- **B — no self-serving exemption.** A newly added path or glob entry in any gate's `# exception-list:` array must not match a file staged in the same commit: an exemption never excuses the change it lands with. Its report lines are byte-sorted, a set rather than an incidental order.

**Honest limit:** the gate blocks by commit *shape*, so it misses semantic weakening inside a legitimate scripts-only commit, and the by-eye diff review of agent gate edits stays a supervisor duty.

`checks/check-gate-tamper.gate` (`precommit`, binary-dispatched). Live mode reads `git diff --cached`. `--fixture <dir>` injects `staged-files` and `added-exemptions` lists for the fixture pair, and a trailing `--fixture` with no directory is refused. The flag stays on the compiled gate: it selects the rule's own input corpus, which no knob resolves, so it is not the arrives-too-late shape gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm deletes.

The union is built in both modes. A registered member resolves through the battery's own order: the gates dir first, then each kit root's `checks/`, `.sh` before `.gate` inside a dir. A member resolving nowhere adds nothing, since resolving it is another gate's job. An absent `gates.list` adds nothing, and one that exists but cannot be read is exit 2. The library is `<GATE_SDK_ROOT>/lib/gate.sh`, dropped when the root lies outside the toplevel, where no staged path can name it. Each element is a literal, so matching it is equality, and the union is additive, never filtering a declared glob. The clean line reports the union's size (`<m> registered gate file(s) covered`), and assertion A's finding names the declared globs "plus every registered gate and the gate library".

**Criterion 4 binds on this gate on every tree.** The union puts every registered kit declaration among the gate files, `check-gate-tamper.gate` included. Staging the gate's own declaration makes it read its own bytes whatever the consumer's globs say, so no configuration clears it (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The port-candidate criteria carries the class).

## The tier binding

A consumer binds each **tier class** to a model once, and every place that chooses a tier reads the binding. The classes are kit vocabulary, highest first: **judgment** (design decisions), **routing** (a split-posture lead's own turns, lifecycle-kit/templates/lead.md §The lead model) and **mechanical** (oracle-running, read-heavy audits, rename and merge sweeps). A spec stage's `design-bearing` work class maps to judgment and its `mechanical` class to mechanical. The order matters only to a floor check, which reads "at or above". `DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` holds the binding, one `<class>=<model>` element per class, read by `--emit knob-values DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL`. An unbound class is one no element names. An empty binding is off: nothing below fires, and tiers stay each dispatcher's to name. The mechanical class may also run on another vendor's agent through a configured adapter (§The foreign-vendor run).

**Why a binding.** Without one, the tier a dispatch rides is written as a harness model alias wherever it is chosen — a definition's `model:`, a lead's per-stage overrides, a batch's `model` parameter — and the class-to-model mapping lives in each dispatcher's head. An alias resolves to whatever model the harness currently names by it, so a roster move changes every dispatch's price and behaviour with no edit anywhere, and no setting chooses between following and pinning.

**A value is an alias or an exact model id, and the choice is the consumer's.** An alias follows: the harness resolves it to the newest model it names by it. A model upgrade then arrives with no edit, and so do its price and its behaviour. An exact id pins: it stays until the consumer changes it. The kit ships no value, because the harness roster churns and the values are consumer config (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The provenance seam).

**Matching.** A running model **matches** a value when the value equals its id or equals one of the id's `-`-separated tokens after the first. So an alias matches every id in its family and an exact id matches only itself. The kit holds no alias list, because the harness's ids already carry their family as a token. The leading token is skipped because it is the namespace every id shares: a value equal to it would match every model and hold no tier. The table validator sees the binding and no id, so it cannot tell a namespace spelling from an alias. The skip therefore makes such a value match nothing, and every reader reports it (D6 blocks, `--model-verdict` reads `UNBOUND`) rather than matching every id silently. The validator refuses the two spellings it can see: an all-digit value, a version token that crosses families, and a value equal to the leading token of a different bound value. One value bound to two classes is not that second spelling, and passes. **Honest limit:** a harness whose ids lead with their family reaches it by exact id only.

**The binding is read at three places.** A definition's `model:` is generated from the class its `tier:` field declares (§check-agent-tier-explicit). A dispatch naming a model is held to a bound value (§The delegation model, D6). A session reads the class of the model it runs on (§model-verdict).

**Honest limit on pinning.** This harness takes a per-dispatch `model` as an alias only: the dispatch tool's parameter is an enum of aliases. So a pinned class cannot ride a per-dispatch override, and a consumer pinning a class it reaches by override dispatches it as a type whose definition carries the pin instead. The definition's `model:` does take an exact id. Measured: a definition pinned to one family's exact id, dispatched from a session running another, ran on the pinned id, which its transcript's `message.model` carried on every record. A harness loads its definitions at session start, so a pin reaches only sessions started after it lands. Pinning an alias's own resolution is harness configuration (its default-model environment settings), outside this kit, on the reasoning §The delegation model applies to `worktree.baseRef`.

**Following is a strength with a stated cost.** An upgrade under an alias changes price and behaviour silently. What surfaces it is a new id in the transcripts, where every assistant record carries the model its turn ran on (`message.model`): drift-kit's `--price-coverage` names an id its price table cannot price (drift-kit/SPEC.md §The price-coverage arm), and the lead reads it before its first dispatch. Under a pin, a new id in a pinned class's transcripts means the pin was bypassed, and `--model-verdict` reports it as a mismatch.

**Two shapes were weighed and refused.** *Three scalar knobs, one per class*: a scalar takes an environment override under its own name (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The knob file). The binding feeds a freshness gate (§check-agent-tier-explicit), so a session exporting an override would red that gate against a tree nothing changed; an indexed knob takes none. *Keeping `model:` hand-written and checking only that its value is some bound value*: two classes bound to one alias make that check blind, and rebinding one of them then leaves every definition that meant the rebound class silently wrong. A definition has to say which class it is.

## check-agent-tier-explicit

Every agent definition under `DELEGATION_KIT_AGENT_DIR` declares a `model:` field in its frontmatter (assertion A), and, where the tier binding is set, its `model:` is the bound value of the class its `tier:` field declares (assertion B). The gate is the oracle over the tracked half of the template's **Match the dispatched model and effort to the unit's shape** rule: the per-dispatch habit leaves no artifact, but a standing choice does.

**Assertion A polices silence, not the choice.** An explicit `inherit` passes, since a type that should ride its dispatcher's tier is a legitimate answer and the gate has no business overruling it. What reds is omission, the one state that looks like a neutral absence and is not one, because an omitted `model:` falls through to the dispatcher's tier — the refused environment override and the precedence chain behind that reading are in §The delegation model. A reader arriving at this gate expecting it to enforce cheapness has the wrong model of it.

**Assertion B holds a generated copy.** The harness reads `model:` and cannot read the binding, so the field is a projection of the binding through the definition's class. `bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --emit agent-tiers [--write]` generates it: for each definition whose first frontmatter block carries `tier: <class>`, the expected `model:` is that class's bound value. Bare, the arm prints one line per tiered definition, `<path>: model: <current> -> <expected>` or `<path>: current`, and exits 0. `--write` rewrites the `model:` line inside the first frontmatter block, or inserts one after `tier:` where none exists, and touches no other byte. It writes nothing until every definition has resolved, so a definition naming an unbound class leaves the tree untouched and exits 2, naming it. A definition without `tier:` is left as it is, and so is every definition when the binding is empty, where the arm prints `binding off`.

The arm is an `--emit-` member, since its exit grammar is the collapse; it writes only inside the tree and spawns nothing, and its declared knobs are `DELEGATION_KIT_AGENT_DIR`, `DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` and the walk's two prune knobs. The harness ignores a frontmatter key it does not recognize, so `tier:` costs a definition nothing; it is kit vocabulary in a harness file, the footing `name:` and `model:` already have as the fields the kit reads.

B reds on a non-`inherit` `model:` with no `tier:`, on an unbound class, and on a stale model line, each finding pointing at `--emit agent-tiers --write`. It calls the arm's rewrite in process and compares whole-file bytes, so the arm and the gate cannot disagree about what `--write` produces. A definition stating `model: inherit` with no `tier:` passes B, on A's ground. B is off while the binding is empty, and the clean line names the state: `binding off`, or the count of definitions holding their class's bound model. The descriptor declares the definitions its projection, and its knob file reaches the trigger through the derived couples (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The `# graph:` manifest), so a binding edit re-runs it at commit.

**Counted inertness.** A consumer with no such directory, or one holding no definitions, scans zero and reports a clean counted line, the derived-scan-set shape the kit's other gates use. The scan set is the directory's contents.

**Honest limit.** It holds the tracked surface only. A dispatch naming no agent type and no `model` parameter inherits and leaves no artifact for any gate to read, which D5 reaches at the dispatch where it is switched on (§The delegation model).

## usage-verdict

Emits a trustworthy budget verdict from a usage snapshot file, closing the three failure modes a raw percentage reading leaves open:

1. **Stale reading** — `now - updated_at` beyond `DELEGATION_KIT_STALE_AGE` → STALE (exit 2): budget-unknown, never blocking delegation — re-read or refresh before trusting the number.
2. **Dead window** — `resets_at <= now` → RESET-OK (exit 0): the percentage is from the dead window and must not be read as a pause signal. The weekly axis carries the same rule per-axis (`seven_day_resets_at <= now` disarms the weekly pause without forcing the whole verdict to RESET-OK — the axes are judged independently).
3. **Post-login lag** — a fresh login starts a new window but the server-fed percentage lags it, and the file-write age check cannot see that. The verdict compares the snapshot's `account` with the **live identity**, the account config's `oauthAccount.accountUuid`. A snapshot naming a different account forces the demand-driven refresh past its short-circuit before the read, and one still naming it after that refresh routes a would-be OK to `STALE (snapshot predates an account switch; …)`. The same account trusts a snapshot taken at least `DELEGATION_KIT_LOGIN_SETTLE` seconds after the credentials file's mtime, the auth event. Where either identity is missing, the verdict falls back to the blanket reroute: within `DELEGATION_KIT_LOGIN_WINDOW` of the mtime, a would-be OK routes to STALE. Letting the lag print OK would emit a fresh-looking chimera, the new account's id beside the dead login's percentage and `resets_at`. The two time-keyed arms are self-limiting, since once the settle floor or the window passes they stop firing; the account-switch STALE holds until a producer writes the live account's snapshot.

   **The reroute is asymmetric: it may suppress an OK, never a PAUSE**, so it follows the axis compares rather than preceding them. The two directions of a lagging reading have opposite costs. A lagging *low* percentage printing OK invites a dispatch the budget may not afford, which the reroute exists to prevent. A lagging *high* percentage suppressed into STALE waves that dispatch through, because STALE never blocks. So the reroute fires on the non-blocking outcome only: on every arm, account-switch, settle floor and blanket window alike, an at-or-over reading still exits 1 on whichever axis fired, and an under-threshold one still exits 2. An account switch swaps both windows, so neither axis escapes the suppression when the outcome is a would-be OK.

   **The mtime is a proxy, and the roll witnesses refute it.** A credentials file's mtime cannot tell a login from a routine OAuth token rotation, which rewrites the same file. So every rotation mints a fake auth event and blinds the verdict for the window's duration, and rotations cluster around long-session resume, exactly when a verdict is asked for. The reroute's *premise* is that a low percentage inside the window is a **lagging** reading. Once the five-hour window has demonstrably rolled, a low percentage is the **expected** reading and the premise is false. The verdict consults two witnesses of a roll, conjunctively, since either alone is ambiguous:

   - the snapshot's `five_hour_resets_at` differs from the `resets_at` of the newest `DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_HISTORY` sample for the snapshot's account (the boundary moved); a snapshot carrying no `account` reads the newest sample of all;
   - the snapshot's `updated_at` is past that **previous** boundary (time actually crossed it, rather than the boundary being restamped by an account switch).

   Both firing disarms the two time-keyed arms, the settle floor and the blanket window, and the reading verdicts on its own merits. They never disarm the account-switch STALE: its premise is a reading that speaks for another account's windows, not a lagging one, and a roll of that account's window says nothing of the live account's. The verdict path is therefore a **reader** of the trend log it appends to, and it reads before this run's own append, so the witness is always the previous reading. It falls open to the unrefuted reroute whenever the log cannot answer: knob unset, file absent or unreadable, no sample for the snapshot's account, or a newest sample carrying no numeric `resets_at`. A first reading after a swap therefore falls open rather than comparing against another account's boundary.

   **Honest limit:** where the snapshot carries no `account` or the account config is unreadable, a rotation with no roll still blinds the blanket window, because no witness contradicts it. A swap that changes neither the credentials file nor the account config is invisible to every arm. A producer stamps `account` from the account config at its read, not from the token behind its percentage. So a swap that rewrites the account config and leaves that token in place labels the old token's reading with the new account, and the comparison passes it.

**The rule is a compiled arm, `bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --usage-verdict`, an `Arm::Run` row that cannot be an `--emit-` member.** The emitting family maps every success to 0 and every failure to 2, and this rule's **1** is the whole blocking signal the budget guard grades. So it takes its own flag spelling and front-end case arm, as `--upgrade-smoke` does (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm). It is **not** a harness-integration arm either: its callers are a session brief, a kit smoke and a gate reaching it in process, the caller set that subsection's definition excludes. With no binary on the host the front-end exits **2**, this rule's budget-unknown code, so an artifact-less host reads as STALE rather than as a verdict it never took.

**Twelve declared knobs, each a row of delegation-kit's static table**: `DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_FILE`, `_CRED_FILE`, `_ACCOUNT_CONFIG`, `_PAUSE_PCT`, `_PAUSE_PCT_7D`, `_STALE_AGE`, `_LOGIN_WINDOW`, `_LOGIN_SETTLE`, `_REFRESH_CMD`, `_REFRESH_MIN_AGE`, `_USAGE_HISTORY` and `_FAN_WIDTH` (§Layout and configuration). The three paths are read through the reader's one empty-means-derive fill, which reads all three together, and the verdict opens `_ACCOUNT_CONFIG` for the live identity. A read of a name the table does not carry is an error rather than an empty value, so the roster and the table are one change and never two.

**Two positionals and one refusal.** `[usage-file [credentials-file]]` arrive as the arm's own argv and override `DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_FILE` and `DELEGATION_KIT_CRED_FILE`. They are arguments the *rule* consumes rather than redirections of resolved config (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm's distinguishing test). A positional beginning with `-` that names no option is a **refusal**, usage on stderr and exit 2; `--` ends option processing; `-h`/`--help` belongs to the front-end (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The bin/-tool contract). The refusal is not decoration: an absorbed flag would report `cannot read --help … -> STALE` at exit 2, which the budget guard routes to **advise**, a non-reading dressed as a reading at the decision point the rule exists to hold.

**One rule, two callers.** `verdict(args) -> (String, i32)` returns the line and the status; the arm prints the line and returns the status, and `agent-budget-guard` calls that same function in process, grading `code == 1` as its block and relaying the `String` verbatim (§The delegation model). A shape refusal returns an **empty** line with its usage already on stderr, so the arm prints nothing on stdout and the status carries the whole signal. The one program the compiled rule spawns is `DELEGATION_KIT_REFRESH_CMD`'s argv, spawned directly with no shell, and only when that knob is non-empty, since it is a command seam.

Check order: parse → RESET-OK → age-STALE → pause axes → account-STALE or login-STALE → OK. The RESET-OK branch is the same decision one step earlier and is ordering precedent for the witnesses: the file already accepts that a demonstrated roll outranks the reroute. It covers only a snapshot from an already-dead window; the witnesses cover a window that rolled *between* samples, where the re-polled snapshot carries a live `resets_at`.

Exit codes: **0** OK / RESET-OK, **1** PAUSE, **2** STALE or unreadable. Exit 2 is fail-closed on *trusting the number* and deliberately fail-open on *refusing work*: budget-unknown never blocks delegation, so a reading that should refuse an expensive dispatch never lands on it. Fail-closed throughout:

- missing keys and a non-numeric percentage route to STALE;
- each threshold compare is a float compare, so a fractional percentage cannot silently skip PAUSE;
- both pause compares are **at-or-over** (`>=`), so a reading exactly at `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT` or `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT_7D` pauses.

**Demand-driven refresh.** The decision point triggers the poll: when `DELEGATION_KIT_REFRESH_CMD` is non-empty, `usage-verdict` spawns its argv before reading the snapshot, so every verdict caller reads live data rather than whatever the last statusline render left. That closes the push producer's blind spot at the point of use, since a lead that delegates stops rendering exactly when it goes static. Empty, the default, keeps the read-only behavior.

The refresh is **short-circuited** by `DELEGATION_KIT_REFRESH_MIN_AGE`: the command runs only when the snapshot is missing, unreadable, or at least that old by its `updated_at`. A snapshot whose `account` differs from the live identity is re-polled whatever its age, since its reading speaks for another account's windows. The verdict's callers fire in bursts rather than on a schedule, once per `Agent` call from the dispatch guard and once per session from the session-start brief. Without the floor a configured refresh would hammer the source across a fan-out, and with it a stale-enough snapshot still polls at dispatch time.

It is **fail-soft**: a non-zero refresh exit leaves the snapshot untouched and the verdict proceeds on the cached file, whose staleness the checks above judge, so a dead producer reads STALE rather than a silent green. Refresh diagnostics are suppressed rather than mixed into the verdict line callers relay verbatim; the snapshot's age is the signal. `usage.txt` stays the last-known-good cache, the source-agnostic seam and the test seam. A refresh soon after an auth event rewrites `updated_at` while the server-fed percentage may still lag the login by about a minute. So the reroute keeps those readings STALE for the settle floor, or for the blanket window where the identity is unreadable, save the at-or-over ones it may not suppress.

**The verdict-string contract.** Every line `usage-verdict` emits carries three parts: the **reading** (the measured fields), the **epistemic status** of that reading (how far to trust the number), and its **decision consequence** for delegation (what the reading gates). A verdict string stating a status without its consequence is incomplete by contract — the reader completes it by inference, and the inference is unconstrained: a session reading a bare STALE infers "do not trust the number" into "do not dispatch". That read is wrong (STALE never blocks) and is structurally uncorrectable downstream, because the budget guard fires *on* dispatch, the act a hesitating session has not yet performed. STALE's status half is site-specific — each emission site names why it is stale — while its consequence half is uniform, because the consequence is uniform: STALE is budget-unknown at every site and never blocks delegation. The consequence clause appends *after* the `` -> <verdict> `` arrow, leaving it disjoint from the `width=<n>` invariant below.

**The `width=<n>` field.** Every verdict line, the fail-closed STALE diagnostics included, carries `width=<n>` immediately before the `` -> <verdict> `` arrow, read from `DELEGATION_KIT_FAN_WIDTH` (§Layout and configuration). It is the knob's mechanical reader, and it reports the bound without enforcing it (§The delegation model). The budget check already runs before every dispatch, and `agent-budget-guard` relays the line verbatim, so the read-only fan-out bound reaches context at the wave-sizing decision. *Verbatim* governs the line's own text, not its frame: the guard prefixes the relayed line and the PAUSE arm appends its corrective, but no arm restates the payload, and the one advise arm adds nothing. The field is a config constant, not derived from the snapshot, so it is present even on an unreadable-snapshot STALE.

**Two pause axes.** The five-hour window is the always-on axis. The weekly (7-day) window is a second, armed only when both `seven_day_used_pct` and `seven_day_resets_at` are present, so a three-line snapshot is judged on the 5h axis alone. The weekly limit can deplete while the 5h window sits comfortable, and a weekly PAUSE costs days rather than hours. So it gates delegation, the discretionary spend and the first to stop near the weekly ceiling, keeping the rest of the week with the supervisor. The axes are judged independently against `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT` and `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT_7D`, and either firing is a PAUSE (exit 1). The message names the axis that fired, `PAUSE (7-day window)` or `PAUSE (5h window)`, the weekly when both fire, because the remediation differs by days.

**Usage-history sampling.** When `DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_HISTORY` is non-empty, `usage-verdict` appends one sample line (§The usage.txt contract) to that log after every parsed snapshot, whatever the verdict. The line carries the raw harness-reported values. Write-time smoothing or correction is forbidden, since a later corrective push is evidence about the earlier sample and only the reader has both. A STALE from an unreadable or unparseable snapshot appends nothing: a sample the verdict would not trust is not history. `--emit-usage-trend` (§Trend reporter) reads the log, and so does the verdict, for a roll witness. That read is strictly fall-open, so the log stays an advisory trend surface rather than a correctness dependency, and sampling off changes no verdict.

### The usage.txt contract

The snapshot is the wire contract between any producer and the gate — three `key=value` lines:

```
five_hour_used_pct=<float>
five_hour_resets_at=<epoch-seconds>
updated_at=<epoch-seconds>
```

Beyond the three mandatory lines a producer may write optional keys its source exposes. `usage-verdict` reads the ones it interprets and passes the rest through:

- `seven_day_used_pct` / `seven_day_resets_at`, the weekly window, which arms the second pause axis;
- `account`, the logged-in account identity: `login_at` detects a switch and `account` says to whom, so the trend reporter groups a multi-account operator's segments per account and the verdict compares it with the live identity (§usage-verdict);
- `tier`, the subscription tier, the denominator behind the percentages;
- `tokens_in` / `tokens_out`, cumulative token counts, the axis that binds an API-billed consumer, to whom no subscription percentage applies.

An optional key is omitted when its source has no value, never written empty.

**Omit-don't-empty binds the reader as much as the producer.** A reader of these lines tests a key's **presence**, defaults an absent one rather than failing on it, and never treats an omitted optional as a malformed line (§Trend reporter enumerates its reader's defaults). A reader demanding every key would red on a line this contract permits.

The kit ships two reference producers, one push and one poll, and any producer honoring the contract works beside them. The `--statusline` arm is the push producer: a statusline hook that parses the harness's rate-limit JSON and atomically writes the snapshot (`tmp` + `mv`) to `<config-home>/usage.txt`. It also produces the weekly pair from the payload and `account` / `tier` from the local account config (`.credentials.json` / `~/.claude.json`, overridable via `DELEGATION_KIT_CRED_FILE` / `DELEGATION_KIT_ACCOUNT_CONFIG`). It ships no `tokens_in` / `tokens_out` producer: this harness's payload carries no cumulative token count, so under the dead-producer rule the keys stay defined here for third-party producers but no dead producer is shipped. Beyond the snapshot write it renders a status bar (§The statusline arm). The snapshot write is the contract; the bar is reference UX a consumer may restyle or discard. The source is pluggable (`DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_FILE`), so no single-operator `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` assumption is baked in.

The `--usage-poll` arm is the poll producer, closing the push producer's blind spot. The statusline fires on the supervising session's own message flow, so a lead that delegates stops producing exactly when it goes static, and a delegated build can run for hours with every budget verdict reading a stale file. The poller runs one poll cycle per invocation:

1. read the harness OAuth token from `DELEGATION_KIT_CRED_FILE`; the token goes into the request header and nowhere else, never logged, echoed or written to the snapshot;
2. query the account usage source;
3. map the payload onto the contract, the three mandatory lines plus whichever optional keys the source exposes, with **no new key**;
4. atomically rewrite the snapshot (`tmp` + `mv`).

No daemon, no loop: scheduling belongs to the consumer, and two modes are sanctioned. **Demand-driven** (§usage-verdict): point `DELEGATION_KIT_REFRESH_CMD` at the poller, and it runs at verdict time with the freshest reading at the decision point. **Timer-driven**: a cron line such as `*/5 * * * * bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --usage-poll`, or a systemd timer, for continuous trend density independent of verdict calls. Either entry is the enabling config; with neither, the producer is dead.

The usage endpoint is harness-account plumbing, not a published contract, so the poller is **fail-soft**. A missing or unreadable credentials file, a fetch failure or an unparseable payload exits non-zero with a `help:` line *without touching the snapshot*. A stale snapshot is already detected downstream, where `usage-verdict`'s staleness check turns it into STALE rather than a silent green, and the non-zero exit lands in the invoking timer's logging and nowhere else. The endpoint URL is the `DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_ENDPOINT` knob, both the test seam (the smoke points it at a local `file://` stub) and the valve when the source moves.

Both producers write the whole snapshot atomically from the same account source, and last-writer-wins is correct: the freshest write is the truest, and `updated_at` arbitrates downstream. Nothing serializes them and nothing needs to. The poller writes the snapshot only, and `usage-verdict` stays the trend log's single append author.

**The shipped statusline arm calls `usage-verdict` nowhere**, and its declared reads omit `DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_HISTORY` for that reason (§The statusline arm). So the sampling that knob buys is whatever the verdict's own per-session and per-dispatch callers produce. The statusline fires far more often, so a consumer wanting denser history calls `usage-verdict` from its own render path rather than appending the log itself. The kit ships the knob and the append author, not the render-path call.

**The sample line.** With sampling enabled (§usage-verdict), `usage-verdict` appends one line per parsed snapshot, the trend log's wire contract with `--emit-usage-trend`:

```
updated_at=<epoch> pct=<float> resets_at=<epoch> verdict=<word> login_at=<epoch>[ account=<word>][ tier=<word>][ pct_7d=<float> resets_7d=<epoch>][ tokens_in=<n> tokens_out=<n>]
```

Space-separated `key=value`, order-insensitive; optional groups are omitted (never written empty) when the snapshot lacks them. `login_at` is the credentials-file mtime the post-login-lag check already reads, stamped per sample so an account switch becomes data; `pct` / `resets_at` are the 5h values verbatim and `pct_7d` / `resets_7d` the weekly ones. The log is append-only (the tmp-prune / boundary-truncate conventions own cleanup), and carries operator-local account identifiers, so it lives under the gitignored measurement dir and never reaches a tracked file.

### The statusline arm

The `--statusline` arm renders model/effort, a context gauge, the 5h and 7d windows with reset countdowns, an `iteration@stage` readout, a queue counter group, and any configured inbox counters. Self-contained ANSI, no external asset (§Out of scope).

The `iteration@stage` readout's two halves come from two surfaces: the iteration from the queue header, the stage from the lifecycle evidence file's **last data line**. Both are repo-root-relative reads in the arm's existing hardcode style — the arm adds no knob. With no cursor to read (no evidence file, or one truncated to its preamble at an iteration boundary) the readout degrades to the iteration alone rather than printing a dangling separator or a partial parse.

**The counter group** is a compact `N12 T3 D48 I7` tally of the queue's task sections, appended last. The arm does not know how many sections exist, what they are called, or which tiers the set contains. It calls queue-kit's counter rendering and renders whatever `<section-name><TAB><count>` lines come back, labelling each with the initial of its name (queue-kit/SPEC.md §The queue-counts arm), so the section vocabulary stays inside the kit that owns it. As soon as two returned names share an initial, **every** label widens to two characters, so a colliding pair stays distinguishable and the group stays aligned rather than only the collision changing width.

**The inbox counters** are a second group, appended after the queue counter group, one `<label><n>` token per `DELEGATION_KIT_STATUSLINE_INBOXES` element whose file holds at least one bullet. The count is the file's lines opening `- `, the one grammar both lifecycle-kit inboxes share. A zero count, an absent file and an unreadable one render nothing, and an empty knob renders no group: a zero on every bar is noise, and the session brief says which inbox is empty. The kit names no path, since delegation-kit may not name a lifecycle-kit one: which inboxes a consumer counts, and under which label, is that consumer's. The knob carries labels beside paths so one knob serves every inbox, and it defaults empty because the counter is optional, unlike the `iteration@stage` readout every bar shows.

**The counter rendering is called in process.** It resolves its knobs through the crate's knob reads, which return `Result` and cannot exit. A malformed queue config becomes an `Err` this arm maps to an empty counter group, and the model, context and rate-limit gauges still render.

An unresolvable queue file, a malformed config and empty output each drop the group and change nothing else about the bar, beside the arm's two other degradations: an absent queue file drops the iteration, and an absent evidence file drops the `@stage` suffix. There is no existence guard: the rendering ships in the same binary, so a profile that vendored this kit without queue-kit has no missing script to guard against.

**The arm's declared reads include the counter rendering's four** (`QUEUE_KIT_QUEUE_FILE` and the three section knobs), since an in-process reader resolves them itself. queue-kit/SPEC.md §The queue-index arm's configured-arm paragraph settles a caller reaching an arm *as* an arm, and an in-process call from a hook module is neither the front end nor a direct binary invoke, so where the four resolve is stated here:

- **They resolve in process from queue-kit's static table.** The four are static knobs, so the binary reads them from the kit's defaults and the consumer's `queue-config.knobs` wherever it runs (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The knob file). The gate-sdk queue file that `QUEUE_KIT_QUEUE_FILE`'s default derives from resolves from gate-sdk's own table the same way. Declaring the four is what lets the knob-file derivation, `check-reads-couples` and `check-gate-substrate-parity` see those reads.
- **The silent-override-ignored failure is structurally unavailable.** No path resolves the four but the kit table and the knob files, so no invocation can substitute a platform default for a consumer's value.
- **So the residual failure is total and visible, never partial and wrong.** A malformed queue config, which the table validator refuses, drops the **whole** counter group and changes nothing else about the bar — one of the degradations above. A consumer's configured tier silently missing from a rendered tally has no spelling on this path.

The render cost is one in-process scan per statusline fire, which is the kit's most frequent trigger by a wide margin (§The usage.txt contract). A full scan of a ~3,600-line queue measures in single-digit milliseconds, so the group takes no cache.

## model-verdict

Emits one verdict line naming the model the session runs on and, with `--expect <class>`, whether that model meets the class (§The tier binding): `bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --model-verdict [--expect <class>] [<transcript.jsonl | session8>]`. Without it every tiering rule is unverifiable from inside the session it binds: a stage session dispatched on the mechanical class cannot tell whether it got it, and a pin cannot be seen holding or failing.

**Which transcript.** A `.jsonl` operand is read as given. An eight-character operand names a transcript through the shared inverse lookup, whose normalization strips a leading `agent-` from each candidate (drift-kit/SPEC.md §The stage-economics meter). Bare, the arm takes the delegation-aware derivation's pick (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §bin/session-id.sh): a top-level session's own transcript, and for a dispatched session the newest transcript under its lead's `subagents/`. The sessions dir is `DELEGATION_KIT_SESSIONS_DIR`, empty meaning derived, as drift-kit's knob is. **Honest limit:** a sibling session writing at the same moment can out-date a dispatched caller's own transcript, so the line prints the key it read, and a caller holding its own id passes it.

**Which model.** The newest assistant record whose usage is not all zero gives `message.model`. The record is read by the meter's own per-record reader, so the two agree on what a record's model and usage are. All-zero is the test `--price-coverage` applies to an id's summed counts, here applied to one record, since the harness's synthetic records carry zero usage. The newest record exists while a tool call runs, so a session asking about itself finds its own current turn. A model switched mid-session is read as the model now running.

**The verdict.** The id's classes are the bound classes whose value it matches (§The tier binding). Without `--expect`, the line is a reading and exits 0. With it, the id meets class `E` when it matches `E` or a class ranked above it. The ranking is judgment, then routing, then mechanical, so a check at a floor passes a stronger model.

| verdict | when | exit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `READ` | no `--expect`, and a model id was read | 0 |
| `OK` | the id meets the expected class | 0 |
| `BELOW` | the id matches only classes ranked under the expected one | 1 |
| `UNBOUND` | the id matches no bound value | 1 |
| `UNKNOWN` | no transcript, no counting record, the binding empty under `--expect`, or the expected class unbound | 2 |

`UNBOUND` is a mismatch rather than an unknown, since the model is known and the binding does not name it. Under a pinned class it is the pin bypassed. Under aliases it is a model outside the consumer's tiers, a forced subagent model for one.

**The line** is `model-verdict: id=<id> session=<key> class=<classes|none> expect=<class|-> -> <VERDICT>`, followed by its consequence, on §usage-verdict's verdict-string contract: reading, epistemic status, consequence.

- `OK` and `READ` carry no consequence clause.
- `BELOW` and `UNBOUND` carry `— the session is not on its expected tier; stop before work that needs it and have it re-dispatched at that tier`.
- `UNKNOWN` carries its cause in parentheses, then `— tier unverified, not refused; the caller's rule says whether to proceed`.

**Exit 2 is fail-soft on refusing work**, as `usage-verdict`'s STALE is. A binding or a transcript that cannot be read is no evidence of the wrong tier, so no caller refuses on it alone.

**The arm** is an `Arm::Run` row: its 1 is the signal its callers grade, so it cannot be an `--emit-` member (§usage-verdict states the same forcing). It spawns nothing and writes nothing. Its declared knobs are `DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` and `DELEGATION_KIT_SESSIONS_DIR`, and the harness's session variables arrive as inputs, as they do for the meter. A dash-led operand naming no option, an `--expect` naming no class of the vocabulary, and a second operand are refusals at exit 2, and `--` ends option processing.

**Callers.** The consult skill at its entry (lifecycle-kit/templates/consult.md), a stage session whose dispatch names its class, and any session asked what it runs on.

**Why the transcript.** The top-level interface already renders the model through this kit's `--statusline` arm, which reads the harness's statusline payload. `SessionStart`'s payload carries a `model` field only optionally, and no other hook event carries one. So the running model reaches a session only through its transcript, and a hook-side route would reach top-level sessions alone, where the statusline already shows it.

**Two shapes were weighed and refused.** *Stamping the model into `--enter-stage`'s record* would change the stamp grammar of lifecycle-kit's state machine for a fact a stage session can ask for at the moment it needs it. It would also leave a consultation, which stamps nothing, uncovered. *Refusing the delegated case, as the overhead meter does*: the meter must never measure a sibling's transcript, because its output is a logged cost. A verdict prints the transcript key it read, and its caller can check that key against its own stamp id or pass its id as the operand, so a verdict can take the newest transcript where a meter cannot.

## Trend reporter

`bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --emit usage-trend [--] [history-file]` reads the history log and reports how the footprint evolves. It is advisory tooling, never a gate: exit **0** when a report is emitted, **2** when the knob is unset or the history missing or unreadable, never **1**, since it renders no verdict and the verdict stays the sole pause authority. The source signal is known-noisy: a rolling-window reading spikes and reverts when the next push corrects a harness over-report. So the reporter separates signal from noise by the window's one physical constraint: within a segment, true usage never decreases.

1. **Segment** samples per axis by that axis's reset epoch, `login_at`, `account` and `tier`. The 5h axis keys on `resets_at` and the weekly axis on `resets_7d`, since the windows roll independently and a weekly segment spans many 5h ones. A timer reset, a `/login`, or an account or tier change each starts a segment. Only within-segment comparisons are meaningful, so an account switch is a boundary, not a flagged anomaly.
2. **Flag** any sample whose pct is below an earlier one in the same segment as a monotonicity violation. The downward correction indicts the elevated samples before it as reader noise, and both sides are excluded from rate math, never averaged in. Median-of-3 smoothing resolves single-sample spikes, and segment endpoints keep their own value.
3. **Report** per segment and axis: first and last smoothed pct, pct-per-hour rate, token deltas when token keys ride, tier, sample count and suspect-sample count. A high suspect ratio means the producer is unreliable, and no number from that segment is trusted. The weekly axis also reports headroom against `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT_7D` at the current rate, the planning number for how much delegation the week still affords. When `account` is present, segments group under an account heading, so a rotating operator reads one weekly trajectory per account.
4. **Combine** the accounts after the per-segment report, where the log carries two or more distinct `account` values: one line per account, its newest weekly segment's last smoothed pct and its headroom against `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT_7D`, then `accounts: <n>, at or over the weekly ceiling: <k>`. The view is advisory and sums nothing: accounts may differ in tier, so a sum would add different denominators, and the pause decision stays per account. An account with no weekly segment prints `-` for both values. The newest segment is the one whose last sample is latest, samples carrying no `account` join no line, and the count is at-or-over, as the pause compare is. A log with one account, or none, prints no block. No line of the block begins with a segment line's prefix, two spaces and then `[5h]` or `[7d]`, on which §Testing anchors its segment counts.

**The family is `Arm::Emit`, because the contract declares no 1.** The emitting family maps success onto 0 and failure onto 2 and can never return 1 (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm). §usage-verdict is an `Arm::Run` because its 1 is the blocking signal a hook grades; here the collapse costs nothing. The front-end composes `--emit-usage-trend` from its own `--emit <name>` operand, so the arm takes no front-end `case` arm, and a member spelled otherwise would be reachable by no shipped front-end.

**Its usage lives at its shape refusal.** The front-end's `--help` gives the `--emit-` family one generic line and enumerates no member, so this arm's usage is not owed there. `--emit usage-trend --help` prints the usage block at exit 2, which discharges the discoverability half of the `bin/`-tool contract (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The bin/-tool contract).

**The argv.** The `[history-file]` positional is an argument the rule consumes rather than a selector of configuration, and it overrides the configured path for test injection. It resolves *lazily*: the knob is read only when no positional rides. A positional beginning with `-` that is not a recognized option is a refusal (usage on stderr, exit 2), with `-h`/`--help` its named instance, and `--` ends option processing so a dash-led path stays reachable. A second or later positional is ignored rather than refused, so no accepted invocation turns into a refusal.

**The declared knobs are `DELEGATION_KIT_USAGE_HISTORY` and `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT_7D`**, both rows of delegation-kit's static table (§Layout and configuration), so no reader-side default can drift from a consumer's override. They differ in how absence reads. The history knob's default **is** the empty string, so an unset knob and a configured-empty one are one reading, which takes the arm's own "unset — no history to report" diagnostic. The ceiling's default is a real value, so a failed read of it is a resolution failure and surfaces as one.

**The wire shape is a contract.** `--usage-verdict` appends the sample line (§The usage.txt contract) with optional keys **omitted rather than emitted empty**. So the reader defaults a missing `account` or `tier` to `-`, a missing `login_at` to `0`, and a missing `verdict`, `tokens_in` or `tokens_out` to `-`. Each default is the reader's half of that rule (§The usage.txt contract). The two-axis emission is the same contract: a 5h record per sample, and a weekly one only when **both** weekly keys are present, so a log without them yields 5h segments alone rather than an error.

**The segment order is a contract the reader reproduces, and its tie rule is a byte comparison of the whole record rather than input order.**

- Records sort by account, axis and tier bytewise, `LC_ALL=C`'s effect, since a locale-aware comparator would diverge silently on a non-ASCII account name.
- Then by `login_at`, the axis reset and `updated_at` numerically, a value carrying no numeric prefix reading as zero.
- The segmenter keys on that tuple **as text** and flushes on change. A different order yields different segments rather than a differently ordered report, and reading a number out of the tuple would merge a boundary two spellings of one epoch keep apart.
- The last resort, the seventh comparison, is the **whole line**, as GNU `sort` falls back to absent `-s`. A merely stable sort keeps input order instead, and on a full-key tie the two choose different segment endpoints and so print different token deltas.

## bin/wait-probe

`bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --wait-probe <subcommand> [args]` is the instrument behind §Operative residency's wait-primitive measurement. It stands a **known-duration producer** up, exercises candidate wait forms against it, and records one line per trial. It is hand-invoked, with no trigger, and wired into **no** tier: it launches processes and sleeps for its declared durations, so a gate tier would put a multi-second sleep on every commit for a measurement bought once. It writes nothing tracked. Producers, markers and launch records go to `GATE_SDK_TMP_DIR`, trials go to the capture-tier evidence file below, and it mints no knob of its own for either (§Layout and configuration owns why).

**Six subcommands, the word an operand rather than part of the flag**: `produce`, `waiter`, `arm-local`, `record`, `report`, `sweep`. That is `--hook`'s shape: the front-end hands the binary an arm's own argv unchanged, so a subcommand-bearing arm is a solved registration (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm).

**It is an `Arm::Run` arm-table row with its own front-end case arm, never an `--emit-` member**, on two independent grounds. The emitting family maps success to 0 and failure to 2, so it can never return the **1** below. And four of the six subcommands emit no document: they stand processes up, write launch records and append a trial line.

**Two declared knobs**, `GATE_SDK_TMP_DIR` and `GATE_SDK_WORKFLOW_DIR`, both resolved in process from gate-sdk's table. The arm's status survives the front-end's `exec` verbatim, since `exec` replaces the shell's process image, so the front-end's own unavailable status is reachable on a dispatch failure alone.

**The exit contract is three-state**: **0** on a completed subcommand, **1** for `report` with no trials recorded, **2** on misuse. The `1` is contract: *no trials recorded* is not misuse, since the invocation is well formed and the tool ran. Folding it into the misuse code would make an honest empty reading indistinguishable from a typo, on the one tool whose value is telling **a reading from a non-reading** (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The bin/-tool contract records that harm). That contract's split binds here: the argv-shape refusal and the `--` escape cross to the arm, and the `-h`/`--help` arm retires to the front-end. A one-line front-end summary cannot carry a six-entry roster, so the arm prints its roster on the **misuse** path, stderr at exit 2, which is not the help arm.

**Three parts of the instrument stay shell, each on its own ground**: gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The port-candidate criteria's *the program is the rule* clause, where the program decides a **measurement** rather than an assertion.

- **The wait body.** The `until [ -f <marker> ]` / `until kill -0 <pid>` loop **is the artifact under measurement**. It arms the marker form `templates/agent-execution.md` prescribes and the inverted liveness form that template names as the trap, which guard-kit's loop-polarity rules are shaped to. A compiled loop would measure a form no session runs. The loop and its four exit traps are one constant the arm **execs** into, so every form arms the same wait body.
- **The arming of the `local` form.** `arm-local` arms the waiter as a **detached shell child**, the harness-uninvolved control, and a crate-armed child would make that form's name false. The arming stays `nohup … &` inside a shell literal, with the launched PID read back and written to the `<key>.run` record.
- **The producer's launch, a hazard rather than a definition.** A shell's backgrounded child is **reaped**, so `kill -0` on its recorded PID fails once it exits. A compiled child whose parent outlives it leaves a **zombie**, and `kill -0` on a zombie **succeeds**. That would invert `producer_alive_at_exit` on every trial, the field cause (i)'s tell reads, while the instrument reported clean.

**The arm `exec`s into the wait body rather than spawning it, for measurement validity.** Backgrounding the wait puts **one** process between the harness and the loop, and a spawning arm would put two. The reaping cause the instrument detects is a property of the process the harness signals, so `exec` keeps the depth and the signal disposition a session runs.

**The retained shell dependencies are declared**: the arm spawns `bash`, and through it `nohup`, `sleep` and `date`. `bash` is on `GATE_SDK_PROGRAM_FLOOR` and `nohup` and `sleep` are not, a stated cost: a known-duration producer *is* a `sleep`, and a detached shell child *is* what `nohup … &` produces, so they are the rule's semantic content. A consumer lacking either gets the failure it already got.

**One trial** launches a producer that sleeps a declared duration and then writes a completion marker. It records the producer's PID at launch in a `<key>.run` file as `templates/agent-execution.md` mandates, so the probe's own producers are visible to `check-producer-liveness` and to guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer`. It arms the wait form under test and records whether the waiter exited **before** the marker appeared. That relation is the whole measurement: a waiter that outlives its condition is working, and one that exits early is the failure.

**The `<key>.run` record's shape is a contract, coupled at run time rather than in any scan.** `check-producer-liveness` and guard-kit rule `git_mutation_under_producer` read the two-field `pid=<n> run=<key>` line the instrument leaves in the scratch directory, never its source. A form writing another shape would leave its producers **invisible** to the align-entry refusal and the tracked-tree mutation guard, with every battery green.

**The producer's duration is swept, not fixed**, since one duration cannot distinguish the candidate causes. The probe reads no harness internals and asserts no ceiling; it varies the input and reports where the behaviour changes. The duration sweep is a constant, never a knob: the durations are the instrument's calibration, and varying them varies the experiment.

**Two axes, and the second earns the instrument its keep.** The *form* axis varies how the wait is armed, and the *predicate* axis varies what the loop tests, a completion marker or the producer's liveness. A form-only instrument can report that a form failed but never **why**, and the observed failures sat on the predicate axis. Both axes are swept in one run on identical producers, so no cell rests on a comparison with a differently shaped trial.

**The per-trial line.** Every field has a named reader, and the field set is closed:

- **`form`** — the wait form the trial armed. Read at the comparison; the only field that distinguishes the candidates.
- **`predicate`** — what the loop tested. Read at the classification; it separates a failing *form* from a failing *condition*.
- **`producer_ms`** — the producer's declared duration. Read at the sweep analysis, where a ceiling shows as a threshold.
- **`waiter_exit`** — the waiter's exit status. Read at the classification, to separate a signal status from a clean zero with the marker absent.
- **`marker_at_ms`**, **`waiter_at_ms`** — elapsed wall-clock at the marker's appearance and at the waiter's exit. Read **only together**: their *order* is the measurement.
- **`producer_alive_at_exit`** — whether the recorded PID still answered `kill -0` when the waiter exited, on the recorded PID and never a process-table pattern.
- **`class`** — `ok` on a clean trial, otherwise the cause from the closed list below.

The producer's PID is **not** logged: the `<key>.run` record holds it as long as the wait needs it.

**The candidate causes are enumerated before the run**, so a run returns a cause rather than an anecdote:

- **(i) `reaped`** — the harness or the session boundary killed the backgrounded shell. Tell: a signal exit status with the producer still alive.
- **(ii) `ceiling`** — a wall-clock deadline the form carries. Tell: early exits cluster above one duration, and short trials never fail.
- **(iii) `predicate`** — the condition went true, or could not go false, for a reason unrelated to the producer. Tell: a clean **zero** exit with the marker absent, reproducing on a form the harness never touched.
- **(iv) `unexplained`** — recorded and reported as such. A closed list that cannot say *I do not know* would force a wrong attribution.

**Honest limits, a division of labour.** Cause (ii)'s tell is a **threshold across the sweep**, which no single line carries. `record` classifies from the tells one trial shows, and `report`, the only reader that sees the sweep whole, names (ii); a line's `class` is not the run's verdict. The probe arms the forms a shell can arm, and the *session* arms the harness's own, calling `record` for each. The tool cannot invoke a harness tool parameter, so a run measuring the harness forms is a session's, and `sweep` is the harness-uninvolved reproducer a second machine executes.

**The instrument *around* the subject is compiled.** `record`'s trial-line assembly and four-arm cause attribution are text and arithmetic over files the subject wrote. `report`'s verdict block carries the per-cell tallies, the **ceiling tell** and the predicate-shaped conclusion; it enforces the limit above, and it is the one verdict no trial line carries. `sweep`'s orchestration keeps the recorded-PID liveness predicate **and its `while` polarity**, since an `until` there would reproduce the defect the instrument found. `produce`, `waiter` and `arm-local` compile their bookkeeping only (the `t0` stamp, the marker and heartbeat paths, the `<key>.run` write, the PID read-back) and keep their shell literals. The shell parts are the artifact under test, not an unfinished port.

**The trials land in `.workflow/wait-primitive-evidence.txt`**: capture-tier, gitignored and advisory (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The workflow directory), declared on the close-surface roster (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §The close-surface roster) with its rotation as the reclaim path:

close-surface: .workflow/wait-primitive-evidence.txt advisory reclaim=bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --emit capture-drain .workflow/wait-primitive-evidence.txt

`advisory` for the reason §The turn-end liveness hook gives its log. The **finding**, the branch the trials select and its grounds, lands in this SPEC and never in the log: the log is evidence, not the record.

## The foreign-vendor run

A read-only audit or a mechanical sweep, the mechanical tier class (§The tier binding), may run on another vendor's coding agent instead of a dispatch. Every dispatch otherwise rides the master harness's own `Agent` tool, so all delegated spend lands on one vendor's window. `bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --foreign-run <adapter> <prompt-file> [--mode audit|sweep] [--key <key>]` runs one such unit. The mode defaults to `audit`. The key defaults to the prompt file's basename without its extension, must be one path component, and names the run's directory, `<GATE_SDK_TMP_DIR>/foreign/<key>/`.

**The executor is kit mechanism and each adapter is consumer config.** An adapter is the program and its argv, carrying its vendor's non-interactive mode, output format, sandbox mode and model. It names a vendor, so it is never a kit literal (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The provenance seam). An adapter speaks its vendor's machine plane, never its interactive screen: a screen relay yields rendered frames rather than turn events and bets on the vendor's least-stable surface.

**A resumable session is one process per turn, its conversation held by the vendor.** A vendor's headless mode exits at its turn end and keeps the conversation in its own session store, which its resume form continues. So the kit holds no conversation, only the binding beside the clone. **A permission request travels as an escalation.** The adapter pins a policy that never prompts, an action it refuses reaches the agent as a failure, and the agent reports it at its turn end like any open question. A widening is a new run under an adapter configured wider, never a flag a resume adds. **The report is the escalation channel, and the kit classifies none.** Whether a turn ended on a result or a question is the dispatcher's reading. The dispatcher answers a question with `--foreign-resume`, and the agent continues in the same clone and the same vendor session.

1. **The tree.** A shared, no-checkout clone of the toplevel, checked out detached at the committed `HEAD`, under the run's directory as `tree/`, with its `origin` remote removed. A clone rather than a linked worktree, on §upgrade-smoke's ground (gate-sdk/SPEC.md): a killed run leaves scratch and no registration. A clone's refs are its own, and with no remote nothing in it can push here, so a foreign commit is unreachable from this repository's refs by construction rather than by a check that could be skipped. The foreign agent sees committed state only, as an isolated child does (§The delegation model, isolation's untracked-blindness cost). A clone an earlier refusal kept under the same key is evidence awaiting inspection, so the run fails rather than overwrite it.
2. **The spawn.** The adapter's argv from `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_ADAPTERS`, spawned directly with no shell, with its working directory the clone and its standard input the prompt file. The `GIT_*` location variables are removed from its environment, so a hook context cannot point it back at this repository. Standard output goes to `report.txt` and standard error to `stderr.txt`, both in the run's directory, bounded by `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_TIMEOUT`, whose expiry kills the adapter's own process.
3. **The shape.** Checked after every spawn that started, a timeout or a non-zero exit included, and a refusal outranks a failure. A clone whose `HEAD` or any ref moved is refused: the foreign agent committed, and its commits are never taken. In `audit` mode, a clone whose status is not clean is refused: an audit wrote. In `sweep` mode, after an adapter exit of 0, the clone's whole change, untracked files included, is written as a binary-safe `change.patch` in the run's directory against the checked-out commit. An empty change writes no patch.
4. **The cleanup, or the session.** On a one-shot adapter the clone is removed, except on a refusal, where it stays for inspection and the line names it. On a resumable adapter, one with a resume form in `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_RESUME`, an `OK` turn opens a **session**. The clone stays, and `session.txt` in the run's directory records `adapter=<adapter> mode=<mode> base=<commit> turn=1 id=<id|->`. The refs the shape check compared are kept beside it as `refs.txt`, for the next turn's check. A resume form needing an id where no marker produced one opens no session: the clone is removed and the line says why.

**The verdict line** is `foreign-run: adapter=<adapter> mode=<mode> key=<key> exit=<status> report=<path|none> patch=<path|none> -> <VERDICT>`. `exit` is the adapter's status, `timeout` when the bound expired, and `-` when nothing was spawned, where `report` reads `none`. An `OK` that opened a session carries the consequence clause `— resumable: --foreign-resume <key> <prompt-file>`, and one that could not open a session carries `— not resumable (<why>)`, §usage-verdict's verdict-string contract of reading, status and consequence. The exits:

- **0 `OK`** — the adapter exited 0 and the shape held.
- **1 `REFUSED (<why>)`** — the shape broke: `committed`, or `audit wrote`, followed by `— clone kept at <path>`.
- **2 `FAILED (<why>)`** — an unknown adapter, an unreadable prompt file, a kept clone or an open session under the key, a clone or spawn failure, a timeout, or a non-zero adapter exit. The report is kept, and the line names the adapter's status.

A malformed argv (an operand count other than two, a dash-led token naming no option, a mode other than the two, a key that is not one path component) is a shape refusal at exit 2, with the usage on standard error. `--` ends option processing.

**What returns, and how it lands.**

- `report.txt` is the unit's return value, and a durable one: it outlives the calling session, so a successor finds it. The adapter selects the vendor mode whose standard output is the report, and the kit reads bytes: parsing each vendor's event stream would put a vendor schema in a kit literal.
- A sweep's `change.patch` is applied by the dispatching session, which verifies it as it verifies any agent commit (§Verify after every agent commit) and commits it under its own attribution. Foreign work thus carries no vendor trailer, and the contributor list does not grow per vendor. A consumer's README states that method once. The foreign agent committing on the shared tree is refused, since it would race the shared git index.
- The adapter's argv carries its own vendor's model choice, an alias or a pinned id, on §The tier binding's follow-or-pin reading. No adapter binds to a tier class: the tier binding's readers all read the master harness's model ids, so the dispatcher picks an adapter by name for the unit's class.

**Honest limits.**

- **Confinement.** The clone is the agent's working directory, not a sandbox. A foreign program can write any path it can reach, so an adapter selects its vendor's read-only or workspace-bounded sandbox mode, and that mode is the only confinement there is. This is the residue §The delegation model records for a program an isolated child runs.
- **No guard fires.** The executor is reached through a shell call, and the budget guard and the dispatch guard fire on the master harness's `Agent` tool, so neither fires on it and nothing budgets a foreign vendor's window. Gating it behind the budget guard is refused: the guard reads the master harness's window, and a foreign run spends a different vendor's, which is the point. A foreign vendor's budget oracle is a later, vendor-keyed seam.
- **The kill.** The timeout kills the adapter's own process. A program the adapter started may outlive it, and the clone is removed beneath it.
- **Resume is the vendor's.** A session resumes only as far as its vendor's store keeps it: an ephemeral open persists nothing to resume, and a vendor that expires a session fails the next turn. A resume form that does not restate the open form's confinement runs the next turn under the vendor's default policy.
- **A turn outlives a foreground call.** The timeout's default exceeds a foreground call's ceiling, so a long turn is launched backgrounded with its liveness record (templates/agent-execution.md, **Background + notification, never poll**). While that record names a live turn, the guard blocks every tracked-tree mutation in the checkout (guard-kit/SPEC.md §The generic ruleset, rule `git_mutation_under_producer`), so the dispatcher commits nothing until the turn ends.
- **The report's own claims.** A report's claims carry the tier a dispatcher gives any child's report: checked before they are built on.

It is an `Arm::Run` row that spawns a consumer's program which may reach the network, so it takes a bare-flag spelling and stays out of the fence-safe set (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm). Its declared knobs are `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_ADAPTERS`, `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_RESUME`, `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_SESSION_MARKER`, `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_TIMEOUT` and `GATE_SDK_TMP_DIR`.

### Resuming a session

`bash gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh --foreign-resume <key> <prompt-file>` runs the next turn of the session under `<GATE_SDK_TMP_DIR>/foreign/<key>/`. It reads `session.txt`, spawns the adapter's resume form from `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_RESUME` as **The spawn** does an open, in the kept clone with the prompt file on standard input, and bounds it by `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_TIMEOUT`. Before the spawn, the previous turn's `report.txt` and `stderr.txt` move to `report.<n>.txt` and `stderr.<n>.txt`, `<n>` the turn they came from, so every turn's return survives. A new `--foreign-run` under a free key clears the rotated reports a closed session left.

**The shape** check runs against `refs.txt` from the open and, in `sweep` mode, regenerates `change.patch` against the session's `base` after every spawned turn, a `FAILED` one included, so the patch is always the session's whole change. Then:

- **`OK`** — the turn counter advances, and the line carries the resumable clause again.
- **`REFUSED`** — the session ends. `session.txt` is removed, so nothing can resume it, and the clone stays as a refusal's evidence.
- **`FAILED`** — the turn counter advances, and the session stays open: a vendor window that refused the turn may be retried after it resets. A missing `session.txt`, an unknown key, an unreadable prompt file or an adapter the knobs do not configure fails without a spawn.

The line is `foreign-resume: adapter=<adapter> mode=<mode> key=<key> turn=<n> exit=<status> report=<path|none> patch=<path|none> -> <VERDICT>`, on the run's exit codes. A field the arm could not read before the spawn reads `-`.

`--foreign-resume <key> --close` ends a session the dispatcher is done with. It removes the clone, `session.txt` and `refs.txt`, keeps every report and the patch, and prints `foreign-resume: key=<key> -> CLOSED` at exit 0. A key with no open session is `FAILED` at exit 2. A session never closed is reclaimed with the scratch dir at the consumer's work-unit boundary. A malformed argv is a shape refusal at exit 2, and `--` ends option processing. It is an `Arm::Run` row with `--foreign-run`'s grounds and declared knobs.

**Honest limit: the dispatcher is a key's one writer.** Nothing serializes commands sharing a key, so two resumes, or a resume and `--close`, running together can rotate one report, write one turn or remove a running turn's clone. The dispatcher runs one command per key at a time; the kit builds no lock.

A gitignored `delegation-config.local.knobs` in the gates dir is the home for a private value a tracked file cannot carry. A file value for an indexed knob replaces its default whole. The kit's table validator refuses a malformed config at exit 2 with every finding — a broken machine gates nothing. Deliberately not knobbed beside it:
  - a fork-type-name knob and an accepted-isolation-values knob, since `fork` and `worktree` are the **harness's** dispatch vocabulary, which a guard carries as literals as the wakeup-guard member matches `ScheduleWakeup`/`CronCreate`, and knobbing them would imply a portability the mechanism lacks;
  - a warn-only or per-rule opt-out, since a doctrine downgradable per session is the honour system these rules exist to end, so the valve stays the whole guard's registration;
  - `isolation: remote` satisfying D2: plausible, since a remote agent cannot touch the local index, but unverified here and gated, so it is excluded rather than admitted on plausibility.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_MUTATING_TYPES` — agent-type names the consumer declares mutating, the only types D4 lets dispatch unisolated (§The delegation model). Default empty, in which case D4 is inert by construction; the entries are the consumer's own roster on `DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES`' provenance ground. `isolation: remote` does not satisfy D4, on the same recorded exclusion as D2's.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_REQUIRE_TIER` — D5's switch, `on` or `off`; default `off`, in which case D5 is inert. The table validator refuses any other value. A switch rather than a roster of model names, because the kit ships no model name and the tier each type rides is its definition's `model:` field (§check-agent-tier-explicit) or the dispatch's own `model`.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_AGENT_DIR` is also D5's lookup root: a type counts as tiered when a definition under it declares that type and states `model:`.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_TIER_MODEL` — the tier binding (§The tier binding): indexed, one `<class>=<model>` element per class, the class one of `judgment`, `routing` and `mechanical`; default empty, which is off. The table validator refuses a malformed element, an unknown class, a class bound twice, an empty value, a value carrying whitespace, an `inherit` value, an all-digit value and a value equal to the leading token of a different bound value. Being indexed, it has no environment spelling: a freshness gate reads it, and a per-session override would red that gate against an unchanged tree.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_SESSIONS_DIR` — the transcript directory `--model-verdict` reads (§model-verdict); default empty, which derives `<config-home>/projects/<cwd-slug>` through the crate's one derivation (lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §bin/session-id.sh), the same emptiness drift-kit's `DRIFT_KIT_SESSIONS_DIR` takes. A kit resolves its own knob and hands the answer to the shared module. So this knob, drift-kit's and lifecycle-kit's environment-only `LIFECYCLE_KIT_SESSIONS_DIR` are three names for one default.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_STATUSLINE_INBOXES` — array of `<label>=<path>` elements, each a repo-root-relative bullet file the status line counts (§The statusline arm); default empty, which renders no inbox group. The table validator refuses an element with no `=`, an empty label or an empty path, at exit 2.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_ADAPTERS` — the foreign adapters `--foreign-run` spawns (§The foreign-vendor run): indexed, each element `<adapter>=<word>`, an adapter's argv being its words in element order. Default empty, which configures no adapter. The table validator refuses an element with no `=`, an adapter name outside `[a-z0-9-]` or an empty word. A word containing `@PROMPT_FILE@` has the prompt file's absolute path substituted. The kit ships no adapter, since every one names a vendor's program, so a consumer's adapters usually live in the gitignored `.local` overlay.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_TIMEOUT` — the wall-clock bound on one foreign run, in seconds; default `1800`, validated a positive integer by the table validator. A run outliving it is killed and reads as failed.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_RESUME` — each foreign adapter's resume form (§The foreign-vendor run): indexed, each element `<adapter>=<word>`, the form's argv being its words in element order, as `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_ADAPTERS`' are. A word containing `@SESSION_ID@` has the session's recorded id substituted, and `@PROMPT_FILE@` the turn's prompt file. Default empty, which leaves every adapter one-shot. The table validator refuses a malformed element and an adapter that `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_ADAPTERS` does not configure. A resume form restates its confinement, since a vendor's resume form may spell its sandbox differently from its open form.
- `DELEGATION_KIT_FOREIGN_SESSION_MARKER` — the literal after which a turn's output carries the vendor's session id: indexed, at most one `<adapter>=<marker>` element per adapter. The id is the run of `[A-Za-z0-9._:-]` after the marker's first occurrence, past any blanks, in the turn's standard output, else its standard error; the blanks are skipped because a knob value cannot end in one. Default empty. An adapter with no marker records no id, so its resume form must not carry `@SESSION_ID@`, and the validator refuses one that does. Such a form resumes through a vendor's "most recent session in this directory" form instead, which the clone's own working directory scopes.

`check-gate-tamper` registers in the consumer's `gates.list` (tier: precommit).

`agent-budget-guard` is a hook, not a gate, so it registers under `PreToolUse` matcher `Agent` in the consumer's `.claude/settings.json` (beside the guard-kit Bash guard) rather than in `gates.list`. Wire `bash "<project-dir>/gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh" --hook agent-budget-guard` (`<project-dir>`: guard-kit/SPEC.md §The shell guard). The verdict rule is compiled into the same binary, so the guard calls it **in process** and resolves no path; its declared knob slice is `--usage-verdict`'s twelve (§usage-verdict). Registration is the whole opt-in: unwired, the guard is inert. A consumer's session brief may also print the verdict line at SessionStart; context-kit's template stays uncoupled from delegation-kit.

`subagent-stop-liveness` is a third hook, and the only one on a non-`PreToolUse` event. It registers under `SubagentStop` in the consumer's `.claude/settings.json` — an event that takes **no matcher**, so the entry carries a `hooks` array alone and is not tool-scoped, the shape a `SessionStart` entry already has. Wire `bash "<project-dir>/gate-sdk/bin/run-gates.sh" --hook subagent-stop-liveness`; it sources no kit lib at all (§The turn-end liveness hook owns why) and reads its own compiled `check-producer-liveness` unless `DELEGATION_KIT_LIVENESS_CMD` overrides it, so wiring alone is enough and no reader path is owed. Registration is again the whole opt-in, and here the opt-in is also the consent: the wiring is a permission-surface write, so it is the consumer's own act and never an agent session's.

`agent-dispatch-guard` is a second hook on that same matcher, registered the same way and **independently**. The harness fires every hook a matcher carries and any exit 2 blocks, so neither guard knows about the other and a consumer may wire either alone (§The delegation model owns why they are two hook members). It reads the consumer's delegation config for D2's and D4's rosters, D5's switch, the agent-definition directory D5 looks a type up in, and D6's tier binding, and nothing else. Registration is again the whole opt-in: unwired, it is inert. An unwired dispatch guard is the exact failure shape it exists to catch, so a consumer that delegates wires it.

**An `OK` verdict is a floor, not a recommendation.** The guard answers one question, whether there is headroom in the window *right now*, and blocks only on `PAUSE`. Everything else a dispatch decision turns on sits outside its reach:

- how long the dispatched work runs;
- what a fan-out beneath the checkpoint costs in total, since the guard re-arms on those children but prices each alone, covered at every depth and aggregated at none (§The delegation model);
- whether anything forces the dispatch now rather than after the window resets.

`OK` is therefore one input to that decision and never the decision itself; a dispatcher reading `OK` as "go" has substituted the guard's question for its own. The asymmetry is deliberate: a wrong `PAUSE` costs a delayed dispatch, while an `OK` that is right about headroom and silent about duration costs the work in flight.

## Testing

The kit's gates speak the full gate contract (`<GATE>: clean (…)` / findings + `help:` lines / exit 0-1-2). Each ships a `good/`+`bad/` fixture pair driven by gate-sdk's `--run-gate-tests` arm:

- **`check-gate-tamper`**, through `--fixture`. The bad case reaches both assertions: it carries a co-staged non-meta path *and* a newly added path exemption matching one of its own staged files, beside a bare token assertion B must pass over.
- **`check-agent-tier-explicit`**, over a fixture-local agent directory passed as its one positional argument, with a case knob file binding two classes. The `good/` side carries both passing shapes, a definition naming a cheaper class outright, with its class's bound model, and one declaring `inherit`, so the pair proves the gate discriminates omission from choice rather than merely finding a `model:` string. The `bad/` side omits the field (A), and carries a stale model line and a definition naming an unbound class (B). The arm's report, its `--write` and its refusal are its own module's unit tests, and the binding's refusals and matcher are crate tests beside the knob table and the matcher.

**The tamper pair cannot reach the live arm, a property of the injection rather than of the cases.** `--fixture` supplies both lists directly, so the function that derives them runs in no fixture case. That function reads each staged gate file's bytes out of the object store and diffs its exemption set against `HEAD`'s. `gate-tests/check-gate-tamper.test.sh` exercises it: each case builds a throwaway git repo, stages a commit shape into a real index, and runs the gate inside it. It pins the four verdicts the pair cannot:

- a HEAD-resident exemption is not "added", even when a co-staged file matches it;
- a newly appended one is;
- an added one matching nothing staged stays silent;
- a staged *deletion* of a gate file reads as an empty exemption set rather than a failure to read.

**The `usage-verdict` decision table.** `usage-verdict` renders a three-state verdict, not a clean/violation pair, so it takes a decision table rather than the gate contract. `usage-tests/cases.tsv` pairs an expected verdict token (`OK`/`PAUSE`/`STALE`/`RESET-OK`) and exit code with scenario knobs: percentage, snapshot age offset, reset offset, credential age, the snapshot's `account` and the live identity. The crate test module `native/src/usage_tests.rs` reads that table off disk. It materializes each case as a generated snapshot file and asserts verdict and exit code. Timestamps are computed relative to *now*, because static fixtures would age into permanent STALE.

- **The subject is reached in process**, one call to the same `verdict(args) -> (String, i32)` the budget guard makes (§usage-verdict).
- **The clock is read once for the whole run**, and every case's timestamps are computed against that one reading. A per-case read introduces cross-case skew that flakes exactly the at-or-over boundary rows the table exists to pin.
- **Each case runs hermetic to the host repo.** Its throwaway sandbox is also the gates directory, so the only knob file on the lookup path is the one the case writes. The ambient `DELEGATION_KIT_*` environment is stripped before each invocation. The table encodes the kit defaults, and a host override (a raised pause threshold, say) must not reshape it. `DELEGATION_KIT_ACCOUNT_CONFIG` always names a sandbox fixture, because its empty default derives the host's own account config and the host's identity would reshape the account rows.

Three properties carry that hermetic contract, and each is a place a reimplementation would quietly differ:

- **The strip is the whole `DELEGATION_KIT_*` namespace, derived at run time** from the running process's own variables. A hardcoded list of knob names would re-create precisely the failure the poison exists to catch. The prefix is the namespace because a static scalar knob's environment override is its own name (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The knob file), the spelling the subject reads. The stripped pairs are **held and restored** at the end. An indexed knob takes no environment override, so a case's `DELEGATION_KIT_REFRESH_CMD` argv is written into the sandbox's knob file, one element per line, rather than exported.
- **The poison stays a real value in the running process**: `DELEGATION_KIT_PAUSE_PCT`, at a threshold that PAUSEs every under-threshold row. It is **re-armed before every case**, so the strip is proved at each one. The seeding *order* is what makes it load-bearing. The case's scalar overrides are applied first, and each kit default is then seeded **only where the name is unset**, so a poison the strip failed to remove survives and the table reddens. A seed that wrote unconditionally would pass every assertion while proving nothing. A negative control beside the table skips the strip once and asserts the surviving poison PAUSEs. The write goes through `knobenv`'s serializing guard, the crate's sole writer of the process-global environment (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §lib/gate.sh), because the subject shares that environment with the test.
- **The per-case expectations stay literal expectations of the kit defaults.** The seeded defaults are the test's own literals rather than reads of the table, so the table does not prove the defaults it encodes are the ones delegation-kit's defaults table ships. Two holders carry that link: `check-knob-default-coupling` holds every literal default against the owning SPEC, and `smoke/install.sh` drives a real front-end invocation under its hermetic prelude. Spawning the front-end from the crate test is refused, since it would buy a `bash` spawn for what those two already cover.

`touch -d "@<epoch>"` sets the credentials mtime, the whole login-window input. It stays a spawn because `File::set_modified` and `FileTimes` stabilised in Rust 1.75 and `native/Cargo.toml` pins `rust-version = "1.71"`. **The MSRV holds this open**, not a shell subject: `native/src/emit/session_id.rs` spawns the identical `touch` in its tests with no shell subject anywhere.

**The columns** are `verdict exit pct age_off reset_off cred_age pct_7d reset7d_off account live append axis desc`. The offsets are seconds from *now*, and `pct_7d` `-` omits the weekly keys. `account` is the snapshot's key and `live` the `oauthAccount.accountUuid` the case writes into the fixture account config, `-` omitting each. `append` is the expected sample-line count, counted as `grep -c ''` counts, a final unterminated line included; a line iterator's count is silently one short of it. `axis` asserts which window a PAUSE names.

**Every verdict branch and both pause axes carry a firing and a non-firing case**, the fixture-pair discipline transplanted. The table covers:

- a weekly PAUSE while 5h is comfortable, with the axis named in the output;
- absent keys disarming the weekly axis, and a dead weekly window not pausing;
- each axis's at-or-over boundary: a reading exactly at the threshold pauses, and just under does not;
- both directions of the login reroute. An under-threshold percentage with a fresh login is STALE, not OK, and an at-or-over one on either axis is still PAUSE, not STALE. The asymmetry keeps the reroute from waving an unaffordable dispatch through;
- the account-keyed arms. A mismatched account inside and outside the login window is STALE naming the account switch, and at or over threshold still PAUSE. A matched account past the settle floor inside the old window is OK, and within the floor STALE. A snapshot without `account` keeps the blanket window. Every row asserts the account-switch wording fires on its arm alone;
- the sample-append discipline: a parsed snapshot appends one line whatever the verdict, a non-numeric snapshot appends none, and `pct_7d` shows present or omitted in the passed-through line.

Every case also asserts the fan-width field on the verdict line, matched **with its trailing space**: without it `width=20` matches `width=2` and the assertion stops discriminating. One case beside the table proves the field tracks `DELEGATION_KIT_FAN_WIDTH` rather than a literal. The default width the per-case match pins, and the refresh floor a diagnostic below names, stay **literal expectations of the kit defaults**. Reading either from the library would make the table agree with whatever the library says rather than assert what it should say.

**The demand-driven refresh** needs a command seam the table's columns do not carry. It is asserted beside the table through a stub `DELEGATION_KIT_REFRESH_CMD`, since a real poll would need the network:

- **armed**: a stale snapshot invokes the command, and the verdict reads the values the refresh wrote, not the cached ones;
- **fail-soft**: a non-zero stub leaves the snapshot byte-identical, and the verdict proceeds to judge the cached reading STALE. No refresh diagnostic leaks into the relayed verdict line. That is a **negative** conjunct over the merged stream, because callers relay the line verbatim and a stdout captured apart from stderr passes it vacuously;
- **short-circuit**: a snapshot under `DELEGATION_KIT_REFRESH_MIN_AGE` never invokes the command;
- **account switch**: a snapshot under that floor naming another account than the live one still invokes the command, and the verdict reads the live account's re-polled percentage; a failing stub over the same snapshot leaves the account-switch STALE.

Armed and short-circuit are the firing/non-firing pair over the same stub, so the absence proves the floor rather than a broken stub.

**The roll witnesses** need a pre-seeded history tail the table's columns do not carry, so they are asserted beside it too. The fixed shape is a low percentage with a credentials mtime inside the login window, which without a witness is STALE. Both witnesses present verdicts OK. Each of the four ways the log can fail to answer holds the STALE: the boundary unmoved, the previous boundary uncrossed, a tail with a non-numeric `resets_at`, and no history at all. The two single-witness cases are the discriminating pair, proving the conjunction is load-bearing rather than either witness carrying it alone. A sixth case asserts the read is not self-witnessing: a run with an empty log still STALEs and leaves exactly its own sample, so the witness can only ever be a previous reading. The witness's account partition is a unit test in the verdict's own module, over a history whose last line is another account's: the snapshot's account reads its own newest sample, an absent account the newest of all, and an account with no sample falls open.

**The usage-trend assertion set.** `--emit-usage-trend` renders no clean/violation verdict, so it ships an assertion set over a static fixture history, `usage-tests/trend-history.log`. Static epochs are safe because the reporter measures within-segment deltas, never against *now*. The same crate module carries it under the same sandbox and strip: the ambient namespace is held aside and reseeded from the kit defaults, so the arm reads the empty history knob and the unset arm fires. **It carries no poison**, because a static knob has one spelling and so no wrong reader for a poison to catch. It asserts:

- per-axis segmentation at a reset boundary, a `login_at` change and an account change;
- per-account grouping reuniting a weekly trajectory across a switch-back;
- a spike-then-correction flagged and excluded from the rate rather than averaged, and a segment ending in one keeping its last reading;
- token deltas and weekly headroom on the report;
- the combine block's golden lines over the fixture's two accounts, and its absence from a one-account log the test writes;
- the fail-closed refusals (knob unset, history missing), each read off the arm's own `Err`;
- the zero-segment **reading**, which stays a document at exit 0 rather than collapsing into the misuse code;
- the `--` escape reaching a dash-led history path. The shape refusal and its `--help` instance are the arm's own test module's (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The non-gate arm).

**Its needles are exact golden strings, and its segment counts are anchored on the reporter's own two-space indentation.** Loosening a needle into a pattern weakens the assertion, and that indentation is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

**No fixture pair for the non-gate members.** Neither runner is a gate, so no fixture pair is owed. Criterion 2's discharge for a non-gate member is the both-substrates comparison bought once at port time (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The port-candidate criteria). Nothing machine-held keeps the two substrates agreeing afterwards, so the shell originals are deleted rather than left running beside the crate tests. Where a member's output is a *measurement*, as `--wait-probe`'s is (§bin/wait-probe), the comparison takes this shape:

- a record file the member writes, such as the `<key>.run` record, is a first-class subject, since nothing static couples a gate to its shape;
- the exit status is compared per subcommand, with an empty-evidence `report` run in both substrates so the three-state contract's `1` is compared and not assumed;
- a wall-clock field is compared by the **relation** it measures (`waiter_at >= marker_at`) and the class that relation produces, never for equality. Byte equality there fails a correct port and passes nothing;
- each row is also held against its own expected value, so two empty outputs cannot pass as agreement.

A member with no live tree corpus, whose input is a crafted snapshot or processes it stands up itself, has its case set for its corpus, so the comparison over it is complete rather than demoted.

**The model verdict.** `native/src/hook/model_verdict.rs`'s test module drives the arm's library function over transcripts it writes, each verdict row both reached and missed. The binding and the sessions dir are written into each case's sandbox knob file under the budget verdict's hermetic contract, with the `DELEGATION_KIT_*` namespace held aside. It covers:

- `READ`, a bare read of an unbound id;
- `OK` by an exact id, by an alias token, and by a stronger class under a floor;
- `BELOW`, a mechanical-only match against `--expect judgment`;
- `UNBOUND`, an id matching nothing;
- `UNKNOWN` for an empty transcript, a transcript whose only assistant record has all-zero usage, the binding empty under `--expect`, and an unbound expected class;
- both operand forms, a path and an eight-character key naming an `agent-` transcript;
- the newest-record rule, a transcript switching model mid-file reading the later model;
- the shape refusals and the `--` escape.

**The foreign-vendor run.** `native/src/emit/foreign_run.rs`'s test module drives the arm's library function against a throwaway source repository. Its stub adapters are programs the crate's tests already spawn: `git` first (`git hash-object` reads the prompt, `git config --file` writes a file, `git commit --allow-empty` moves `HEAD`), and `bash` for a sweep's two writes and for the timeout row's `exec sleep` under a one-second bound. It covers:

- `OK` in both modes: an audit whose adapter prints and writes nothing returns its output as the report, and a sweep writing a tracked and an untracked file yields a patch that `git apply --check` accepts in the source repository, while an empty change yields none;
- `REFUSED`: a committing adapter in both modes as `committed`, and an audit that wrote as `audit wrote`, each keeping its clone, and a kept clone failing the next run under its key;
- `FAILED`: an unknown adapter, an unreadable prompt file, a non-zero adapter exit with its report kept, and a timeout;
- `@PROMPT_FILE@` substitution, standard input carrying the prompt, the clone removed on every non-refusal exit of a one-shot adapter, the clone carrying no remote, the argv shape, and an adapter's words assembled in element order.

The session rows run stub resume forms the same way:

- **Open.** A resumable adapter's `OK` keeps the clone and writes `session.txt` and `refs.txt`. A marker in standard output, and one only in standard error, each record the id. A resume form carrying `@SESSION_ID@` with no id captured opens no session and removes the clone, and a form without it opens one recording `-`.
- **Resume.** `@SESSION_ID@` and `@PROMPT_FILE@` are substituted. The previous turn's report is rotated, and the turn counter advances. A sweep's patch covers the session's whole change across two turns, a failed turn's included.
- **The resume verdicts.** A resume whose stub commits is `REFUSED` and removes `session.txt`. A non-zero resume is `FAILED` and keeps the session. A resume of an unknown key fails without a spawn.
- **Close and collision.** `--close` keeps every report, a second `--close` fails, and a new open clears the rotated reports. A new `--foreign-run` under an open session's key fails.
- The resume argv shape, and `session.txt` reading back what it wrote.

The adapter table's refusals, the resume and marker tables' refusals (a resume element for an unconfigured adapter, a second marker for one adapter, `@SESSION_ID@` with no marker) and the timeout's shape are crate tests beside the knob table.

**The dispatch and budget guards.** `agent-budget-guard` and `agent-dispatch-guard` are hooks rather than gates, so each speaks exit-2 + hook JSON rather than the gate output contract. Their cases run as crate `#[test]`s reaching the subject directly. **A runner lives in the crate when its *cases* can be driven from there, the subject reached in process *or* spawned.** No crate test spawns a kit **`bin/` tool as a test subject**. Crate tests do source `gate-sdk/lib/gate.sh` to read the shell library's *own* answer: a pre-binary accessor, the gates-directory default or the source stamp. That is deliberate, because a Rust literal would restore the second source of truth those tests exist to hold, and that library stays shell on its own grounds (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §lib/gate.sh).

The crate cases run under `check-crate-arms` at every commit *and* under the `native_crate` validate suite. The whole case set costs under a second of wall clock against an arm that already runs at every commit.

**`usage-tests/dispatch-guard-cases.tsv` is kit test data read by the crate test.** It pairs an expected outcome (`block`/`advise`/`fallthrough`) with `subagent_type isolation nested model desc`, and fixes one roster for its whole run (`DELEGATION_KIT_READONLY_TYPES=(ro-type)`). The type column carries four sentinels:

- a `noroster:` prefix runs the row against an empty roster, proving D2 inert on the roster's *absence* rather than on a type missing from a populated one;
- an `armed:` prefix runs it with D4's roster (`mut-type`), D5 on and one definition stating `model:` (for `tiered-type`), so D4 and D5 are driven without a directory, and with the tier binding empty, so D6 stays inert there;
- a `bound:` prefix runs it with the tier binding `judgment=big-model` and `mechanical=small`, so D6 is driven;
- `UNPARSEABLE` is the degraded row that makes the fail-open-but-loud posture testable rather than merely stated.

The `model` column's `-` is an absent model and any other value a named one, the kit naming none. Moving the table into Rust literals would trade a reviewable table for a recompile. The budget guard keeps no table: its guard-side assertion is a two-branch routing, exit 1 blocks and every other status advises, which the member's own crate test covers directly.

**Every rule still carries a firing and a non-firing case:**

- D1: a fork blocks, and a typed dispatch under an empty roster does not.
- D2: a rostered type without isolation blocks. The same type with `isolation: worktree` does not, nor does an unrostered type under a populated roster, nor the empty-roster case.
- D4: an undeclared type unisolated blocks, and so does an absent type. The same type isolated, a declared mutating type unisolated and any type under an empty mutating roster do not.
- D5: a dispatch naming no model, of a type no definition tiers, blocks. The same dispatch naming its model, a type whose definition states `model:`, and any dispatch with the switch off do not.
- D6: a named model outside the binding blocks. A named bound value, a model id carrying one as a token after its first, a dispatch naming no model, and any dispatch under an empty binding do not.
- D3: a payload carrying `agent_id` advises, and one without it falls through.

The definition lookup itself, a declared `name:` before the file stem and a definition stating no model not counting, is a crate unit test beside the table, and so is D6's refusal under an unreadable door.

**The consumer smoke.** `smoke/install.sh` copies the templates and `bin/` tools into the scratch consumer and registers the tamper gate. It drives one crafted snapshot through the `--usage-verdict` arm and asserts a verdict. It is the self-verifying install, and the holder that keeps a real front-end run and a real knob resolution in this kit's coverage.

**It opens with one hermetic env prelude covering the whole file.** The prelude strips the whole `DELEGATION_KIT_*` namespace, then sets the knobs with no per-call home: sampling off, and both pause thresholds at the defaults the 95% reading is judged against. Pinning per instance misses a knob by construction, and the miss is **silent**. An unpinned history path makes every smoke verdict append a synthetic sample into whatever trend log the ambient config names, indistinguishable in shape from a real operator reading. Stripping the whole namespace cannot miss a knob.

The credentials pin stays **at each invocation**, because gate-sdk/SPEC.md §check-test-hermetic reads it there: a line-local pin is evidence a reader and a gate can both see without tracing file-level state. The poller's own snapshot, credentials and endpoint paths stay inline too, as genuine per-call arguments.

- **The 95% assertion is code-specific.** It captures the exit status and compares it to the PAUSE code, reporting the status it observed, so a STALE regression cannot pass under a "did not PAUSE" message (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §check-assertion-strength).
- **The poll producer is driven through its `file://` stub seam.** The happy path writes a contract-valid snapshot, asserted by driving it through `usage-verdict`. A fetch failure exits non-zero and leaves a pre-seeded snapshot byte-identical, the fail-soft contract end to end.

`smoke/violation.sh` stages a gate edit co-staged with a product file in the scratch consumer and asserts the battery reds (assertion A). The violation is craftable, so the file is mandatory (gate-sdk/SPEC.md §Consumer smoke).

Both scripts stay on the shell substrate permanently and carry `# no-port:` saying so. The disposition is the class ruling at gate-sdk/SPEC.md §Consumer smoke, *The port disposition*. It reaches them by its **ground** rather than by its scope: its stated-contract cut covers the recipes answering to §Consumer smoke, and these two answer here, but its legs 2 and 3 hold of them identically.

## Out of scope

The split is protocol and mechanisms here, and a consumer's rosters, batteries and bespoke UX in its own copies.

- A consumer's validate battery (its compile, lint and test set and rename-corruption sweeps), its shared-file roster, and its width and burn anecdotes are rule content, referenced only as marked-section examples.
- A task-output tailer is not shipped: it hardcodes local harness paths and exists to break the template's **Background + notification, never poll** rule.
- The `usage.txt` write contract and a reference producer rendering the gauge bars and `iteration@stage` readout ship, its ANSI self-contained and pulling no external asset. A consumer's *particular* statusline styling, and any asset its own gauges pull, stay with the consumer.
