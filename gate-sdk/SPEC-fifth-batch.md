# SPEC amendment: fifth-batch

The **fifth budget batch** of the native port — **seven** members, a width the
operator ruled **fresh for this cut** on 2026-08-19 in the lead session through
the harness's question mechanism. It delivers one increment of
`native-gate-port-remaining-corpus`, whose deliverable is the whole corpus, so
its terminal move is a demotion rather than a Done move (canon-kit/SPEC.md
§Merging an amendment, the entry-outlives-the-amendment branch).

**The fourth cut's 6–8 is that cut's own ruling and is not citable here.** Cut
widths are ruled per cut and never inherited; this one was put and answered on
its own evidence, and a later selector reading the two together as a standing
envelope is reading a precedent that was refused twice over.

**The width was re-examined *after* this amendment was authored, on cost
evidence that had moved, and held at seven.** The operator's ruling was taken
against a survey ranking the corpus by declaration size and logic hazards;
authoring found the cut's real cost sits in **interfaces** instead (delta 3),
plus a pre-port library unit (delta 10), two caller re-pointings behind no gate
(delta 5) and one member whose largest work item that ranking cannot see (delta
9a). The moved evidence was put to the lead and the ruling **held**, on grounds
recorded here rather than left in a message thread: the member set is ruled and
promoted unchanged; **drop-any-member survives in the envelope**, so build keeps
that relief without anything being re-opened, and build is the stage that feels
the cost and may exercise it; and the added work is concentrated and named rather
than diffuse, two of its items being one-time. Recorded because a later reader
who finds the member audit would otherwise reasonably conclude the width was
never re-examined against it — the ruling is *held*, not merely un-revisited.

**The cut is executed as several build sessions, and the lead ruled who closes
what — recorded here because an obligation with no owner is the failure the split
creates.** The batching is an execution economy and never a re-scoping: each unit
is still completed whole and closed by the session that finishes it.

- **The last batch touching *this* unit takes its closing set**: delta 8's
  assertion-C re-run *after the last descriptor lands*, delta 7's criterion-5
  residual against the post-batch registry, delta 1's
  `gate-sdk/SPEC.md §The fifth budget batch`, this amendment's merge and

  The `gh-account` amendment's merge and deletion and

## The envelope

Stated first because it is what a build session needs before it cuts, and
because it bounds what may move without re-opening design.

**Asserted.** Seven members, composed by the **budget arm** off an evidence-backed
cost survey (delta 2); each takes its own descriptor, its own registry entry, its
own parity run and its own shell deletion, in its own commit. The batch's price is
measured, not reasoned.

**Not asserted, and deliberately so.** No **joint proof** binds the members. They
share no corpus derivation — `--group` partitions the owed set into singletons —
so no member's parity run lends another proof, and **build may drop any member
without invalidating the rest**. Dropping one costs the batch one member and
nothing else. The three exceptions are about *sequence*, never about which
members are admissible: delta 6's shared-snapshot constraint, delta 5's
caller re-pointing, and the `gh-account` rider's ordering — **the kind lands
after `check-identity`'s port, in its own commit, in the crate only**, and both
halves of that order are forced. Landing the kind shell-side first builds it
twice, which is what the operator's paired ruling refuses. Landing it *before*
the port's cross-substrate parity run is worse: parity is taken while both
implementations exist, so a kind added to one side first leaves the port with
nothing to be equal to and this batch's own oracle would be comparing two
different rules. Recorded here in full because the rider's own amendment merged
and was deleted with the kind, and this is the surface the ordering is a fact
about.

**Out of envelope — escalate rather than absorb.** Widening the batch past seven
or admitting a member outside the ruled set; admitting a member carrying a
criterion-7 blocker or an unsettled design fork; changing what a `.gate`
descriptor may carry; a change to **lifecycle-kit's** `LIFECYCLE_KIT_ENTRY_PREFLIGHT`
grammar (delta 5 rules the consumer-side discharge precisely so this batch does
not reach into another kit's contract); and any ruling about `check-tree-terms`'
criterion-4 hold, which is `cohort-held-members-port-prerequisites`' work.

## What changes

### 1. gate-sdk/SPEC.md gains `### The fifth budget batch`

record-only-findings rule: membership is derivable from the tree and the count
from `scripts/measured-claims.sh`'s `ported-gate-members`. A roster written here
would be a second copy that drifts the moment a member is dropped.
*design-bearing.*

### 2. The composition ground, and what is new about it

The precondition is met and was re-read at this cut rather than inherited: `bash
gate-sdk/bin/port-blockers.sh --group` reports **14 groups of exactly one, 0
undecidable, 19 still owed, 14 takeable**, so no group has a second member to
amortize a walk across and the size arm stays permanently exhausted (§The fourth
budget batch owns why that exhaustion is permanent rather than per-cut).
*mechanical.*

**What is new is that the cut composes off a *filed, witnessed* cost survey
rather than off a property read at the cut.** The fourth batch composed by
declaration size plus criterion cleanliness, reading both at its own cut; this
one takes the seven cheapest of the fourteen takeable as ranked by a survey scope
filed under lifecycle-kit/SPEC.md §The survey record, whose witness this stage
re-ran and passed — no corpus diff since the recorded rev, and the `--group`
verdict above unchanged. That makes the selection auditable after the fact and
re-runnable by a later reader, which a cut-time read is not. *design-bearing.*

**The band is exhausted downward, and that is the honest reading of the width.**
The fourth cut consumed the members at or under ~103 declaration lines; this
one's run 93 to 140, so the ceiling rose only because the floor did. Width seven
buys the same thing width eight bought last cut and nothing more — **session
overhead** spread over more members, since cheapness is not a shared derivation
and the batch amortizes nothing across its members (§The fourth budget batch
states that limit in full and it is cited rather than restated). *design-bearing.*

### 3. This cut's cost is in **interfaces**, not in logic — the batch's defining finding

The survey ranked the corpus by declaration size and per-member hazards, which is
a ranking of *logic*. What it could not see, and what this stage probed, is that
**six of the seven members carry a command-line argument** and two of those
arguments are documented on a governed surface. §The non-gate arm's ruling —
that a gate argument selecting where configuration comes from cannot survive a
port, and that a porting session should check for the shape **before sizing a
member** because it is an interface removal rather than an implementation detail
— therefore binds six times in one cut, which is more often than in the four
prior batches combined. *design-bearing.*

**Build's verdicts, taken per member and recorded as they land.** The first cut
of four found the ruling binds *less* often than the count of arguments suggests:
a **gates-dir positional** is consumed by the rule — it names the registry and
the first resolve dir — and ports unchanged, which `check-kit-enum` and
`check-root-tiering` had already established in tree; so does
`check-gate-binary-fresh`'s tree-stamp file, which is the rule's own input. Two
members delete: `check-identity`'s two arms, ruled below, and
`check-gate-fixture-coverage`'s positionals — whose ground is narrower than the
others and is stated at that member's own section, since its gates-dir positional
was what the *tests-dir* default derived from and delta 10's resolved knob
supersedes that derivation. A later member's verdict is taken the same way, on
the test rather than on the count.

**It is not a blanket deletion, and the reading that makes it one is the error to
avoid.** `check-root-tiering` kept **both** its positionals through its port
(§The third budget batch), so an argument is not unportable merely by being an
argument. The distinguishing test, stated here because six verdicts now depend on
it: an argument is unportable when it redirects something **`gate_command` has
already resolved** from the tree's own config before the exec — it arrives a
process too late and would silently change nothing. An argument the **rule
itself** consumes, arriving as argv into the subcommand, ports unchanged. Build
takes the verdict per member against that test; this amendment rules the one
member a rider depends on. *design-bearing.*

**Where a deleted argument was documented, the doc is part of the port.**
`delegation-kit/README.md` documents `check-gate-tamper`'s `--fixture <dir>` as
an invocation a reader may make, and `gate-sdk/SPEC.md`'s own
`check-gate-binary-fresh` section spells that gate's positional form. A port that
deletes an argument and leaves the sentence standing ships a documented flag that
does nothing, which is the state §The non-gate arm calls worse than no flag.
*mechanical.*

### 4. `check-identity`'s argument verdict, ruled here because a rider depends on it

The `gh-account` rider lands a third expectation kind on this member
immediately behind its port, and that rider's injection point is whatever survives
here — so the verdict is ruled at spec rather than left to the cut. The rider has
since landed and merged into gate-sdk/SPEC.md §check-identity, which is where its
own rulings now live. *design-bearing.*

**The `[manifest]` positional is deleted.** It overrides `GATE_SDK_IDENTITY_FILE`
and nothing else, so a knob already redirects every path it redirects: the
third-batch ground applies verbatim and the argument is redundant before it is
unportable. *design-bearing.*

**The `--fixture <dir>` arm is deleted too, but the third-batch ground does *not*
reach it as written, and pretending otherwise is how this member ships a hole.**
That ground was *every path the fixture arm redirected, a knob already
redirected*. Here it is **false**: the arm redirects three things, and only the
manifest has a knob. The other two are the clone's actual identity — what
`git config user.email` says, and what the clone's remotes are — which no knob
redirects because they are not paths the library resolves. So the port **mints
the missing knobs first and then deletes the arm**, rather than deleting the arm
on a ground that does not hold. *design-bearing.*

Two knobs, one per redirected actual, resolvable on names in `gate-sdk/lib/gate.sh`
so the bridge can find them; unset, each falls through to the live git read, which
is the production path. The rider's `GATE_SDK_GH_HOSTS_FILE` is the third member
of that family. **This is criterion 2's shape rather than an ergonomic**, and the
precedent is context-kit/SPEC.md §check-memory-off, which deleted its own
`--fixture` arm and states the payoff exactly: reaching the gate through knobs is
what makes the pair a parity oracle **for the live arm** instead of for a
fixture-only second code path. *design-bearing.*

**The honest limit, since the knobs are new capability and not only test
plumbing.** A knob that redirects what the clone's identity *is* can be set to
make the gate agree with a manifest it should disagree with. That is not a
weakening: the manifest is local config the same operator writes, the gate is a
self-check rather than a security boundary, and its own scope fence
(§check-identity) already puts *performing* the mapping outside it. Stated so the
capability is a ruling rather than a side effect. *design-bearing.*

### 5. Two live callers name a batch member's declaration path, and **no gate would catch either**

This is §The third budget batch's lesson arriving twice in one cut — *a port's
Point-5 reader enumeration covers gates, and a direct shell-out from a non-gate
script is outside every one of them* — and both instances were found by grepping
the tree, not by a red. *design-bearing.*

- **`gate-sdk/bin/install-hooks.sh`'s identity rung, and it fails *silently*.** It
  resolves `check-identity` through `gate_resolve` — correctly — and then runs
  `bash` on the path that comes back. After the port that path is a `.gate`
  **descriptor**: a non-executable data file whose entire content is comment
  lines. `bash` on it exits **0**. So the rung does not crash, it *passes*, and
  the opt-in identity verification the rung exists to perform is gone with no
  diagnostic anywhere. A crash would have been the better failure. The fix is the
  third batch's: re-point onto `gate_command`, which resolves the invocation argv
  for either spelling and fails closed on an absent binary. This is a **kit**
  surface, gate-sdk's own, so it lands with the port rather than in a consumer.
  *design-bearing.*
- **This consumer's close-stage entry pre-flight, and it *refuses the stage*.**
  `scripts/lifecycle-config.sh`'s `LIFECYCLE_KIT_ENTRY_PREFLIGHT` names
  `check-evidence-manifest`'s declaration path as a literal, and
  `lifecycle-kit/bin/enter-stage.sh` execs the configured argv **directly** —
  no interpreter word, so it rides the exec bit. A `.gate` descriptor is index
  mode `100644` and the ported member's `.sh` is deleted outright, so the exec
  fails, the non-zero exit refuses the entry, and **`close` cannot be entered**.
  The iteration that ports this member is the iteration whose own close it
  breaks, which is the worst session to be discovering it in. *design-bearing.*

**The discharge for the second is ruled, and its shape is chosen to keep this
batch out of another kit's contract.** The pre-flight entry stops naming a
declaration path and reaches the member through `gate_command`, via a
**consumer-side** front-end in this repo's own `scripts/` — the same re-pointing
the third batch performed on two smoke call sites. The rival shape, teaching
lifecycle-kit to resolve a *gate name* in `LIFECYCLE_KIT_ENTRY_PREFLIGHT`, is a
kit contract change and is **out of this envelope**; if build finds the
consumer-side front-end insufficient, that is an escalation and not a widening.
*design-bearing.*

**Each of these two lands *with its own member's port commit*, and that is a
different sequencing from delta 10's — the distinction matters enough to state,
because collapsing the three into one "pre-port fixes" bucket gets two of them
wrong.** Delta 10's knob fix is one edit to one shared library that three members
trip, so it lands **once, in front of the whole batch**, before the first
descriptor. These two are **per-member**: the `install-hooks` re-point belongs to
`check-identity`'s commit and the pre-flight re-point to
`check-evidence-manifest`'s. Neither is a library unit and neither can be batched
in front, because each is only *reachable* once its own member is being ported —
and neither may land **after**, which is the half that actually binds. A window
in which the descriptor exists and the caller has not moved is a window in which
the identity rung silently passes, or `close` cannot be entered; same-commit is
what makes that window zero-width. *design-bearing.*

**And the class is wider than these two instances, so the port owes the sweep
rather than the fix.** Three gate-test harnesses (`*.test.sh` siblings for
`check-gate-output`, `check-gate-binary-fresh` and `check-evidence-manifest`) also
invoke their subject by literal `.sh` path, and each is a hermetic harness the
battery runs. Every member's port re-greps the tree for its own declaration path
before the deletion lands, because the enumeration that finds these is a grep and
never a gate. *mechanical.*

### 6. Criterion 4 binds on two members, and this cut adds two instance classes to the register

Surveyed at spec by reading each member's walk rather than its name, on §The
port-candidate criteria's own instruction that the verdict is taken by **reading
the walk** and never by reading the trigger field. Four members clear by
construction, their corpora holding no declaration path at all:
`check-amendment-queue` (the queue and the amendment files),
`check-evidence-manifest` (the workflow evidence files and the queue),
`check-identity` (one manifest), and `check-gate-binary-fresh` — which reads
declaration paths as a **set**, testing existence and a path suffix and never a
byte, exactly as its conservation row has recorded since before the first cohort.
**Build re-runs the verdict at the cut** rather than reading it off this
paragraph — the corpus is derived from the tree. *design-bearing.*

**`check-gate-output` is the unambiguous bind, and the reading that misses it is
the plausible one.** Its no-fixture tag detection greps the resolved declaration
path for **every** registered member before any branch, with no short-circuit —
so it reads its own declaration's bytes on every run. The natural reading, that it
only greps the members carrying the tag, is true of its *second-stage* pattern
check and false of the first-stage detection, and a designer who checks the
second stage stops one branch early. *design-bearing.*

**`check-gate-tamper`'s verdict *flips on which configuration is read*, and that
is a new instance class the criterion's register does not yet carry.** Under the
kit-shipped `DELEGATION_KIT_GATE_FILES` default the corpus is the consumer's own
gates directory, which holds no kit declaration, and criterion 4 **clears**. Under
*this* repo's `scripts/delegation-config.sh` override the globs widen to every
kit's check dir, the gate's own declaration at `delegation-kit/checks/` falls
inside them, and staging it makes the gate read its own bytes — criterion 4
**binds**. The criterion is therefore not a property of a gate at all but of a
gate **against a consumer's config**, and reading the kit default — the natural
first stop — gives the wrong answer for the tree the port actually runs against.
Recorded beside the couple-clears-walk-binds instance, because it produces the
same failure (a missed hold) through a third route. *design-bearing.*

**`check-gate-fixture-coverage` is the cut's sharpest distinction: it is
*self-immune*, so criterion 4 clears while the ordering constraint still binds.**
It reaches a declaration's bytes only for a member with no resolvable fixture pair
— and this gate must carry a pair to pass its own rule, so it can never reach its
own bytes. The criterion's predicate is *the gate's own declaration path lies
inside the corpus it scans as content*, and for this member the answer is no for
itself and yes for its siblings. So **criterion 4 and delta 6's ordering
constraint are independent facts**, the way a conservation row and a criterion-4
hold already are: the criterion protects the *parity oracle* from the member's own
port, the ordering protects every comparison from a *sibling's*. This cut is where
they come apart, and a session that clears one by clearing the other gets a
verdict it did not earn. *design-bearing.*

**The shared snapshot is a *rev*, not a moment, and build recorded which one.**
The cut is executed as several build sessions, so "before the first descriptor of
the batch lands" is not reachable for a member ported in a later session. It does
not have to be: the pre-descriptor tree is `5bcef863`, the commit that lands the
delta 10 library fix and nothing else, and a later member's live-tree comparison
is taken against a worktree of that rev
(`git worktree add .tmp/pre-descriptor 5bcef863`) rather than against the tree as
it then stands. **Reap that worktree off `git worktree list`, never off
`git status`**: `.tmp/` is gitignored so the status stays clean while an
unreaped worktree still aborts a clean-tree precondition, which bites at the
commit or the smoke rather than where it was created. That is the same corpus every earlier member was compared over,
which is exactly the property the constraint asks for. Recorded here because a
session reading the paragraph below would otherwise conclude the property was
lost with the first descriptor.

**The ordering constraint the width creates is the fourth batch's and applies
unchanged**: assertion A forbids both spellings in one resolve dir, so a member's
shell form vanishes the instant its own port lands while the live corpus of every
binding sibling moves with it. **Every binding member's live-tree comparison is
captured against one shared pre-descriptor snapshot, before the first descriptor
of the batch lands** — one capture for the batch, never one per member
interleaved with the landings, which would silently compare each later member
against a corpus its predecessors already changed. The verdict is recorded as *no
disagreement found on the pre-descriptor tree*, never as parity proved; the
fixture-pair runs carry no such constraint, `gate-tests` being pruned from every
live-tree walk. *design-bearing.*

### 7. Criterion 5 is priced per member and paid per batch

The aggregate price is the **binary-less residual**, measured by `installer_smoke`'s
binary-less leg against the post-batch registry, from a clean checkout of the
batch's own commit, reached by path. *mechanical.*

**The growth is the count of the batch's `zero-config` members, not of its
members**, on the install disposition: a `never` or `on-surface` member is not
seeded into a freshly initialised consumer's registry, so no artifact-free `init`
can lose it. Probed at spec across all seven: **five** are `zero-config`, one is
`never` (`check-gate-binary-fresh`) and one is `on-surface` (`check-identity`), so
a seven-member batch grows the residual **by at most five**. The number is
measured at build; this is the prediction it is checked against, and a divergence
is a finding. The judgment is the standing one — **accept and declare**: an
adopter on an uncovered platform loses each ported member and receives that
omission declared in its own `gates.list` rather than as a broken battery. *design-bearing.*

### 8. Assertion C is re-run fresh after the last descriptor lands

On the rule §The first budget batch established: a port moves a declaration path,
which can move *other* members into or out of the derived substrate-sensitive
set, so the reading is never inherited from an earlier cut. Any row it yields
lands in §Meta-gate conservation for the binary substrate. *mechanical.*

**One row is already written and must not be re-derived as new.**
`check-gate-binary-fresh` is a batch member *and* carries a conservation row
written ahead of its own trigger, recording that it reads declaration paths as a
**set** and never a gate's source — so a port is the case that switches it on
rather than its blind spot. A cut that reports it as a fresh row is reporting a
row that has been there since before the first cohort. **And this is gate-sdk's
meta-gate conservation assertion C, not lifecycle-kit's `check-stage-entry`
assertion C** — the two share a letter and nothing else, and the second is the one
this amendment's own cross-component body trips at the next stage's entry.
*mechanical.*

### 9. Fixture-pair pre-work is real on every member, and on two it is most of the port

The pair is the only corpus a port cannot invalidate, so where an arm of a
member's rule has no case, the port ships the hole the criterion points at. The
finding that generalizes is the fourth batch's and holds here too — **a clear
criterion-4 verdict says the parity oracle is not self-referential, never that the
pair reaches every arm** — and this cut sharpens it: every one of the seven pairs
is a **single** good case and a **single** bad case, so any member with more than
two branch outcomes is under-exercised by construction, before anyone reads a
line. *design-bearing.*

**Two members are not "widen the pair" but "write the pair", and sizing that
misses this sizes the batch wrong.** `check-amendment-queue`'s one bad case
exercises **one of the eight violation classes** its rule carries — the other
seven, including both disk-pairing arms (a dangling `[spec:]` ref and an orphan
amendment), have zero fixture reach, so the parity run would compare two
implementations over an eighth of the rule. `check-gate-tamper` is worse in kind
rather than in degree: **both** its cases run the fixture arm exclusively, so the
function that actually reads gate-file byte content through `git show` — the one
the port must reproduce — is exercised by no committed case at all, and assertion
B's *violation* has no bad case in either direction. *design-bearing.*

**Three shapes recur across the rest and are named so a build session widens
before porting rather than after**: an arm reachable only through a **`.gate`
member**, which the shell-era pairs had no reason to construct; a **clean-skip or
early-out** branch, cheap to case and exactly where a vacuous pass hides; and a
**default-derivation** branch that every case bypasses by passing an explicit
override — `check-gate-binary-fresh`'s git-based stamp arm is the live instance,
its two cases both taking the hermetic stamp file, which leaves the derivation the
gate exists for untested. *design-bearing.*

### 9a. Two implementation costs the size ranking cannot see, both concentrated in one member each

**`gate_native_source_stamp` has no runtime Rust counterpart**, and the crate
carrying a stamp already is what hides it: what exists is a **build-time-baked**
constant plus a test-only comparison, not a callable routine. Porting
`check-gate-binary-fresh` therefore means writing the tree-side hash derivation as
real crate code for the first time — a runtime git invocation sequence — which is
the largest single new-work item in the batch and sits on the member the line
count ranks fourth-cheapest. *design-bearing.*

**`check-evidence-manifest` has a same-name-different-shape trap in the crate, and
reaching for the shared spelling silently changes the rule.** Its data-line
helper is a plain comment-and-blank filter; the crate's shared helper of that name
requires a `---` separator and is a **different primitive**. The correct
counterpart is a local, non-shared copy already living beside a sibling
evidence-kit gate, spec-cited to the shell helper. The port promotes that copy or
duplicates it; what it must not do is bind to the shared one, which would compile,
pass a thin pair, and be wrong. *design-bearing.*

**And the `c7=?` on `check-gate-binary-fresh` is adjudicated rather than carried.**
`port-blockers.sh` cannot decide whether the gate's invocation of the binary's own
stamp arm counts as an external program, and declines. The port **dissolves** the
question rather than answering it: a compiled member calls that arm **in-process**
as a function, the way the freshness family's comparators already call their
emitters (§The non-gate arm), so there is no spawn left to weigh. The `?` is a
scanner limit on a self-reference, not a blocker, which is the same reading §The
fourth budget batch already recorded for this member's `?` column.
*design-bearing.*

### 10. The seam, ruled per member rather than assumed

Every member of this batch reads consumer content — a gates roster and fixture
roots, a workflow evidence manifest and a queue, a governed queue plus the
amendment glob, an identity manifest, and a meta-path exemption set — and **none
of it may become a crate literal**: a Rust `const` holding one project's roster,
queue path or account vocabulary publishes that project's configuration as
everyone's mechanism (CLAUDE.md §The provenance seam). *design-bearing.*

The discharge is criterion 6's strongest form rather than a concession: each value
crosses as a **bridged knob**, computed once in the owning kit's shell library, so
the binary holds no default to drift from. The per-member check the fourth batch
paid for is inherited unchanged: any **fallback set** the shell form carries must
be re-examined, since a fallback is exactly where a project-shaped literal hides
in plain sight. *design-bearing.*

**Four knobs this batch needs are bridge-blind, and the fix is a *pre-port* unit
rather than a per-member cost — which is why it is a delta and not a checklist
item.** The bridge can only carry a knob whose default is visible to `declare -p`
**after the kit library is sourced**; a value defaulted inline at its use site, or
inside a helper function's body, is invisible to it and hits the emitter's
undeclared-knob refusal on the member's very first post-port run. Surveyed at
spec: the identity manifest path, the gate-tests dir, and both native-crate
locators are all defaulted that way today, while their structurally identical
siblings carry the guarded top-level form. **Three of the seven members —
`check-identity`, `check-gate-fixture-coverage` and `check-gate-binary-fresh` —
would trip it**, and no already-ported member declares any of the four, so this is
untested territory the batch enters immediately. *design-bearing.*

**So it lands once, in front of the batch, and that is an ordering constraint of
the same standing as delta 6's snapshot.** Resolving each onto a name in
`gate-sdk/lib/gate.sh` is one edit to one library; discovering it three times
inside three ports is three debugging sessions against a refusal whose message
names the bridge rather than the missing default. §The fourth budget batch paid
this six times by finding it per member; recording it as a precondition is what
stops the fifth paying it three more. *design-bearing.*

**The seam holds through that fix rather than being loosened by it**: naming a
default in a kit library is where a kit default already belongs, and none of the
four values is consumer vocabulary — a manifest *path*, a tests *dir*, a crate
*location*. The vocabulary they point at stays in the consumer's own files, which
is the `graph-vocab.sh` pattern working as designed. *mechanical.*

**This batch is the first to carry that rule into three kits with no ported
member of their own kind of content**, canon-kit, evidence-kit and delegation-kit
each contributing one member; the ranking survey's claim that
`check-evidence-manifest` is the *first port into evidence-kit* is **false** and
is corrected here rather than carried, that kit already dispatching two members to
the binary. What is true, and is the part that costs, is narrower: the member's
own helpers have no Rust counterpart, which is a helper-level fact and not a
kit-level one. *design-bearing.*

### 11. Per member, the port procedure is unchanged

`.gate` descriptor carrying only `# graph:`, `# spec:`, `# install:` and any
`# no-fixture:`; a Rust module registered in `gates::REGISTRY`; the `gates.list`
entry unchanged; the descriptor's `couples=` naming **every crate module the
implementation reaches, transitively**, stopping at the universal layers; the
shell original **deleted**; the cross-substrate parity run taken while both
implementations exist. *mechanical.*

**Two freshness obligations ride every commit** and neither discharges the other:
the full battery, which runs the crate's lint and test arms through
`check-crate-arms`, **and** `bash gate-sdk/bin/build-native.sh`. *mechanical.*

### 12. The generated projections the batch stales

Every ported member changes `scripts/gates.list`'s resolution and the generated
pre-commit hook; the deleted declarations and the amendments' own prose also
stale the `docs/` SPEC mirrors. The fan-out and each regen command are rostered at
docs/site-architecture.md §Generated projections; each freshness gate prints its
own command on red. Regenerate rather than hand-edit. *mechanical.*

### 13. Two takeable members are excluded by name, and both grounds are sizing

- **`check-template-copy-parity`** — takeable, clean on criteria 4 and 7, and the
  survey's own optional eighth. It is excluded because the width is seven and
  because its cost is concentrated in one place the line count hides: reproducing
  a hand-rolled `case`/`esac` surface classifier faithfully. It is the first
  member a later cut should reach for, and naming it is what keeps that from being
  rediscovered. *design-bearing.*
- **`check-graph`** — excluded on a stronger ground than width, and it is a
  ruling rather than a deferral. At 632 declaration lines with 27 shared helpers,
  it emits a self-contained HTML artifact, which makes it a **non-gate arm that
  must be designed before it is ported** (§The non-gate arm), on the
  generated-projection family's own precedent. Bundling it into a budget batch
  would violate the never-as-one-cohort property the batch mechanism rests on: a
  batch whose members must be droppable cannot carry a member whose design is a
  prerequisite for the rest of its own work. It wants its own iteration.
  *design-bearing.*

## Producers and consumers

The batch introduces **no new state, event or interface** of its own. Each
member's port is a substrate move behind an unchanged contract: the gate name, its
`gates.list` registration, its `# graph:` manifest grammar, its verdict and its
clean-line format are invariant across the seam, which is the property the parity
run proves. That absence is the causal-completeness answer rather than a gap in
it, and it is stated because a batch of seven ports invites the assumption that
seven new things were introduced. The **exceptions** are the two knobs delta 4
mints, whose producers and consumers are named there and whose third sibling
`GATE_SDK_GH_HOSTS_FILE` — landed with the account kind and merged into
gate-sdk/SPEC.md §check-identity — takes the same shape.

What each port *does* move is a **declaration path**, and its readers are named
and enumerated:

| reader | what it reads | red condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gate_resolve` (§lib/gate.sh) | `<name>.gate` before `<name>.sh` in one resolve dir | both present in one dir |
| `gate_command` (§lib/gate.sh) | the same, resolved to an invocation argv | a `.gate` member with no built binary |
| `gen-pre-commit` / `check-graph` | the manifest off whichever spelling resolved | manifest absent or malformed |
| `check-gate-substrate-parity` A/B | declaration set vs the roster `--list` prints | a descriptor with no subcommand, or the reverse |
| `check-gate-binary-fresh` | the descriptor set makes the binary load-bearing | binary older than the crate source stamp |
| `check-gate-fixture-coverage` | the fixture dir resolved for each member | a member with no pair and no `# no-fixture:` |
| `check-gate-output` | each member's clean line and `help:` remedy | a member whose module is absent or silent |
| `check-exec-bit` | file modes under `checks/` | a descriptor that is executable |
| `check-kit-enum` | which roots are kit roots, by `checks/` content | a kit root the registry does not know, or the reverse |
| `check-gate-exemption-tasks` | `check-*.sh` declarations under the check dirs | an exemption element with no live disposition |
| `gate-sdk/bin/install-hooks.sh` | the resolved `check-identity` declaration, **executed** | *none — it exits 0 on a descriptor* (delta 5) |
| `LIFECYCLE_KIT_ENTRY_PREFLIGHT` | a literal declaration path, **exec'd** | the path is absent or non-executable (delta 5) |

**The last two rows are the point of the table.** Every reader above them is a
gate, and each would red visibly. The two below are ordinary shell callers: one
reds *nothing at all* while silently retiring an assertion, and the other refuses
a stage entry. A Point-5 enumeration that stops at gates misses both, which is
delta 5's whole content.

**Three of the gate readers are themselves batch members** —
`check-gate-fixture-coverage`, `check-gate-output` and `check-gate-binary-fresh`.
A member the batch ports while it is reading a corpus the batch is changing is
exactly the shape delta 6's ordering constraint exists for.

**The narrowing question, answered rather than skipped** (canon-kit/SPEC.md §The
causal-completeness check, point 5). A port **narrows one corpus**: every
`<kit>/checks/*.sh` walk loses one file. So each reader is enumerated by its **red
condition**, not by its subject, and the non-monotone ones are the work:

- **`check-exec-bit`** is the sharp one and the port is a **swap** rather than a
  removal: the narrowing deletes a file whose required mode is *executable* and
  adds one whose required mode is *non-executable*, so it **adds** a violation
  class — point 5's own attested shape.
- **`check-kit-enum`** reds on a *set inequality* between kit roots and registry
  members, non-monotone in both directions and therefore not clearable by
  inspection. It was **probed rather than argued** at the fourth cut: the kit-root
  predicate is the existence of a `checks/` or `smoke/` **directory**, not a `*.sh`
  glob. This batch is the case that makes the probe load-bearing — porting
  `check-gate-tamper` leaves delegation-kit with **no** `check-*.sh` at all — and
  the probe's answer covers it: the directory and its two sibling descriptors
  remain, so the root does. *Recorded because the mechanical reading gives the
  opposite answer and this cut is where it would have been believed.*
- **`check-gate-fixture-coverage`** reds on a member with no resolvable pair. A
  port keeps the pair and re-points it, so the verdict is monotone here — but only
  if the pair survives the deletion of the shell original, which §The third budget
  batch already paid for once. It is also a **member of this batch**, so its own
  port must not be the commit that loses a sibling's pair.
- **`check-gate-fail-closed`** reds on a naked capture in a `check-*.sh`. Its glob
  is not substrate-adaptive, so each port removes one file from its corpus with no
  red — a *silent* narrowing, already ruled correct: a ported member has no shell
  to lint.
- **`install-hooks.sh`'s rung has no red condition at all**, which is why it is in
  the table with that stated rather than omitted as uninteresting. Its verdict is
  the exit status of a file it interprets, and the narrowing replaces that file
  with one that interprets to success. Monotonicity is not the right question for
  it; *having* a verdict is.
- The remaining readers red on *presence of a malformed thing*, monotone under a
  narrowing, and are clearable by inspection.

**No probe silences stderr.** A `2>/dev/null` on a path grep reads a bad path as
"no reader" and manufactures exactly the false negative this section exists to
close — and delta 5's two findings are both of that shape, reached by greps that
were allowed to fail loudly.

      reader at a named transition.

      coherent document a reader who never saw the amendment can use alone.
- [ ] **Amendment deleted** — this file removed on merge; none remain for the
      component (`ls gate-sdk/SPEC-*.md`).
- [ ] **Removals propagated** — grepped every spec for names this change
      retired; nothing dangles.

      debt tasks (a build-time causal gap is resolved that session, not
      deferred).
