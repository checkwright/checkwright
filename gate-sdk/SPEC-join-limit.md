# SPEC amendment: join-limit

§check-path-dialect names the third text-level primitive, joining a root onto a
segment, as unasserted, and leaves open whether a dataflow predicate is owed for
it. **This amendment rules that none is owed.** The join stays held by review and
by `walk.rs`'s producer monopoly, and the section says so as a settled limit
instead of an open question.

**The measurement the ruling rests on (2026-09-21).**

- **The corpus is large and almost all lawful.** `grep -rn 'format!("{}/' native/src | wc -l`
  prints 418. The entry was filed with 441, and the count has fallen as later
  edits routed composition through `walk` helpers. Construction is lawful by the
  contract (§Porting to Rust does not retire dialect exposure). Only a join that
  *escapes* into a printed, matched or prefix-tested value is the subject, so a
  form scan over those lines would red hundreds of permitted sites.
- **The defect-bearing escape already reds.** The containment arm holds a bare
  trailing-slash composition wherever it is bound, and a separator-bearing
  `format!` handed straight to `starts_with` or `strip_prefix`. That is the shape
  of the attested Windows defect: a synthesized `<anchor>/` prefix-tested a
  statement later. So the prefix-test escape the contract fears most is caught.
- **What stays unheld is a joined value bound to a name and printed or compared a
  statement later.** A diagnostic that prints a composed path is the lawful
  majority of that remainder. Telling it from an escaping comparison needs
  per-function taint tracking, a different gate shape from the line-local scan
  §check-path-dialect's locality rule deliberately keeps.

**Why nothing is built.** The cost while deferred is `event/low`: review of a crate
edit, the status quo. A same-function taint tracker would buy precision over
418 sites with no attested defect in the unheld remainder. It would also give up the
one-occurrence locality the arm was designed on, on the ground §check-path-dialect
already gives for keeping that locality.
