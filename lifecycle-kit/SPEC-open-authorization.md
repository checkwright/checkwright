# SPEC amendment: open-authorization

`templates/lead.md` §Opening an iteration governs everything about an iteration that is *opening* —
who selects the unit set (not the lead), what the lead relays (the operator's directive, verbatim),
how scope's proposal routes back — and never states **who decides one opens**. This amendment states
it, and states the symmetric half the same silence leaves at the other end: what a lead does when an
iteration closes and no next one has been authorized.

**The rule lands as kit mechanism, undated and impersonal.** The seam rule is that a ground carried
*as provenance* — attached to an authority, a date, a channel, or an identifier that resolves only
inside one repository — belongs in the consumer's own ruling record, while the same ground stated
undated and impersonally is engineering prose the template keeps. The test is the identifier, not
the tense. So every delta below states the engineering ground in full and carries no date, no
channel, no queue slug, no iteration name and no pointer to any consumer's ruling record.

**It mints no slot and adds no knob**, deliberately. A new `*<slot: …>*` token would red the
binding-shim pairing gate until every consumer's shim bound it in the same commit, and this rule
needs no consumer value: it names an authority the template already names five times over, and the
authority *vocabulary* stays the consumer's on its own always-loaded surface, as the sibling
boundary template already has it. A knob is refused on the same ground the kit already records for
ruling-authority names — nothing in the machinery would branch on the value.

**The mechanical floor is refused for this unit, with the shape named for whoever wants it later.**
Delta 4 owns that refusal and its grounds.

**This amendment is one of five in an iteration spanning gate-sdk, lifecycle-kit and drift-kit**, so
`check-stage-entry` assertion C is armed on the amendment file count alone and the audit stage's
stamp will be demanded at build's entry.

## What changes

### (1) §Opening an iteration gains the authorization sentence, prior to and distinct from the directive

The section gains a lead paragraph stating that **opening an iteration is the operator's decision,
not the lead's**, obtained explicitly and separately before anything else in the section applies
{design-bearing}. The standing directive is what bounds the survey **once** an iteration is opening;
it is not what makes one open, and its absence is not a licence.

**The engineering ground, stated in full and impersonally, because the rule is only obeyed if the
cost is legible.** Opening an iteration commits a *scale* of spend — a boundary reset that truncates
the evidence surfaces, a full stage walk, and a session per stage — and the scale is the operator's
to choose even where the *content* plainly routes through the lifecycle. That split is what makes
the failure hard to see from inside: an instruction to fix filed work can be entirely correct about
what should be done and say nothing about how much machinery should be spent doing it, so a lead
reading it as authorization is reasoning correctly about content and silently about scale. The rule
therefore binds on the ambiguous case by name: **an instruction to fix filed work is not an
authorization to open an iteration, and the lead asks.**

**The rule is drafted as the twin of the operator-class carve-out this template already carries**,
and reads as one design with it rather than as a second, unrelated prohibition: there the lead may
not *reverse* a recorded ruling however well-grounded its evidence, here it may not *open* however
plainly the work routes through the lifecycle. Both are classes where the lead's inference is
locally sound and the decision is not the lead's to make.

### (2) The "absent a directive" sentence is repaired in place, being the licence the silence created

The section's own sentence — that absent a directive the lead dispatches scope undirected — is left
standing but is no longer readable as a licence to open {design-bearing}. It is scoped explicitly:
it describes what an **authorized** iteration with no theme looks like, and the undirected path
stays lawful on that reading.

**Repairing it in place rather than deleting it is the point.** The sentence is true and useful —
scope surveys and recommends either way, and an operator who authorizes an iteration without naming
a theme has not thereby withheld anything. What made it a licence is that it was the section's only
permissive sentence, sitting beside a single prohibition about unit selection, so a reader takes
away "you may not pick the units, but you may proceed with no input at all." Deleting it would
remove a true statement and leave the inference available anyway; scoping it removes the inference
and keeps the statement.

### (3) The post-close state is stated, and it is the same rule read at the other boundary

The template gains a short closing section stating that when an iteration's final stage completes,
the lead **stops and reports what it believes is owed**, and does not open the next one
{design-bearing}. Reporting is the lead's whole remaining act at that boundary: the state of the
queue as the closing stage left it, anything filed for the next intake, and what the lead would
recommend — offered as information for a decision, never as a decision taken.

**The silence at this end is worse than the silence at the other, and that is why one rule is not
enough.** The template today says nothing at all about a lead after close: it is not merely
unruled, it is unmentioned. Meanwhile the kit's own contract positively designs for a lead that is
*live at the boundary* — its session marker and its journal are the two scratch artifacts that
survive the boundary wipe, precisely so a lead can file a boundary judgment for the entering
session's intake. So the design puts a session with its full context and its preserved notes at the
exact moment the next iteration would open, and hands it no instruction. Under the **unified**
posture that session is the previous scope session itself, which is the case most likely to roll
straight forward, since nothing about its situation has changed except that a queue drained.

**One rule stated at the boundary rather than two stated at the ends.** Delta 1's rule is about who
decides an iteration opens; this delta is the same rule met from the other side, and stating them as
one keeps a later reader from repairing one end and leaving the other. Both postures are covered by
construction, because the rule attaches to the boundary rather than to a posture.

### (4) The mechanical floor is refused, and the shape that would fit is named

No entry precondition, no flag, no stamped authorization line {design-bearing}. The rule ships as
prose the lead follows, with its honest limit stated beside it in the SPEC rather than left to be
inferred.

**The refusal's ground is that the fact is not encodable, not that encoding is expensive.** An
authorization is a fact about a conversation. Every encoding of it — a flag on the entry tool, a
committed line naming an authority, a ledger arming — is written by the same session it binds, so it
raises the cost of proceeding unauthorized and does not make an unauthorized open detectable
afterward. That is the same honest limit the kit already records for its other bypassable
affordances, and stating it is what keeps a later reader from mistaking the prose rule for a weaker
version of a mechanism that was available.

**Two mechanisms were weighed and the record says which is which.** The **one-shot entry valve** is
ruled out by its own contract rather than by preference: it does not reach any iteration-boundary
refusal, and its ledger matches the queue header's iteration name while the boundary entry stamps
the unnamed placeholder — so at the moment an authorization would be checked there is no name an
arming could have been written against. The **boundary-require precondition** is the shape that
would actually fit: it already fires only on the first stage, reads a committed file, and fails
closed when the file is absent. Two costs are named with it rather than discovered later — it is
guarded on the closing iteration having a name, so a consumer's very first iteration skips it, and
its refusal text is written for the release-disposition semantics it currently serves, so a second
semantic needs either a second knob or a generalized message. **Naming the shape is not proposing
it**; it is what a later reader needs so that "no floor was buildable" is not the conclusion drawn
from this refusal.

### (5) The directive may still arrive from the lead, and the non-implication is stated

The authoring stage's own template already says a directive received "from a lead or operator"
bounds its survey, and that stays true {design-bearing}. Authorization and directive are separate
slots: the operator authorizes the open, and the theme may reach the surveying stage through the
lead, which is the relay the template already describes.

**The non-implication is what needs saying: a directive arriving from the lead is not evidence the
open was authorized.** A surveying stage cannot tell an authorized open from an unauthorized one and
is not asked to — its contract is the survey, and adding an authorization check there would give one
fact two holders, the second of which has strictly less information. Stated in §Opening an iteration
rather than in the surveying stage's template, so the obligation sits with the party that holds it,
and that template needs no edit.

## Producers and consumers

**No new state, event, interface, field or configuration is introduced.** The change is prose in a
template plus the coupled statement of it in the kit's own contract inventory. The
causal-completeness points are answered against that, rather than left blank, because a blank here
would read as an omission.

**The rule's producer is the operator, through the conversation the lead is already in.** *Reader* —
the lead session, at the boundary transition where it would otherwise dispatch the first stage; and
again at the transition where an iteration's final stage completes. The enabling configuration is
nothing: the rule reads a fact the lead already has, which is exactly why delta 4 refuses to
manufacture an artifact for it.

**The changed reader-facing surface is the template**, whose consumers are every lead session that
loads it — directly, or through a consumer's binding shim, both of which read the same file.

**No mechanized reader gains or loses a subject, and this was probed rather than assumed.** The
registration gate's entire input is the consumer's always-loaded marker block plus the stage roster
and the queue path, none of which a template edit can move; the lead is a boundary skill and joins
no stage set, so the stage-coverage gate never reads it; and no gate byte-compares this template to
anything. The two gates that *do* read it read it for shape, not content: the binding-shim pairing
gate reads slot tokens and this amendment mints none, so that corpus is unchanged, while the
shim-restatement gate reds on a long normalized n-gram shared between a consumer's shim and this
corpus — which is why the new prose must not be drafted in a shim's words, and why that gate is run
rather than reasoned about.

**Narrowing check.** This delta set narrows no corpus — no file, field, invocation or fixture is
removed, and the one thing that narrows is the set of acts a lead may take on its own authority,
which no scanner reads. Point 5's monotonicity question therefore does not arise. The readers that
do red are freshness gates over derived measurements: the per-kit footprint page and the value
rollup derived from it both stale on **any** template markdown byte change, red on a **byte
mismatch**, and are non-monotone in both directions, so both are regenerated in the landing commit
rather than cleared by inspection.

## Definition of Done

- [ ] **Causal completeness** — every new state/event/interface has a named, reachable producer and
      a named consumer; every new field has a named reader at a named transition. (This amendment
      introduces none, and §Producers and consumers says so rather than leaving it blank.)
- [ ] **The seam holds at the word level, not the paragraph level** — the landed template prose
      carries no date, no authority-plus-channel attribution, no queue slug, no iteration name and
      no pointer to a consumer's ruling record; the provenance for this change lives in the ruling
      record and the commit, and the kit file shows no trace of the instance that prompted it.
- [ ] **No slot is minted** — the template's slot set is unchanged, so no consumer shim owes a
      binding in this commit.
- [ ] **The restatement gate is run, not emulated** — the new prose is checked against the shim
      corpus with the gate itself, the consumer's always-loaded authority bullet being the likely
      collision surface.

      which stale on any template markdown byte.

      the amendment can use alone.
